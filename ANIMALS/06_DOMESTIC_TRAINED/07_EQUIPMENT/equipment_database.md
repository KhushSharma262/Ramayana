# HISTORICAL EQUIPMENT CONTROL

## Purpose

This section governs the reconstruction and production use of animal-associated equipment in historical, ancient, canonical, archaeological, ethnographic, or otherwise uncertain settings.

Historical equipment must never be introduced into a scene merely because the equipment is visually plausible, familiar from modern practice, or commonly associated with the animal today.

Every historically reconstructed equipment element must have an explicit evidence state.

## Evidence States

Allowed historical-equipment evidence states:

- SUPPORTED
- PLAUSIBLE
- CONTESTED
- UNKNOWN
- BLOCKED

### SUPPORTED

Direct or strong evidence supports the equipment form, material, use, period, geography, and relevant animal context.

### PLAUSIBLE

The equipment is consistent with available evidence but is not directly established for the exact required context.

### CONTESTED

Available sources disagree or the reconstruction depends on disputed interpretation.

### UNKNOWN

Insufficient evidence exists to establish the equipment reliably.

### BLOCKED

The equipment cannot be used in production because its historical basis is inadequate, contradictory, anachronistic, or otherwise invalid.

## Temporal Control

Modern equipment must not automatically be projected backward into ancient scenes.

Modern:

- saddles
- bridles
- harnesses
- bits
- reins
- collars
- packs
- carts
- yokes
- armor
- ceremonial equipment
- riding equipment
- veterinary equipment
- stable equipment

must each be independently evaluated before use in an ancient or historical reconstruction.

Visual similarity does not establish historical continuity.

## Canonical vs Historical Equipment

Canonical textual mention and archaeological/historical evidence are separate evidence classes.

A textual reference may establish that an animal or equipment category exists within the narrative tradition without establishing:

- exact construction
- exact dimensions
- exact materials
- modern-equivalent design
- manufacturing technique
- precise archaeological form

Therefore:

CANONICAL MENTION != ARCHAEOLOGICAL PROOF

Historical reconstruction must preserve this distinction.

## Equipment-to-Animal Compatibility

Equipment must be physically compatible with the approved animal state.

Check:

- species
- body size
- body structure
- age
- sex where relevant
- conditioning
- training
- workload
- attachment location
- load
- balance
- movement restrictions
- injury risk
- duration of use

Biology and Movement remain authoritative for physical limits and mechanical consequences.

Domestic / Trained owns the historical and operational equipment context.

## Equipment-to-Training Compatibility

Equipment must be consistent with the animal's approved training state.

For example:

- an untrained animal must not silently receive advanced working equipment;
- a trained riding animal must have a compatible training history;
- specialized military equipment requires evidence of specialized use;
- ceremonial equipment must not automatically imply military training;
- working equipment must correspond to the animal's documented work role.

## Equipment Continuity

Once an equipment state is approved for an important individual animal, it persists across scenes unless an explicit continuity event changes it.

Allowed equipment continuity events include:

- EQUIPPED
- REMOVED
- REPLACED
- DAMAGED
- REPAIRED
- MODIFIED
- LOST
- TRANSFERRED
- RETIRED

A regenerated AI image does not constitute an equipment-change event.

## Equipment Identity

Visual resemblance alone does not establish that two pieces of equipment are the same object.

Important equipment may require:

- equipment_id
- type
- owner
- assigned_animal
- material
- distinctive features
- condition
- modifications
- continuity_state
- evidence_state

## Historical Equipment Firewall

The following are prohibited unless explicitly supported and approved:

- modern equipment silently inserted into ancient scenes
- modern breed-specific equipment projected backward
- historically unsupported mechanical designs
- historically unsupported materials
- historically unsupported ornamentation
- modern safety equipment presented as ancient
- modern saddlery presented as ancient
- modern military equipment presented as ancient
- equipment copied from AI-generated historical imagery and treated as evidence

AI-generated images, videos, prompts, concept art, and generated reconstructions are NOT historical evidence.

Historical equipment may enter production only when:

→ EVIDENCE
→ HISTORICAL EQUIPMENT CLAIM

→ ANIMAL COMPATIBILITY
→ TRAINING COMPATIBILITY

If any required stage is unresolved:

PRODUCTION = BLOCKED

## Ownership Boundary

Domestic / Trained owns:

- historical animal equipment context
- equipment use
- equipment assignment
- equipment continuity
- equipment-training relationship
- equipment-handler relationship
- historical reconstruction status

Biology owns:

- anatomy
- morphology
- physiological limits
- physical tolerance

Movement owns:

- physical equipment interaction
- load mechanics
- gait effects
- balance effects
- movement restrictions
- mechanical consequences

Ecology owns:

- environmental consequences of equipment use where applicable

Directing owns:

- framing
- composition
- dramatic emphasis

AI Production owns:

- generation
- rendering
- asset creation

No system may silently overwrite another system's authority.

## Historical Equipment Control Rule

> No historical animal equipment may appear in a production scene unless its historical status, temporal context, geographical context, animal compatibility, training compatibility, and continuity state have been explicitly resolved or approved according to the Domestic / Trained evidence-control system.
