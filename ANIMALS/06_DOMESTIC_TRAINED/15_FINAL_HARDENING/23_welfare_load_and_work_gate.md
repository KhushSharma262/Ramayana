# WELFARE / LOAD / WORK GATE

## Purpose

Prevent training, domestic use, historical reconstruction, equipment, workload, or production requirements from exceeding the animal's biological and biomechanical limits.

## Master Principle

A historical practice, training objective, narrative requirement, or cinematic requirement does not override biological or physical reality.

## Required Assessment

Before approving significant work or specialized use, evaluate:

- species
- individual_id where applicable
- age
- life_stage
- sex where relevant
- body size
- body condition
- conditioning
- health state
- injury state
- fatigue state
- workload
- load
- duration
- frequency
- terrain
- surface
- weather
- temperature
- hydration
- rest/recovery
- equipment
- equipment fit
- task type
- training state

## Biological Authority

Biology is authoritative for:

- anatomical limits
- physiological limits
- developmental limits
- health constraints
- injury constraints
- species-specific physical requirements

Domestic / Trained cannot override Biology.

## Movement Authority

Movement is authoritative for:

- load mechanics
- balance
- gait effects
- acceleration effects
- turning effects
- stopping effects
- equipment interference
- terrain interaction
- biomechanical consequences

Domestic / Trained cannot override Movement.

## Behavior Authority

Behavior is authoritative for:

- fear
- stress response
- resistance
- avoidance
- aggression
- fatigue-related behavior
- learned response
- behavioral recovery

A trained animal does not become behaviorally incapable of fear, fatigue, pain, or stress.

## Ecology Authority

Ecology remains authoritative for environmental constraints such as:

- resource availability
- water availability
- heat
- drought
- seasonal conditions
- habitat limitations
- environmental carrying capacity

Domestic use cannot override ecological reality.

## Work Capacity

Work capacity must be evaluated against the individual animal where individual identity is relevant.

A species-level assumption is insufficient when the scene depends on:

- unusually heavy load
- prolonged work
- extreme terrain
- extreme weather
- high-speed movement
- military use
- repeated strenuous activity
- injury or recovery
- advanced specialized work

## Training Does Not Equal Unlimited Capacity

The following are invalid assumptions:

TRAINED = UNLIMITED

EXPERT = UNLIMITED

WORK_READY = UNLIMITED

MILITARY = UNLIMITED

DOMESTICATED = UNLIMITED

Training may improve performance within biological and biomechanical limits.

Training does not remove those limits.

## Equipment Compatibility

Equipment must be evaluated for:

- species
- body size
- anatomy
- attachment location
- fit
- weight
- balance
- material
- condition
- task
- duration
- movement consequences

Equipment that is historically plausible but physically incompatible remains BLOCKED.

## Load

Where load is relevant, document:

- load_type
- estimated_load
- load_distribution
- attachment_method
- duration
- terrain
- movement_required
- animal_condition
- evidence
- approval

Do not invent exact load values when evidence does not support them.

Use UNKNOWN rather than false precision.

## Fatigue

Fatigue is a state, not merely a visual effect.

Where relevant track:

- exertion
- duration
- recovery
- environmental stress
- hydration
- nutrition
- rest

Fatigue must propagate through continuity when the next scene occurs before adequate recovery.

## Recovery

Recovery must be physically plausible.

A later scene cannot automatically reset an animal to full condition.

If recovery is relevant:

WORK
→ FATIGUE
→ REST / RECOVERY
→ UPDATED_STATE

## Injury

An injury must not disappear between scenes without an appropriate recovery event.

Injury affects relevant:

- movement
- behavior
- workload
- equipment compatibility
- training
- recovery

Biology and Movement remain authoritative for consequences.

## Historical Practice

Historical evidence may establish that a practice existed.

It does NOT automatically establish:

- exact load
- exact duration
- exact animal condition
- exact equipment fit
- exact physiological safety

Historical practice and biological feasibility are separate claims.

## Cinematic Exaggeration

Cinematic emphasis may be permitted only where the resulting depiction remains physically plausible.

If an exaggeration becomes physically impossible:

CINEMATICALLY_EMPHASIZED_MOVEMENT

must not become:

SUPERNATURAL

without explicit canonical/supernatural authorization.

## Welfare Gate

A production action is acceptable only when:

BIOLOGY = COMPATIBLE

AND

MOVEMENT = COMPATIBLE

AND

BEHAVIOR = PLAUSIBLE

AND

ECOLOGY = COMPATIBLE

AND

DOMESTIC/TRAINING = APPROPRIATE

## Failure Conditions

Any of the following may block production:

- excessive unsupported load
- incompatible equipment
- developmentally inappropriate workload
- unresolved injury
- unresolved health limitation
- physically impossible movement
- unsupported endurance
- impossible recovery
- environmentally impossible working conditions
- unsupported historical claim
- unresolved animal condition

## Fail-Closed Rule

If safety or physical feasibility cannot be established:

WORK_STATE = BLOCKED

If the disputed condition affects the scene:

PRODUCTION = BLOCKED

Do not solve missing evidence by assuming the animal can tolerate it.

## Master Rule

USE MUST FIT BIOLOGY.

EQUIPMENT MUST FIT THE ANIMAL.

WORK MUST FIT CAPACITY.

MOVEMENT MUST FIT MECHANICS.

TRAINING MUST NOT ERASE LIMITS.

HISTORICAL PRACTICE MUST NOT OVERRIDE PHYSICAL REALITY.
