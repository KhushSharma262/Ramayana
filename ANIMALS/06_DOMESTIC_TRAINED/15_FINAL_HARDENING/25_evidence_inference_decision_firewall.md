# EVIDENCE / INFERENCE / DECISION FIREWALL

## Purpose

Ensure that every important Domestic / Trained claim remains traceable from source material through interpretation, decision, approval, snapshot, scene and final production.

## Mandatory Claim Chain

Every important claim must follow:

SOURCE
→ EVIDENCE
→ INTERPRETATION
→ INFERENCE
→ DECISION
→ APPROVAL
→ SNAPSHOT
→ SCENE
→ PROMPT
→ ASSET
→ FINAL SHOT

Not every claim requires an inference step.

If inference is unnecessary:

SOURCE
→ EVIDENCE
→ DECISION

is acceptable.

## SOURCE

A source is the underlying material from which information is obtained.

Examples:

- primary textual source
- archaeological evidence
- historical record
- scholarly publication
- expert source
- ethnographic source
- biological research
- approved canonical material

Source identity must be recorded where relevant.

## EVIDENCE

Evidence is the specific information actually supported by the source.

Evidence must not contain unsupported additions.

A source saying:

"horses were used"

does NOT automatically establish:

- exact breed
- exact equipment
- exact saddle
- exact training level
- exact load
- exact color
- exact appearance

## INTERPRETATION

Interpretation explains what the evidence may reasonably mean within its context.

Interpretation must remain distinguishable from direct evidence.

## INFERENCE

Inference is a conclusion derived from evidence and/or interpretation rather than directly stated by the source.

Inference must be explicitly labelled.

Inference cannot silently become evidence.

## DECISION

A production or research decision is an approved conclusion about how the project will represent the available evidence.

A decision must preserve uncertainty where uncertainty remains.

## APPROVAL

Approval establishes that the decision is acceptable for the current project state.

Approval does not transform uncertain evidence into certainty.

## Evidence States

Allowed evidence states:

- DIRECT
- STRONG
- MODERATE
- WEAK
- INDIRECT
- CONTESTED
- UNKNOWN
- UNSUPPORTED
- BLOCKED

## Historical Confidence

Historical reconstruction may use:

- SUPPORTED
- PLAUSIBLE
- CONTESTED
- UNKNOWN
- BLOCKED

These states must not be collapsed.

## Forbidden Conversions

The following are invalid:

INFERENCE = EVIDENCE

PLAUSIBLE = SUPPORTED

COMMON = HISTORICALLY_ESTABLISHED

MODERN_PRACTICE = ANCIENT_PRACTICE

POPULAR_CULTURE = HISTORICAL_EVIDENCE

FILM_DEPICTION = SOURCE

AI_OUTPUT = EVIDENCE

VISUAL_PLAUSIBILITY = HISTORICAL_PROOF

ABSENCE_OF_EVIDENCE = EVIDENCE_OF_ABSENCE

UNKNOWN = ABSENT

## AI Contamination Firewall

The following can help with visual production but cannot establish biological, historical, domestic, training, or equipment truth:

- AI images
- AI videos
- AI-generated concept art
- AI-generated character sheets
- AI-generated prompts
- AI-generated historical reconstructions
- model suggestions
- generated visual references

AI output may represent an approved claim.

It cannot create or upgrade the claim.

## Secondary Source Duplication

Multiple secondary sources repeating the same unsupported claim do not automatically create independent evidence.

Where possible, identify the underlying source.

Repeated citation != independent confirmation.

## Source Conflict

If sources disagree:

1. preserve each source
2. record the disagreement
3. classify source authority
4. assess temporal relevance
5. assess geographical relevance
6. assess species relevance
7. assess methodological quality
8. determine whether the conflict is resolvable
9. record the decision
10. preserve uncertainty if unresolved

Do not silently select the most visually convenient source.

## Temporal Firewall

Evidence must be evaluated against the correct temporal layer.

Do not silently transfer:

MODERN
→ ANCIENT

or:

LATE_HISTORICAL
→ EARLIER_HISTORICAL

without justification.

## Geographic Firewall

Evidence must be evaluated against the correct geographical context.

Do not silently transfer:

EUROPEAN
→ INDIAN

or:

MODERN_INDIAN
→ ANCIENT_INDIAN

without justification.

## Species Firewall

Evidence for one species cannot automatically establish the same claim for another species.

For example:

HORSE_EVIDENCE != ELEPHANT_EVIDENCE

ELEPHANT_EVIDENCE != OX_EVIDENCE

Broad terms must remain unresolved when species identity is uncertain.

## Claim Granularity

Claims must be no broader than their evidence.

If evidence establishes:

"animals were used for transport"

do not upgrade it to:

"this exact animal used this exact harness for this exact journey."

The more specific the production claim, the more specific the supporting evidence must be.

## Negative Evidence

Absence of a source statement does not automatically establish absence of the practice.

Therefore:

NOT_MENTIONED != ABSENT

ABSENT requires appropriate justification.

## Decision Preservation

When evidence is uncertain, the uncertainty must travel with the decision.

Example:

EVIDENCE = PLAUSIBLE

therefore:

DECISION = PLAUSIBLE_RECONSTRUCTION

not:

DECISION = HISTORICAL_FACT

## Research Record

Important claims should preserve:

- claim_id
- subject_id
- claim_text
- source_id
- source_type
- source_date where relevant
- source_location
- evidence_text_or_reference
- evidence_state
- interpretation
- inference
- decision
- uncertainty
- approval
- version
- snapshot_id

## Revision

When new evidence changes a decision:

OLD_DECISION
→ NEW_EVIDENCE
→ REASSESSMENT
→ NEW_DECISION
→ NEW_APPROVAL
→ NEW_SNAPSHOT

Do not silently rewrite the previous decision.

## Production Firewall

A production claim must have:

- identifiable source basis
- evidence state
- decision state
- approval
- snapshot reference

If required evidence is missing:

CLAIM = BLOCKED

If the decision cannot be justified:

DECISION = BLOCKED

If approval is missing:

PRODUCTION = BLOCKED

## Master Rule

SOURCE IS NOT EVIDENCE.

EVIDENCE IS NOT INTERPRETATION.

INTERPRETATION IS NOT INFERENCE.

INFERENCE IS NOT FACT.

A DECISION DOES NOT ERASE UNCERTAINTY.

AI OUTPUT IS NEVER THE SOURCE OF TRUTH.

Every production claim must remain traceable back to its evidence.
