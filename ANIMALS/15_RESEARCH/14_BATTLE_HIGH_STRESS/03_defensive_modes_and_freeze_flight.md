# DEFENSIVE MODES: FREEZE / FLIGHT / DEFENSE

## Scientific Finding

Threat can produce qualitatively different defensive modes.

Research describes freezing and active defensive reactions such as fight-or-flight.

Freezing is not simply "doing nothing"; it can be an active defensive state associated with motor inhibition and threat processing.

SOURCE:
Freeze for action: neurobiological mechanisms in animal and human freezing.

PMC5332864.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332864/

## Production Implications

A frightened animal may plausibly:
- become still
- orient toward the threat
- avoid
- flee
- defend itself
- seek safety
- remain vigilant

The exact response must be species-specific.

## Important Boundary

The scientific literature used here does NOT justify assigning one universal response to every animal.

Rodent laboratory evidence is particularly useful for understanding defensive-state mechanisms, but cannot automatically be transferred to:
- elephants
- horses
- cattle
- monkeys
- birds
- reptiles
- amphibians
- fish

without additional species-specific evidence.
