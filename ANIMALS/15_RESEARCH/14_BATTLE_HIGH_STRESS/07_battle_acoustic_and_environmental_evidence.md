# BATTLE ACOUSTIC AND ENVIRONMENTAL EVIDENCE

## Canonical Evidence

Valmiki Ramayana Yuddha Kanda 6.44 describes:
- dust from horse hooves
- dust from chariot wheels
- drums
- conches
- other battlefield sounds

The text explicitly connects battlefield movement with dust.

SOURCE:
https://www.valmikiramayan.net/yuddha/sarga44/yuddharoman44.htm

## Additional Canonical Evidence

Yuddha Kanda 6.52 compares the battle's soundscape using:
- bowstrings
- horse neighing
- elephant trumpeting
- other battle sounds

SOURCE:
https://www.valmikiramayan.net/yuddha/sarga52/yuddhasans52.htm

## Production Interpretation

These passages support representing the battlefield as acoustically and physically dense.

They do NOT establish that every horse:
- neighs continuously
- panics
- flees
- becomes aggressive

They establish the presence of battlefield animal sounds and environmental disturbance.

## Dust

Dust is not merely visual decoration.

It may:
- reduce visibility
- alter the appearance of the battlefield
- accompany horse/chariot movement

The exact physiological impact on a particular animal remains a separate research question.

## Audio Rule

Animal audio must remain subordinate to:
- species
- behavior
- actual event
- acoustic environment

Do not create continuous generic animal screaming merely because a battle is occurring.
