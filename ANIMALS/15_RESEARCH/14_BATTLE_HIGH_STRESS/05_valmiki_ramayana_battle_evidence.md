# VALMIKI RAMAYANA — BATTLE ANIMAL EVIDENCE

## Primary Canonical Research Target

Valmiki Ramayana.

This file records what the text describes.
It does NOT attempt to convert every poetic description into modern ethology.

## A. Horses And Elephants Are Explicitly Present In Battle

Aranya Kanda 3.25 describes:
- horses yoked to chariots
- elephants with riders
- horses with riders
- chariots
- infantry

The passage explicitly describes horses and elephants being affected by the battle.

SOURCE:
Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda, Sarga 25.

https://www.valmikiramayan.net/aranya/sarga25/aranyaroman25.htm

## B. War Preparation Of Elephants And Horses

Yuddha Kanda 6.33 describes elephants being prepared/caparisoned for war and chariot horses being prepared for battle.

SOURCE:
Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 33.

https://www.valmikiramayan.net/yuddha/sarga33/yuddharoman33.htm

## C. Battlefield Sound

Yuddha Kanda 6.44 describes:
- horse sounds
- chariot-wheel sounds
- drums
- conches
- battlefield noise

It also describes dust raised by horse hooves and chariot wheels obstructing the senses of combatants.

SOURCE:
Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda, Sarga 44.

https://www.valmikiramayan.net/yuddha/sarga44/yuddharoman44.htm

## D. Horses And Elephants As Battlefield Casualties

Aranya Kanda 3.25 explicitly describes numerous horses and elephants being felled during the battle.

This is important for production because the canonical battle environment is not a clean, consequence-free battlefield.

SOURCE:
https://www.valmikiramayan.net/aranya/sarga25/aranya_5F25_prose.htm

## E. Animal Presence Does Not Automatically Establish Animal Psychology

The text establishes:
- animal presence
- animal roles
- equipment/context
- battle exposure
- casualties
- battlefield sensory conditions

It does NOT automatically provide a modern scientific description of:
- cortisol
- heart rate
- freezing mechanism
- fear conditioning
- exact escape probability
- exact recovery duration

Those must come from scientific evidence or remain unknown.

## Canonical Research Rule

CANONICAL TEXT
=
PRIMARY EVIDENCE FOR WHAT THE TEXT DESCRIBES

SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH
=
EVIDENCE FOR HOW A REAL ANIMAL COULD PHYSICALLY/BEHAVIORALLY RESPOND

Neither source type should impersonate the other.
