# CANON STATUS

## PURPOSE

Controls how strongly an animal-related claim may be treated as canonical.

## DIRECT_CANON

Use only when the selected canonical source directly establishes the relevant information.

Examples of direct information can include:

- explicit animal mention
- explicit animal name
- explicit event involving an animal
- directly stated animal relationship

Do not extend direct canon beyond what the source actually establishes.

## DERIVED_CANON

Use when the conclusion follows reasonably from direct information but is not literally stated in the same form.

Derived canon must preserve the reasoning chain.

## TRADITIONAL_INTERPRETATION

Use for established traditional/commentarial interpretations that are not directly established by the selected primary source.

## HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION

Use for conclusions reconstructed from historical, environmental, cultural or archaeological evidence.

Historical plausibility is not automatically canon.

## NON_CANON_SUPPORTING_FACT

Use for scientific or zoological information needed to represent the animal realistically.

## UNCERTAIN

Use when evidence exists but multiple conclusions remain viable.

## UNKNOWN

Use when the project currently lacks enough evidence.

## ABSOLUTE RULE

Scientific plausibility cannot upgrade something to canon.

A visually common representation cannot upgrade something to canon.

An adaptation cannot upgrade something to canon.

An AI model cannot upgrade something to canon.

## REQUIRED OUTPUT

Every animal claim should answer:

WHAT IS KNOWN?
WHAT IS INFERRED?
WHAT IS RECONSTRUCTED?
WHAT IS UNKNOWN?
WHY WAS THE DECISION MADE?
