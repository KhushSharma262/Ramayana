# SPECIES BIOLOGY BASELINES

## Asian Elephant

Scientific name:
Elephas maximus

Broad biology:
Large terrestrial Asian proboscidean with highly developed trunk, tusk variability by sex/population, large ears relative to African elephants, complex social behavior and strong environmental dependence.

Important biological constraints:

- trunk is a muscular hydrostat and major sensory/manipulative organ
- locomotion must preserve realistic foot placement and body mass
- trunk movement must remain anatomically continuous
- body size varies with sex, age and individual
- adult males and females differ in social context and reproductive state
- heat management and water access are important
- calves have different proportions and capabilities from adults
- elephants do not move like horses or large carnivores
- vocal and non-vocal communication is species-specific

Exact measurements must be sourced per individual asset.

---

## Bengal Tiger

Scientific name:
Panthera tigris tigris

Broad biology:
Large solitary felid adapted for ambush predation.

Constraints:

- locomotion is digitigrade
- body movement is flexible but strongly musculoskeletal
- acceleration is possible but cannot be treated as unlimited
- stalking and ambush behavior differs from pursuit predators
- stripes are individual identifiers
- sensory behavior includes strong visual, auditory and olfactory components
- adults and cubs differ strongly in size and behavior
- territorial and reproductive behavior is context-dependent

Do not automatically make every tiger continuously aggressive.

---

## Indian Leopard

Scientific name:
Panthera pardus

Broad biology:
Flexible carnivore capable of climbing and using diverse habitats.

Constraints:

- climbing ability must respect limb mechanics and body mass
- leopard body proportions differ from tiger
- rosette patterns are individual
- behavior should not copy tiger behavior automatically
- ambush behavior is context-dependent
- nocturnal/crepuscular activity can vary with environment and human pressure

---

## Sloth Bear

Scientific name:
Melursus ursinus

Broad biology:
Indian bear species with specialized feeding adaptations.

Constraints:

- long claws and elongated snout influence feeding and locomotion
- locomotion must reflect bear anatomy rather than dog/cat anatomy
- climbing and digging behavior must remain physically plausible
- diet includes significant insect/plant components
- defensive behavior must not be represented as permanent aggression.

---

## Dhole

Scientific name:
Cuon alpinus

Broad biology:
Social wild canid.

Constraints:

- group behavior matters
- body plan differs from wolf and domestic dog
- movement is cursorial and coordinated
- vocal and social behavior should not be copied from domestic dogs
- pack size and behavior must be sourced for the required population/context.

---

## Golden Jackal

Scientific name:
Canis aureus

Broad biology:
Adaptable canid with omnivorous/scavenging tendencies.

Constraints:

- anatomy differs from foxes and domestic dogs
- behavior varies with food availability and human proximity
- vocalization must be species-specific
- body size should not be exaggerated.

---

## Indian Fox

Scientific name:
Vulpes bengalensis

Broad biology:
Small South Asian canid adapted to open and semi-open environments.

Constraints:

- smaller body than jackal
- characteristic fox locomotion and posture
- diet is opportunistic but species-specific
- visual identity must not default to European red fox appearance.

---

## Rhesus Macaque

Scientific name:
Macaca mulatta

Broad biology:
Highly adaptable social primate.

Constraints:

- complex social interactions
- grasping hands and feet must remain anatomically consistent
- facial expressions and body movements are species-specific
- infant behavior differs from adult behavior
- troop context matters
- human-associated behavior may include habituation.

---

## Bonnet Macaque

Scientific name:
Macaca radiata

Broad biology:
South Asian macaque with species-specific morphology and social behavior.

Constraints:

- must not be substituted visually with rhesus macaque
- crown hair and facial morphology require species-appropriate design
- social behavior is group-dependent
- age and sex alter appearance.

---

## Gaur

Scientific name:
Bos gaurus

Broad biology:
Large wild bovine.

Constraints:

- enormous body mass
- strong musculoskeletal structure
- bovine locomotion
- horn geometry and body profile are species-specific
- must not be visually substituted with domestic cattle.

---

## Wild Water Buffalo

Scientific name:
Bubalus arnee

Broad biology:
Large wild bovine associated with wetlands and grasslands.

Constraints:

- horn morphology differs from domestic buffalo
- body proportions differ from domestic water buffalo
- wetland behavior and habitat interaction matter
- enormous mass constrains acceleration and turning.

---

## Sambar

Scientific name:
Rusa unicolor

Broad biology:
Large Asian deer.

Constraints:

- species-specific antler morphology
- large body size
- deer locomotion
- sex differences matter
- alarm and vigilance behavior must not be identical to chital.

---

## Chital

Scientific name:
Axis axis

Broad biology:
Spotted Asian deer.

Constraints:

- persistent spotted coat pattern
- herd behavior
- high vigilance
- sex and age differences
- locomotion must preserve deer biomechanics.

---

## Blackbuck

Scientific name:
Antilope cervicapra

Broad biology:
Open-country antelope.

Constraints:

- slender cursorial body plan
- strongly distinctive spiral horn structure in adult males
- pronounced sexual dimorphism
- movement must preserve antelope biomechanics
- open habitat affects vigilance and escape behavior.

---

## Nilgai

Scientific name:
Boselaphus tragocamelus

Broad biology:
Large Asian antelope.

Constraints:

- strong sexual dimorphism
- male/female appearance differs
- body mass and locomotion must remain realistic
- cannot be treated as deer despite superficial ecological overlap.

---

## Chinkara

Scientific name:
Gazella bennettii

Broad biology:
Small Asian gazelle.

Constraints:

- light cursorial body
- arid/semi-arid adaptation
- movement differs from larger deer
- horns and facial markings must remain species-specific.

---

## Four-horned Antelope

Scientific name:
Tetracerus quadricornis

Broad biology:
Small Asian bovine with distinctive horn arrangement in males.

Constraints:

- four-horn structure is a key identity marker
- small body size relative to gaur/buffalo
- shy/secretive behavior must be distinguished from larger bovids.

---

## Horse

Scientific name:
Equus caballus

Broad biology:
Domestic equid with specialized cursorial anatomy.

Constraints:

- hoof-based locomotion
- large hindquarter propulsion
- neck/head balance
- species-specific gait mechanics
- breathing must remain coordinated with exertion
- age, breed/type and conditioning affect performance
- domestication/training is handled separately.

---

## Donkey

Scientific name:
Equus asinus

Broad biology:
Domestic equid adapted to efficient movement and harsh environmental conditions.

Constraints:

- anatomy differs from horse
- ears are proportionally distinctive
- gait and body proportions differ
- do not simply shrink a horse model.

---

## Domestic Water Buffalo

Scientific name:
Bubalus bubalis

Broad biology:
Domestic bovine used for agriculture, transport and milk production.

Constraints:

- body mass and horn structure vary with type
- heat management and water/mud interaction can be important
- working capacity depends on conditioning
- domestic type must be recorded separately from species identity.

---

## Domestic Goat

Scientific name:
Capra hircus

Broad biology:
Small domestic ruminant.

Constraints:

- climbing and balance capability must remain realistic
- horn presence varies
- coat and body form vary substantially
- feeding involves browsing/grazing depending on conditions
- breed/type must not be invented.

---

## Domestic Sheep

Scientific name:
Ovis aries

Broad biology:
Domestic ruminant with extensive breed variation.

Constraints:

- wool/coat varies greatly
- horn presence varies
- body shape varies by breed/type
- flock behavior matters
- grazing differs from browsing.

---

## Domestic Dog

Scientific name:
Canis lupus familiaris

Broad biology:
Domesticated canid with extreme morphological variation.

Constraints:

- breed/type must be explicitly identified where visually relevant
- size range is enormous
- ear/tail/coat morphology varies
- behavior must not be assumed from modern pet stereotypes
- working, village, hunting and companion dogs can differ.

---

## Domestic Cat

Scientific name:
Felis catus

Broad biology:
Small domesticated felid.

Constraints:

- locomotion remains feline
- hearing and vision influence behavior
- body size varies
- hunting behavior differs from tiger/leopard
- domestication does not remove predatory behavior.

---

## Chicken

Scientific name:
Gallus gallus domesticus

Broad biology:
Domestic fowl descended from red junglefowl.

Constraints:

- bird locomotion differs from mammals
- pecking, scratching, dust bathing and flock behavior are species-appropriate
- sexual dimorphism can be substantial
- rooster/hen/juvenile must be distinguished.

## Indian Cobra

Scientific name:
Naja naja

Broad biology:
Venomous elapid snake.

Constraints:

- movement is limbless
- hood display must remain anatomically plausible
- strike behavior must not be represented as constant
- snake body must retain continuous contact/curvature consistent with movement
- no impossible upright posture.

---

## Russell's Viper

Scientific name:
Daboia russelii

Broad biology:
Large venomous viper.

Constraints:

- body form differs from cobra
- defensive display differs
- movement is limbless
- head/neck anatomy must remain viper-like
- venomous status does not mean constant striking.
