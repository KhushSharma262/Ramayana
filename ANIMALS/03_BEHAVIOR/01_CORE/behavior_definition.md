# BEHAVIOR DEFINITION

## DEFINITION

Animal behavior is the observable and internally driven response of an animal
to internal biological state, external stimuli, environmental conditions,
social relationships, learned experience and immediate needs.

## EVERY BEHAVIOR RECORD SHOULD ANSWER

1. What species?
2. What individual, if relevant?
3. What age/life stage?
4. What sex, if behaviorally relevant?
5. What environment?
6. What time of day?
7. What season?
8. What triggered the behavior?
9. What did the animal perceive?
10. What internal state followed?
11. What need or motivation is relevant?
12. What behavior follows?
13. What physical signs accompany it?
14. How does the behavior escalate?
15. How does it stop?
16. How does the animal recover?
17. What previous experience modifies it?
18. What happens in the next shot?

## BEHAVIORAL CAUSALITY

Never use:

"animal behaves aggressively"

without defining the context.

Prefer:

TRIGGER
→ PERCEPTION
→ STATE
→ MOTIVATION
→ RESPONSE
→ ESCALATION
→ DE-ESCALATION
→ RECOVERY

## REALISM RULE

Animals should not perform behavior merely because it makes a shot
visually interesting.

A behavior requires biological, environmental, social or learned justification.

## NATURAL IMPERFECTION

Real animals:

- hesitate
- misjudge distance
- interrupt actions
- change attention
- investigate
- lose focus
- groom
- rest
- scratch
- reposition
- react asynchronously
- occasionally make mistakes

Do not make all background animals behave identically.

## FORBIDDEN DEFAULTS

Do not automatically assume:

horse = obedient
elephant = wise
dog = loyal
monkey = playful
tiger = constantly aggressive
bird = constantly flying
snake = constantly attacking
cow = permanently calm

Behavior must follow species, context and individual state.
