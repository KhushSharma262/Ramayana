# IMPERFECTIONS

## Purpose

Describe deviations from ideal structure, including defects, processing
traces, wear, and damage.

## Candidate classes

- Geometric deviations and waviness.
- Scratches, abrasion, cuts, and gouges.
- Cracks, chips, fracture, and spalling.
- Pores, voids, inclusions, and pits.
- Fiber, grain, and weave irregularities.
- Corrosion, erosion, deposits, and surface reactions.
- Delamination, wear-through, and coating failure.
- Contamination, adhered particles, and repair marks.

These are candidate classes, not a universal list for every material.

## Causal consistency

Each important defect should have a supported origin or an explicit UNKNOWN
status. Consider material structure, loading, contact, environment, processing,
and state when relevant.

Wear should be consistent with contact and use. Fracture should have plausible
geometry and material context. Deposits should be consistent with the relevant
environmental process where known.

Do not infer a complete history from appearance alone.

## Spatial statistics

Avoid identical cloned marks, unjustified uniform placement, disconnected
features from a single process, arbitrary scratch directions, and visible
procedural tiling.

## Representation

Color changes alone cannot reproduce deep geometry, silhouette changes,
parallax, or physical occlusion. Select geometry, displacement, normal,
color, or state changes according to the effect being represented.

Prevent accidental double representation of the same defect.

## Validation

Inspect grazing light, close views, contact, motion, shadows, and continuity.
Distinguish observed defects, inferred defects, simulated defects, and
art-directed features in production records.
