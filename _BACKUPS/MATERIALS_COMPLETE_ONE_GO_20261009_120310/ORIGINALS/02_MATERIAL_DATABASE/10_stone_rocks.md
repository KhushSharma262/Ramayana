# STONE AND ROCK MATERIALS

## Scope

Natural lithic materials used for architecture, sculpture, roads, steps, walls, floors, tools, weapons, weights, ornaments and landscape.

## Material families

### Granite
Hard crystalline silicate rock. Visually variable depending on mineral composition. Do not treat every coarse crystalline stone as granite.

### Basalt
Dark volcanic rock. Dense and generally fine-grained. Actual colour and texture vary with mineralogy and weathering.

### Sandstone
Clastic sedimentary rock dominated by sand-sized grains. Colour depends strongly on mineral composition and cement.

### Limestone
Carbonate rock. Can be dense or porous and can vary greatly in colour and texture.

### Marble
Metamorphosed carbonate rock, typically recrystallized. Decorative appearance varies by mineral impurities and veining.

### Slate
Fine-grained metamorphic rock with pronounced cleavage.

### Schist
Foliated metamorphic rock with visible mineral alignment.

### Quartzite
Quartz-rich metamorphic rock, generally hard and resistant.

### Laterite
Iron-rich weathered soil/rock material. Must not be treated as generic red stone.

### Soapstone / steatite
Soft talc-rich stone used historically for carving and objects.

### Agate
Banded/chalcedonic silica material used for beads and ornaments.

### Carnelian
Red-orange chalcedony variety.

### Jasper
Opaque microcrystalline silica with varied colours and patterns.

### Chalcedony
Microcrystalline silica family including several ornamental varieties.

### Flint / chert
Microcrystalline silica; fracture behavior differs from ordinary sandstone or limestone.

## Identity rule

"Stone" is a class, not a material identity.

Every architectural or prop asset should identify the actual stone where evidence permits.

## Historical note

Ancient Indian archaeological records document extensive stone working, stone shaping, stone carving and use of decorative/ornamental stones. Harappan evidence includes large-scale stone construction at Dholavira and ornamental stones such as carnelian.
