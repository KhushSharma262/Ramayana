# SURFACE AND ENVIRONMENT INTERACTION

## Scope

Document how surface structure can change through contact, moisture, deposits,
temperature, chemical exposure, wear, damage, and other relevant processes.

This folder records structural consequences and dependencies. Detailed
physical state-transition models belong to their designated project folders.

## Required distinctions

Separate:
- Inherent material structure.
- Processing-induced structure.
- Temporary deposits and contamination.
- Moisture or fluid at the surface.
- Wear and damage.
- Environmental changes.
- Artistic additions.

Do not conflate these categories merely because they look similar.

## Causal consistency

When applicable, identify the process, environmental conditions, affected
feature scale, directionality, time dependence, and reversibility.

Examples such as gravity-driven deposits, contact wear, or moisture-induced
changes are mechanisms to evaluate, not universal assumptions.

## State consistency

A feature must not appear, disappear, or change without a plausible transition
or a documented representation change. Preserve continuity across frames and
shots.

Do not assume that a wet, dusty, oxidized, polished, or damaged surface can
be represented solely by a color overlay.

## Validation

Test the intended state, transition, motion, lighting, and relevant camera
distances. Record assumptions and unresolved material-specific behavior.
