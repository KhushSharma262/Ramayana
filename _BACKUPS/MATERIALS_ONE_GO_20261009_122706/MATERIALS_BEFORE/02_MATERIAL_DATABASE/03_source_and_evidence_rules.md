# SOURCE AND EVIDENCE RULES

## Source classes

Use the global source classes established by `01_CORE`:

- direct_canon
- derived_canon
- traditional_interpretation
- historical_reconstruction
- scientific_fact
- engineering_reference
- visual_reconstruction
- directorial_choice
- production_choice
- unknown
- uncertain

## Priority

For a canonical claim:

direct_canon > derived_canon > traditional_interpretation > historical_reconstruction > visual_reconstruction > directorial_choice

For a scientific material property:

scientific_fact / engineering_reference > visual reconstruction > directorial choice.

For historical technology:

archaeological/historical evidence is preferred over modern aesthetic assumptions.

## Critical distinction

Three questions must not be conflated:

1. Could this material physically exist?
2. Was this material historically available in the relevant culture/period?
3. Does the source actually require this material in this Ramayana scene/object?

These are separate evidence questions.

## Conflict rule

When sources disagree:

- record both
- identify the conflict
- do not silently choose
- state the reason for any production decision
- mark the result as provisional if necessary

## Uncertainty

Use:

HIGH confidence
MEDIUM confidence
LOW confidence
UNKNOWN

A low-confidence identity must not be rendered as an exact species/alloy merely for visual convenience.

## Research note

The initial inventory is intentionally a broad production seed. Each Ramayana-specific assignment must receive its own evidence review before being locked.

## Legacy migration controls — Evidence traceability

The following controls preserve requirements recovered from the previous
MATERIALS system. Their inclusion does not by itself establish historical,
scientific, or production validation.

- Every material-specific claim should identify its source, locator, claim scope, evidence class, confidence, limitations, and linked material record.
- Separate source-supported claims from interpretation, scientific or engineering reference, historical reconstruction, visual reconstruction, and production choice.
- When sources conflict, preserve the alternatives and document the selected treatment and reason.
- Modern scientific data can inform physical behavior without proving that a particular material was used in the ancient setting.
- AI-generated imagery and cinematic references are not independent proof of historical material identity.

## Migrated legacy controls — material selection

Select and justify materials using the available evidence and the requirements of the scene. Evaluate the following together rather than choosing by appearance alone:

- **Evidence:** direct source evidence, traditional source evidence, historical research, technical reconstruction, or an explicitly identified directorial choice.
- **History and tradition:** relevant period, cultural, textual, and traditional context, where applicable and supported.
- **Function:** the object's intended use and the performance demanded of the material.
- **Environment:** exposure to weather, moisture, heat, soil, handling, and other scene conditions.
- **Character and context:** the established identity, role, resources, and circumstances of the character or object, where supported.
- **Construction:** plausible composition, assembly, finish, and manufacturing or craft method.
- **Physical plausibility:** whether the proposed material and its behavior are credible under the depicted conditions.
- **Continuity:** whether the choice preserves established material identity and state across shots and scenes.
- **Feasibility:** whether the choice can be represented consistently by the planned production and rendering workflow without disguising uncertainty as fact.

Do not treat technical reconstruction as canon or as direct textual evidence. If the source does not establish a material, document plausible alternatives, their supporting evidence, the selected option, confidence, and rationale. Mark the choice as a reconstruction or directorial choice when appropriate. Avoid anachronistic modern materials unless there is a documented justification.

These criteria guide decisions; they do not establish a particular material choice without evidence for that choice.
