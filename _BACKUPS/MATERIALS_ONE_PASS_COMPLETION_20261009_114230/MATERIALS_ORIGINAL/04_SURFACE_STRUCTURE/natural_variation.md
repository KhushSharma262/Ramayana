# NATURAL VARIATION AND CORRELATION

## Principle

Variation must be linked to a material, process, environmental condition,
or explicitly labelled production choice. Randomness alone is not a causal
explanation.

## Dimensions to consider

- Feature size, shape, and orientation.
- Density, spacing, clustering, and connectivity.
- Color and optical-response variation.
- Layer thickness and structural gradients.
- Grain, fiber, pore, and inclusion distributions.
- Wear, contamination, and environmental gradients.
- Correlation across neighboring features and scales.
- Temporal evolution and shot continuity.

## Correlated variation

Features produced by a common cause may share orientation, position,
distribution, or temporal behavior. Preserve these dependencies when supported
by the material and process.

Do not apply illustrative causal patterns as universal laws.

## Procedural controls

Document:
- Distribution family and its justification.
- Parameter values and uncertainty.
- Correlation length and directional dependence.
- Spatial-frequency limits.
- Random seed and reproducibility policy.
- Boundary conditions and layer relationships.
- Cross-scale dependencies.
- Clamping and failure behavior.
- Tiling prevention and continuity controls.

A distribution name does not establish realism. Validate it against data
or mark it as a reconstruction choice.

## Continuity

Camera movement changes projection and visibility, not physical feature size.
LOD must avoid popping, flicker, abrupt highlight changes, and unjustified
roughness changes.

## Validation

Test multiple seeds, adjacent shots, motion, and spatial statistics against
reference data when available.
