# SUB-MICRO STRUCTURE

## Scope

Structures below ordinary direct geometric resolution that may still affect
optical, thermal, mechanical, chemical, or fluid behavior.

Possible mechanisms include pores, inclusions, fine layering, particles,
fibrous arrangements, internal interfaces, and wavelength-scale structure.
Their presence and importance must be established for the material in question.

## Required distinction

Do not equate:
- Sub-microstructure with a generic noise texture.
- Physical particle size with texture pixel size.
- Optical roughness with a measured geometric height.
- A visually plausible normal map with a validated scattering model.
- A thin surface coating with a homogeneous bulk material.
- Surface detail with volumetric structure.

## Model selection

Choose a model appropriate to the relevant length scales and phenomenon.
Depending on the case, this may require an effective optical model,
a participating-medium model, a layered model, a wave-optics treatment,
or a justified approximation.

Do not apply a wave-optics or volumetric model solely because it sounds more
physically complete. Establish that its assumptions and scales are appropriate.

Record:
- Structural hypothesis and evidence.
- Characteristic dimensions and distributions.
- Relationship to wavelength and relevant physical scales.
- Effective-model assumptions.
- Parameters that are measured versus fitted or assumed.
- Model validity and known omissions.
- Required renderer or simulation support.

## Energy and scale consistency

Effective parameters must not be combined with explicit geometry in a way
that counts the same physical mechanism twice. Validate conservation and
limiting behavior for the chosen model.

## Uncertainty

Sub-microstructure is frequently not recoverable uniquely from a single
photograph. Mark non-identifiable features and parameters as uncertain.
Do not invent microscopic composition, internal structure, or optical
constants to explain an ambiguous appearance.

## Validation

Use suitable measurements, published reference data, calibrated samples,
or targeted simulation. A final image match alone does not uniquely validate
the underlying microscopic explanation.
