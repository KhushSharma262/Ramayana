# SUB-MICROSTRUCTURE

## Scope

Structure below ordinary direct geometric resolution that may still influence
optical, mechanical, thermal, chemical, or fluid behavior.

Possible mechanisms include internal pores, inclusions, fine layering,
particles, fibers, interfaces, and wavelength-scale structure. Their
presence and importance must be established for the particular material.

## Distinctions

Do not equate:
- Physical particle size with texture pixel size.
- A normal map with measured microstructure.
- Surface roughness with internal scattering.
- A homogeneous bulk material with a multilayer coating.
- Visual similarity with a uniquely established microscopic mechanism.
- A generic noise texture with physical evidence.

## Model selection

Select a model appropriate to the relevant scale and phenomenon. Depending
on the case, a validated effective model, layered model, participating-medium
model, or more detailed physical treatment may be appropriate.

Do not use a more complicated model merely to imply greater accuracy.
Record assumptions, applicable scales, evidence, and known omissions.

## Energy and overlap

Do not combine effective parameters and explicit structure in a way that
counts the same mechanism twice. Check the conservation and limiting behavior
appropriate to the chosen model.

## Uncertainty

A single photograph usually cannot uniquely establish microscopic composition
or structure. Record non-identifiable parameters as uncertain instead of
inventing them.

## Validation

Use appropriate measurements, published reference data, calibrated samples,
or targeted simulations. A visual match alone does not uniquely validate the
underlying microscopic explanation.
