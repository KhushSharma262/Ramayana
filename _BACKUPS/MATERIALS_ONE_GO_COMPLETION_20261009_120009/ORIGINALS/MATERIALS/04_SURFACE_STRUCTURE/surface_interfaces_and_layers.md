# SURFACE INTERFACES AND LAYERS

## Purpose

Define how structural boundaries between layers, coatings, fibers, particles,
substrates, and surrounding media are represented.

## Record when applicable

- Layer identity and order.
- Thickness and thickness variation.
- Interface geometry and continuity.
- Adhesion or separation evidence.
- Porosity and connectivity.
- Surface versus bulk location.
- Optical, mechanical, fluid, and thermal significance.
- Processing and state dependencies.
- Evidence, uncertainty, and validity limits.

## Physical consistency

Do not invent layers or coatings because they improve an image. Distinguish
observed structure from inferred or artistic reconstruction.

A thin layer may have effects not reproduced by changing the base material
alone. Select a model appropriate to the layer's thickness, optical behavior,
and relationship to other layers.

Avoid impossible intersections, gaps, layer-order changes, and inconsistent
thickness across shots.

## Representation and transport

Geometry, layered shaders, volume representations, and texture masks are not
interchangeable. State the chosen method and its limits.

Do not count the same coating, absorption, scattering, or roughness effect
both in an effective base parameter and in a separate layer without a
documented physical separation.

## Validation

Inspect grazing light, transmission where applicable, silhouettes, edges,
contact, motion, and representative layer thicknesses. Compare to measurement
or traceable evidence when available.
