# MEDIUM STRUCTURE

## Scope

Intermediate features such as grains, pores, fibers, weave, scales, pits,
inclusions, veins, layers, and clusters.

## Required descriptors where relevant

- Feature shape and dimensional distribution.
- Orientation, directionality, and anisotropy.
- Density, spacing, packing, and connectivity.
- Clustering and spatial correlation.
- Layer thickness and boundary structure.
- Relationship to macro geometry and microstructure.
- Relationship to processing, wear, and environment.
- Evidence and uncertainty.

## Statistical structure

Randomness alone is not a physical model. Determine whether observed features
are aligned, periodic, clustered, graded, connected, or spatially correlated.

Do not apply one generic noise pattern to unrelated materials. A procedural
distribution must be measured, justified by a source, or explicitly labelled
as a reconstruction or production choice.

Avoid tiling, cloned clusters, arbitrary orientation, and independent
randomization of features that should share a cause.

## Representation

Choose geometry, displacement, normal maps, texture-driven shading, or a
hybrid based on physical scale, parallax, occlusion, silhouette, camera
distance, and renderer capability.

Record filtering and LOD behavior. Keep dimensions consistent across all
maps and representations.

## Validation

Inspect close, medium, and distant views; changing orientations; grazing
light; motion; and LOD transitions. Compare statistical distributions to
reference measurements when possible.
