# CINEMATOGRAPHY PRINCIPLES

## PRIMARY PRINCIPLE

Cinematography is the controlled recording of physical reality to serve story, performance, space, time and emotion.

The system must never begin with a desired visual effect and invent physics afterward.

The correct causal order is:

STORY
→ SCENE
→ BEAT
→ STORY REQUIREMENT
→ VISUAL REQUIREMENT
→ PHYSICAL REQUIREMENT
→ CAMERA / LENS / FRAMING / LIGHTING / GRAMMAR / SHOT DESIGN
→ VALIDATION
→ FINAL IMAGE

## PRIORITY HIERARCHY

1. Physical reality
2. Optical and camera physics
3. Material and light behavior
4. Spatial continuity
5. Temporal continuity
6. Human/operator plausibility
7. Shot and editorial grammar
8. Composition and framing
9. Narrative and emotional intent
10. Stylistic character

A lower layer may never violate a higher layer merely to create a cinematic effect.

## REAL-LIVE-ACTION REQUIREMENT

Every generated shot must be plausible as footage captured in a real physical environment.

Avoid:

- plastic or wax-like skin
- artificial eyes
- unstable facial geometry
- texture crawling
- impossible reflections
- floating objects
- inconsistent shadows
- impossible motion blur
- artificial depth blur
- unstable hair or fabric
- unexplained glow
- digital-looking camera movement
- synthetic lens artifacts
- temporal flicker
- geometry morphing
- physically impossible perspective

When uncertain, choose the simpler physically plausible solution.

## MINIMUM INTERVENTION

Do not add a camera movement, optical effect, lighting effect, framing device or transition unless it has a physical, narrative or editorial reason.

## HUMAN CINEMATOGRAPHIC JUDGMENT

Professional cinematography is deliberate rather than mechanically perfect.

Human operation may produce subtle:

- correction
- settling
- timing variation
- repositioning
- tracking imperfection
- focus adjustment

These must remain physically plausible and subordinate to the shot's purpose.

Human realism does not mean random shake.

## CONTINUITY

Camera, lens, lighting, geography, subject position, screen direction, focus, performance and environmental state persist across shots unless a justified change occurs.

## EMOTION

Emotion is a narrative requirement, not a preset.

Never use mappings such as:

sad = close-up
angry = handheld
powerful = low angle
divine = glow
horrifying = red light

Instead:

emotion
→ dramatic requirement
→ visual requirement
→ physical cinematographic solution

## FINAL RULE

If an effect looks impressive but would reveal that the footage is AI-generated, it is a failure.

Realism is not an optional style.
