# CINEMATOGRAPHY HISTORY

## PURPOSE

Track how cinematographic practice developed across technological eras.

==================================================
ERAS

Research may cover:

- early photographic cinema
- silent cinema
- studio-era cinematography
- location cinematography
- color-film development
- widescreen systems
- anamorphic cinema
- lightweight cameras
- documentary realism
- digital cinema
- modern large-format capture
- virtual production.

==================================================
HISTORICAL CONTEXT

For every historical technique consider:

TECHNOLOGY
+
AVAILABLE EQUIPMENT
+
PHYSICAL LIMITATIONS
+
PRODUCTION PRACTICE
+
ARTISTIC INTENT.

==================================================
IMPORTANT

Historical limitations often explain visual characteristics.

Do not recreate a historical visual characteristic without understanding its physical cause.

==================================================
EXAMPLE

A historical image may appear:

- softer
- lower contrast
- grainier
- less stable
- differently exposed.

Possible causes include:

- film stock
- lens
- filtration
- processing
- optical design
- camera support
- lighting
- exposure.

Do not collapse these into one "vintage look."

==================================================
PROJECT USE

Historical research is useful for understanding physical cinematography.

It does not automatically determine the project's visual style.

==================================================
FINAL RULE

Understand the cause before reproducing the appearance.
