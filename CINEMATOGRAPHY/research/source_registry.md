# SOURCE REGISTRY

## PURPOSE

Track the origin and reliability of cinematography research.

==================================================
SOURCE CLASSES

### PRIMARY

Highest-value sources include:

- cinematographer interviews
- production notes
- camera reports
- lens reports
- technical manuals
- behind-the-scenes documentation
- production stills
- original footage
- verified crew statements
- official technical documentation.

### SECONDARY

Useful sources include:

- reputable cinematography publications
- professional interviews
- technical analysis
- educational material from recognized professionals
- scholarly cinematography research.

### TERTIARY

Lower-confidence sources include:

- forums
- social media
- unsourced articles
- fan analysis
- anonymous claims.

These may generate hypotheses but should not establish technical facts alone.

SOURCE HIERARCHY

When sources conflict:

PRIMARY
>
SECONDARY
>
TERTIARY

However, a primary source describing artistic intent does not automatically establish technical fact.

==================================================
SOURCE AGE

Historical sources may be extremely valuable.

Do not reject old information simply because it is old.

Instead determine whether it describes:

- historical practice
- current practice
- obsolete equipment
- enduring physical principle.

==================================================
FINAL RULE

Every important technical claim should be traceable to a source or explicitly marked as inference.
