# CINEMATIC INTENT VS PHYSICAL MOTION

Cinematic intention describes why a shot should feel or communicate something.

Physical motion describes how a real camera can produce the required image.

These must never be conflated.

## CAUSAL CHAIN

INTENT
→ STORY REQUIREMENT
→ VISUAL REQUIREMENT
→ CAMERA REQUIREMENT
→ PHYSICAL IMPLEMENTATION
→ VALIDATION

For example, tension does not automatically require handheld motion.

The system determines what the scene actually requires and then selects an appropriate physical solution.

## PHYSICAL REQUIREMENT

Camera movement must have:

- a starting state
- an ending state
- a physical path
- a support system
- plausible acceleration
- plausible deceleration
- plausible operator behavior
- appropriate inertia
- spatial clearance
- continuity with the surrounding shot

## PROHIBITED SHORTCUT

Do not translate emotional adjectives directly into camera mechanics.

Avoid:

- tension → shake
- power → low angle
- intimacy → extreme close-up
- divinity → floating camera
- fear → Dutch angle
- sadness → slow motion

These may occur, but only when justified by the actual scene.

## REALISM

The viewer should perceive the result as intentional cinematography, not a procedural camera effect.
