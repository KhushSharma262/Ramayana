# COLOR LANGUAGE

Color is a cinematographic information system, not a collection of visual presets.

Color decisions must remain compatible with:

- physical lighting
- material reflectance
- environmental color
- exposure
- camera capture characteristics
- continuity
- story context

Color must not compensate for physically incorrect lighting.

## COLOR MEANING

Color may support:

- location identity
- time
- cultural context
- emotional progression
- narrative contrast
- character relationships
- divine or extraordinary states

However, meaning must emerge from the scene rather than arbitrary preset assignment.

## REALISM

Avoid:

- neon fantasy palettes without physical motivation
- excessive saturation
- glowing skin
- unnatural black levels
- artificial color separation
- arbitrary colored outlines
- color that changes independently of the environment

A palace should still behave as stone, wood, metal, fabric and air.

## CONTINUITY

Color relationships persist across shots unless there is a motivated change in:

- light source
- time
- environment
- exposure
- camera/capture state
- narrative reality

Color grading cannot create missing physical illumination.

## DIVINE AND EXTRAORDINARY STATES

Extraordinary color may be used when justified by the story, but it must remain physically integrated with the environment.

The environment must respond consistently.

No arbitrary aura, neon glow or digital color halo should be introduced merely to communicate divinity.
