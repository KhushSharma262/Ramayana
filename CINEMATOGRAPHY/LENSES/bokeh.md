### BOKEH BEHAVIOUR

Render bokeh as a natural optical consequence of the selected lens, aperture, focus distance, subject distance, and depth of field.

Do not add bokeh automatically to every shot.

Bokeh should only become visually noticeable when there are sufficiently out-of-focus elements or point-like light sources in the foreground or background.

When bokeh is present:

- render naturally defocused background and foreground elements
- maintain realistic depth-of-field falloff
- preserve a clear transition between in-focus and out-of-focus regions
- keep bokeh consistent with the selected focal length and aperture
- make out-of-focus highlights respond naturally to the lens's optical character
- preserve realistic highlight brightness and shape
- maintain natural variation in bokeh size according to distance from the focal plane
- keep the subject's important features appropriately sharp unless shallow depth of field is intentionally specified

### BOKEH SHAPE

Bokeh shape should follow the selected lens design.

Spherical lenses:
- generally produce approximately circular or aperture-shaped highlights
- may show cat-eye deformation toward the frame edges
- may show mechanical vignetting depending on lens design and aperture

Anamorphic lenses:
- may produce subtly horizontally elongated / oval highlights
- apply this only when consistent with the selected anamorphic system
- do not exaggerate the oval shape

Vintage or character lenses:
- may produce distinctive highlight shapes, swirls, unevenness, or optical aberrations
- use these only when the selected lens profile explicitly calls for them

Do not artificially stretch every background light into an obvious bokeh shape.

### BOKEH INTENSITY

Default:
NATURAL / SUBTLE

Increase bokeh visibility only when justified by:

- wide aperture
- longer focal length
- close subject distance
- greater subject-to-background distance
- shallow depth of field
- nearby point light sources
- intentional subject isolation

Reduce bokeh when:

- aperture is narrow
- the scene requires environmental context
- subjects occupy multiple depth planes
- the background contains important story information
- deep focus is cinematographically appropriate

### DEPTH-OF-FIELD CONSISTENCY

Bokeh must correspond to the actual depth-of-field characteristics of the shot.

Do not create:
- a sharply focused face with an implausibly blurred object immediately beside it
- extreme background blur with a physically inappropriate aperture
- uniformly blurred backgrounds regardless of distance
- artificial "portrait mode" cutouts
- inconsistent focus between adjacent objects at similar distances

The transition from sharp to soft must be gradual and optically plausible.

### FOREGROUND BOKEH

When appropriate, use out-of-focus foreground elements to create depth.

Foreground bokeh may partially obscure the frame, but must not accidentally obscure important facial features, actions, or story information.

Use foreground bokeh deliberately for:
- depth
- framing
- intimacy
- visual layering
- environmental immersion

### BACKGROUND BOKEH

Background bokeh should help separate the subject without completely destroying the environment.

For wide environmental shots, keep sufficient background detail to preserve spatial context.

For intimate close-ups, stronger background separation may be appropriate.

### LIGHT SOURCES

Point-like sources such as:

- lamps
- candles
- torches
- diyas
- stars
- distant practical lights
- reflections
- fire highlights

may produce visible bokeh when they fall outside the focal plane.

Their appearance must remain consistent with the selected lens and aperture.

Do not turn every light source into a glowing circular orb.

### VIDEO CONSISTENCY

Because this is AI-generated VIDEO:

Bokeh must remain temporally stable.

During:
- camera movement
- subject movement
- rack focus
- dolly movement
- changes in subject distance

the size, position, shape and intensity of bokeh should change naturally according to the changing optical geometry.

Do not allow bokeh to:
- randomly pop in or out
- change shape between frames
- shimmer unnaturally
- flicker
- jump in size
- detach from its corresponding light source

During a rack focus, background bokeh should progressively transition between sharp and defocused states rather than switching instantaneously.

### STORY-FIRST RULE

Bokeh must serve the shot rather than become the visual subject.

Use it when it helps:
- isolate a character
- create depth
- establish intimacy
- guide attention
- enhance atmosphere
- separate foreground, subject and background

Avoid strong bokeh when:
- environmental information is important
- multiple characters must remain readable
- geography of the scene is important
- the shot requires deep focus
- excessive blur would reduce realism

### NATURALISM RULE

The final result should look as though the image was captured through a real physical lens with realistic optical depth of field.

Do NOT use:
- artificial background blur
- uniform Gaussian blur
- excessive cinematic bokeh
- oversized glowing circles
- perfectly identical bokeh shapes
- unrealistic subject cutouts
- exaggerated shallow depth of field

Bokeh should be a consequence of the lens and shot parameters, not an added visual effect.

### PRIORITY

Determine in this order:

1. Shot purpose
2. Lens selection
3. Focal length
4. Aperture
5. Focus distance
6. Subject distance
7. Background distance
8. Depth of field
9. Resulting bokeh

Never decide "add bokeh" first and then construct the shot around it.
