### BOKEH BEHAVIOUR

Render bokeh only as a physically plausible optical consequence of the selected lens, aperture, focus distance, subject distance, sensor format, and depth of field.

Do not add bokeh automatically to every shot.

Bokeh becomes visually noticeable only when sufficiently out-of-focus elements or point-like or small bright sources are present in the foreground or background and the optical conditions allow them to resolve as defocused highlights.

When bokeh is present:

- render naturally defocused foreground and background elements according to their physical distance from the focal plane
- maintain physically realistic depth-of-field falloff
- preserve a continuous and physically plausible transition between in-focus and out-of-focus regions
- keep defocus behaviour consistent with the selected focal length, aperture, focus distance, sensor format, and lens design
- make out-of-focus highlights respond to the actual optical character of the selected lens
- preserve physically plausible highlight brightness, shape, size, and intensity
- make bokeh size and degree of defocus vary according to object distance, focal-plane distance, focal length, aperture, and lens geometry
- do not introduce arbitrary bokeh variation between nearby objects that have equivalent optical conditions
- preserve subject detail according to the physically resulting depth of field; do not artificially sharpen or soften important features to compensate for an incorrect focus or depth-of-field result

### BOKEH SHAPE

Bokeh shape must be derived from the selected lens design, aperture geometry, optical construction, sensor format, focus distance, and position of the defocused highlight within the image.

Spherical lenses:
- generally produce approximately circular or aperture-shaped defocused highlights
- may produce cat-eye deformation toward the frame edges when supported by the lens design and mechanical vignetting
- may show mechanical vignetting depending on lens construction, aperture, and ray geometry

Anamorphic lenses:
- may produce subtly horizontally elongated or oval defocused highlights when supported by the selected anamorphic optical design
- apply the shape consistently with the selected anamorphic system and shot conditions
- do not exaggerate ovality beyond what the physical lens design supports

Vintage or character lenses:
- may produce distinctive highlight shapes, swirls, unevenness, or optical aberrations only when the selected physical lens profile explicitly supports those characteristics
- never introduce character-lens bokeh merely to make the image appear cinematic, vintage, artistic, or distinctive
- optical character must remain consistent with the selected lens throughout the shot and sequence

Never use bokeh shape to conceal incorrect focus, depth of field, geometry, lighting, or subject rendering.
### BOKEH INTENSITY

Default:
NATURAL / SUBTLE

Bokeh visibility must be determined by the physically resulting degree of defocus and the optical characteristics of the selected lens and camera configuration.

Increase bokeh visibility only when physically supported by:
- wider aperture
- longer focal length
- closer subject distance when combined with the required framing
- greater subject-to-background distance
- greater defocus relative to the focal plane
- suitable lens design and optical construction
- sensor format and active sensor area
- appropriate focus distance
- sufficiently small or point-like bright sources that can resolve as defocused highlights
- actual optical conditions that produce visible bokeh

Creative intent may determine whether a physically available bokeh characteristic is desirable, but creative intent must never manufacture, exaggerate, or override physically unsupported bokeh.

Reduce bokeh visibility when physically supported by:
- narrower aperture
- shorter focal length
- reduced subject-to-background distance
- reduced defocus
- deeper depth of field
- focus conditions that keep background or foreground elements sufficiently near the focal plane

Do not suppress physically required bokeh merely because the scene is intended to look clean or cinematic.

Do not increase bokeh merely because:
- the shot is cinematic
- the subject should feel isolated
- the scene is emotional
- the scene is dramatic
- the background should look attractive
- the image is intended to look professional

When bokeh is physically weak or absent:
    bokeh visibility = NONE or SUBTLE according to the actual optical conditions.

When bokeh is physically strong:
    render it only to the degree supported by the selected lens, aperture,
    focus distance, subject/background geometry, sensor format, lighting,
    exposure, and optical construction.

Never use digital blur, artificial highlight shapes, or arbitrary background
softening to simulate unsupported optical bokeh.
### DEPTH-OF-FIELD CONSISTENCY

Bokeh must correspond to the actual depth-of-field characteristics of the shot.

Depth of field and defocus must be derived from the physical camera and lens configuration, including:
- sensor format and active sensor area
- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- subject distance
- foreground and background distances
- lens optical design
- camera position
- required framing

Do not create:
- a sharply focused face with an implausibly blurred object immediately beside it
- extreme background blur with a physically inappropriate aperture
- uniformly blurred backgrounds regardless of distance
- artificial "portrait mode" cutouts
- inconsistent focus between adjacent objects at similar distances
- arbitrary blur boundaries that do not correspond to scene geometry
- digitally simulated bokeh where the physical optical configuration would not produce it

The transition from sharp to soft must follow the physically resulting circle-of-confusion and depth-of-field behaviour of the selected camera and lens configuration.

Objects at similar distances from the focal plane must exhibit consistent defocus behaviour unless their optical conditions genuinely differ.

### FOREGROUND BOKEH

Foreground bokeh may occur when foreground elements are sufficiently out of focus relative to the selected focal plane and the lens, aperture, focus distance, and scene geometry physically produce visible defocus.

Foreground bokeh may partially obscure the frame, but it must not be artificially introduced or strengthened merely to create depth, framing, intimacy, or atmosphere.

If foreground elements physically fall within the depth-of-field region, they must not be artificially blurred.

When physically present, foreground defocus may contribute to:
- visual layering
- framing
- subject isolation
- environmental immersion
- perceived depth cues

Bokeh itself must not be treated as the source of physical scene depth. Depth must remain consistent with actual camera position, scene geometry, object distances, perspective, and lighting.

Foreground bokeh must not accidentally obscure important facial features, actions, or story information when the physical composition does not require such obstruction.

### BACKGROUND BOKEH

Background bokeh must result from the actual defocus of background elements relative to the selected focal plane.

Background separation must remain consistent with:
- aperture
- focal length
- focus distance
- subject/background distance
- sensor format
- lens design
- camera position
- required framing

Do not increase background blur merely because stronger subject isolation would appear more cinematic.

For wide environmental shots, preserve background detail according to the physically resulting depth of field and do not artificially restore or suppress detail.

For intimate close-ups, stronger background separation is permitted only when the physical camera and lens configuration actually produces it.

The background must not become uniformly blurred when different background elements occupy materially different distances from the focal plane.
### LIGHT SOURCES

Point-like or sufficiently small bright sources such as:

- lamps
- candles
- torches
- diyas
- stars
- distant practical lights
- small specular reflections
- fire highlights

may produce visible bokeh only when they are sufficiently defocused relative to the focal plane and the selected lens, aperture, focus distance, sensor format, exposure, and optical construction physically support visible defocused rendering.

A source must not be treated as a bokeh source merely because it is bright.

Large-area light sources, diffuse illumination, and broad reflections should not be converted into artificial circular or geometric bokeh unless the physical optical conditions actually produce such defocused structure.

Their appearance must remain consistent with the selected lens, aperture, focus distance, exposure, and optical conditions.

Do not turn every light source into a glowing circular orb.

Do not invent bokeh highlights that have no corresponding physical light source, reflection, or bright scene detail.

### VIDEO CONSISTENCY

Because this is AI-generated VIDEO:

Bokeh must remain temporally stable and physically continuous.

During:
- camera movement
- subject movement
- rack focus
- dolly movement
- changes in subject distance
- changes in camera-to-subject distance
- changes in aperture or exposure
- changes in lighting

the size, position, shape, brightness, and degree of defocus of bokeh must change continuously according to the changing optical geometry and exposure conditions.

Bokeh characteristics must not change independently of the scene, camera, lens, focus, aperture, lighting, or exposure state.

Do not allow bokeh to:
- randomly pop in or out
- jump position
- change shape without a physical cause
- change size without a corresponding change in optical geometry
- flicker independently of the source or exposure
- multiply, duplicate, or disappear without a corresponding scene event
- crawl, shimmer, morph, or wobble unnaturally
- remain fixed in screen space while the physical scene or camera moves
- change optical character between frames without a change in the selected lens or physical conditions
### STORY-FIRST RULE

Bokeh must serve the shot rather than become the visual subject.

Story intent may determine whether physically available bokeh is desirable,
but story intent must never manufacture, exaggerate, or override physically
unsupported bokeh.

Use physically resulting bokeh when it helps:
- isolate a character
- reinforce existing depth cues
- establish intimacy
- guide attention through physically plausible focus
- support atmosphere when the optical conditions naturally produce it
- separate foreground, subject, and background when their physical distances and depth of field support the separation

Do not create bokeh merely to:
- make a shot appear more cinematic
- make a character appear more important
- create artificial depth
- create artificial atmosphere
- imitate photographic references
- compensate for weak composition or lighting

Avoid strong bokeh when:
- environmental information is important
- multiple characters must remain readable
- geography of the scene is important
- the shot requires deep focus
- excessive optical defocus would reduce physical realism
- the required camera and lens configuration would not produce the requested degree of blur

### NATURALISM RULE

The final result must look as though the image was captured through a real physical lens with physically consistent depth of field and optical defocus.

Do NOT use:
- artificial background blur
- uniform Gaussian blur
- excessive cinematic bokeh
- oversized glowing circles unsupported by the optical conditions
- perfectly identical bokeh shapes when the physical geometry would produce variation
- unrealistic subject cutouts
- exaggerated shallow depth of field
- digitally painted or screen-space bokeh
- blur masks that ignore scene depth
- bokeh that remains fixed in screen space while the physical camera or scene moves

All visible bokeh must correspond to an actual defocused scene element, bright source, reflection, or highlight and must remain consistent with the selected lens, aperture, focus distance, sensor format, exposure, lighting, and scene geometry.

When physical conditions do not support visible bokeh:
    render NONE.

When physical conditions support only weak bokeh:
    render SUBTLE.

Never increase bokeh solely to satisfy a stylistic or cinematic preference.
### PRIORITY

Determine in this order:

1. Shot purpose
2. Physical camera configuration
3. Lens system and lens selection
4. Sensor format and active sensor area
5. Camera position and orientation
6. Focal length
7. Aperture
8. Focus distance
9. Subject distance
10. Foreground and background distances
11. Lighting and exposure conditions
12. Depth of field
13. Physically resulting bokeh

Never decide "add bokeh" first and then construct the shot around it.

Never modify the physical camera, lens, aperture, focus, geometry, lighting,
or exposure merely to produce a desired bokeh effect unless that physical
configuration is independently justified by the shot.

Physical optical behaviour has priority over cinematic preference.

If the physical configuration does not produce visible bokeh:
    bokeh = NONE.

If it produces weak bokeh:
    bokeh = SUBTLE.

If it produces strong bokeh:
    render only the physically resulting degree of defocus.

Bokeh must always be a consequence of the physical shot configuration,
never the cause of that configuration.
