# ULTRA-WIDE LENS BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control the physical optical behaviour of ultra-wide lenses.

An ultra-wide lens provides a very large field of view relative to the selected sensor format and can strongly reveal spatial relationships when the camera is positioned close to foreground subjects.

Ultra-wide behaviour must emerge from:

- physical focal length
- sensor format
- active sensor area
- camera position
- subject distance
- subject depth
- background distance
- lens projection
- lens distortion
- focus distance
- aperture
- lens design
- camera movement
- lighting

An ultra-wide lens is NOT:

- a digital wide-angle effect
- an automatic distortion filter
- an automatic perspective-expansion filter
- a fake immersive-camera effect
- a mandatory action-camera look
- an automatic deep-focus lens
- an automatic fisheye lens

==================================================

==================================================

Ultra-wide behaviour must always emerge from a physically valid camera, lens, sensor, projection, subject distance, and scene geometry.

ULTRA-WIDE is primarily a field-of-view classification.

It is NOT:

- a generic distortion effect
- a synthetic perspective effect
- a fake immersive effect
- a digital widening filter
- a mandatory fisheye appearance.

Choose the physically plausible and least noticeable result.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / PROJECTION FIREWALL
==================================================

Ultra-wide does not automatically mean fisheye.

Determine the actual projection type first:

- rectilinear
- fisheye
- specialized projection
- anamorphic projection where applicable.

A rectilinear ultra-wide must not inherit fisheye curvature.

A fisheye must not be treated as a normal rectilinear ultra-wide.

Do not use projection changes merely to increase visual drama.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / CAMERA-POSITION FIREWALL
==================================================

Perspective is determined by camera position and scene geometry.

A wide field of view does not independently create exaggerated perspective.

Strong foreground/background scale differences require appropriate camera proximity.

If the camera is moved closer:

relative scale differences may increase.

If the camera remains fixed and focal length changes:

FOV and framing change, while perspective geometry remains fundamentally unchanged.

Never manufacture perspective through digital warping.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / DISTORTION FIREWALL
==================================================

Lens distortion is independent from perspective.

Do not use barrel, moustache, or other distortion to manufacture:

- depth
- immersion
- scale
- perspective
- spatial expansion.

Distortion must follow the selected lens profile and image position.

Highly corrected modern ultra-wide lenses must not be given exaggerated vintage distortion without explicit lens justification.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / EDGE FIREWALL
==================================================

Edge behaviour must remain physically dependent on:

- lens design
- projection
- focal length
- frame position
- sensor format
- image circle.

Do not automatically stretch objects near the edges.

Critical faces, limbs, architecture, and objects must retain stable geometry unless their physical position relative to the lens justifies the observed perspective.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / FACE FIREWALL
==================================================

Close faces can exhibit strong perspective changes when positioned near an ultra-wide lens.

This must arise from actual camera distance.

Never use ultra-wide optics to justify:

- changing facial proportions between frames
- enlarged eyes
- stretched noses
- widened jaws
- unstable ears
- changing head shape
- artificial facial distortion.

If facial geometry becomes implausible:

recalculate camera distance, focal length, framing, or lens choice.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / SENSOR FIREWALL
==================================================

The lens must physically cover the active sensor.

If:

IMAGE_CIRCLE < ACTIVE_SENSOR_AREA

then the configuration is invalid unless the actual camera/lens mode explicitly permits that coverage.

Do not conceal incompatible coverage through:

- digital cropping
- generated image extension
- corner repair
- artificial vignette removal
- synthetic edge reconstruction.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / ANAMORPHIC FIREWALL
==================================================

Ultra-wide does not imply spherical or anamorphic.

Determine optical format first.

If spherical:

do not add:

- anamorphic squeeze
- oval bokeh
- anamorphic horizontal streaks
- anamorphic edge behaviour.

If anamorphic:

apply only the selected lens profile's actual behaviour.

Never use generic "cinematic anamorphic" characteristics.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / FOCUS FIREWALL
==================================================

Focus must remain physically valid.

Respect:

- minimum focus distance
- focus distance
- aperture
- depth of field
- focus transition
- breathing
- subject/background distance.

Never compensate for an impossible focus configuration with:

- digital blur
- depth masks
- AI focus
- sharpening
- synthetic macro.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / PARALLAX FIREWALL
==================================================

Parallax must emerge from real camera translation through a real three-dimensional scene.

During:

- dolly
- tracking
- orbit
- crane
- jib

near objects must exhibit greater relative motion than distant objects.

Occlusion must change continuously.

Never construct parallax using:

- 2D image layers
- independent object translation
- depth-map warping
- background sliding.

==================================================
ULTRA-WIDE / MOVEMENT FIREWALL
==================================================

Camera movement must remain physically coherent.

A dolly changes:

- camera position
- perspective
- parallax
- spatial relationships.

A zoom changes:

- focal length
- FOV
- framing
- magnification.

A dolly zoom requires both.

Never replace either physical operation with digital scaling.

- lens design

==================================================
TEMPORAL ULTRA-WIDE VALIDATION
==================================================

Across consecutive frames:

- focal length remains stable unless zooming
- FOV changes only when focal length changes
- camera position changes continuously
- perspective changes only with camera movement
- distortion remains consistent with lens position
- edge behaviour remains stable
- parallax remains continuous
- occlusion remains coherent
- focus transitions remain continuous
- optical artifacts remain physically motivated
- architecture remains geometrically stable
- faces remain stable.

No unexplained optical change is permitted.

==================================================
FIELD OF VIEW
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses capture a large portion of the environment.

FOV must be physically calculated from:

- focal length
- sensor dimensions
- active sensor area
- aspect ratio
- projection type.

Do not digitally widen a normal lens.

Do not crop or stretch an image to simulate ultra-wide optics.

==================================================
SENSOR FORMAT
==================================================

Sensor format changes the FOV produced by a given focal length.

For smaller sensors:

the same physical focal length produces a narrower FOV.

Crop factor is a FOV/framing relationship.

It does NOT:

- change physical focal length
- create perspective
- create distortion
- create compression
- create anamorphic behaviour.

Always use the actual active sensor area.

==================================================
PROJECTION
==================================================

Most conventional ultra-wide cinema lenses use rectilinear projection.

Rectilinear ultra-wide lenses attempt to keep straight lines straight, subject to the lens's actual distortion profile.

Do not assume that every ultra-wide lens bends straight lines dramatically.

==================================================
FISHEYE
==================================================

Fisheye is a specialized projection.

It must NOT be automatically applied to ultra-wide lenses.

Only use fisheye behaviour when the selected lens explicitly supports a fisheye projection.

Fisheye may produce:

- strongly curved lines
- extreme edge stretching
- distinctive spatial rendering.

Without explicit fisheye selection:

NO FISHEYE PROJECTION.

==================================================
PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses can reveal strong perspective relationships when the camera is close.

For example:

a nearby object may appear significantly larger than a distant object.

This is a real consequence of camera position.

Do not artificially enlarge foreground objects.

Do not digitally shrink backgrounds.

==================================================
FOREGROUND SCALE
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses are particularly sensitive to foreground placement.

A subject very close to the camera may appear substantially larger relative to distant subjects.

This can be used for:

- immersive viewpoints
- environmental character shots
- dynamic movement
- large foreground objects
- architectural interiors
- battlefield foregrounds.

The effect must remain physically plausible.

==================================================
FACIAL PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses placed close to faces can produce strong perspective.

Possible consequences:

- nearer facial features appear larger
- nose may appear more prominent
- ears may appear farther back
- facial depth becomes more pronounced.

This must remain anatomically plausible.

Do not use ultra-wide lenses close to faces unless the perspective is narratively justified.

NEVER allow:

- stretched noses
- enlarged eyes
- distorted jaws
- unstable facial proportions
- face morphing.

If facial accuracy is critical:

increase camera distance and/or select a more appropriate focal length.

==================================================
ARCHITECTURE
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses are useful for:

- palaces
- temples
- halls
- courtyards
- caves
- forests
- large interiors
- monumental structures.

Maintain:

- stable geometry
- physically correct perspective convergence
- coherent verticals
- consistent architectural scale.

Do not artificially bend architecture.

==================================================
VERTICALS
==================================================

If the camera is tilted upward or downward:

vertical lines may converge because of perspective.

This is not automatically lens distortion.

Distinguish:

PERSPECTIVE CONVERGENCE

from:

OPTICAL DISTORTION.

Do not digitally straighten lines unless the workflow explicitly specifies optical correction.

==================================================
LENS DISTORTION
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses may have:

- barrel distortion
- pincushion distortion
- moustache distortion
- complex distortion
- highly corrected rectilinear projection.

Distortion must come from the selected lens profile.

Do not assume:

ULTRA-WIDE = HEAVY BARREL DISTORTION.

Modern ultra-wide cinema lenses can be strongly corrected.

==================================================
EDGE BEHAVIOUR
==================================================

Objects near the frame edges may appear more spatially exaggerated because of the combination of:

- wide FOV
- camera position
- perspective
- lens projection
- optical distortion.

Maintain continuous geometry as objects move through the frame.

Do not allow:

- sudden stretching
- edge morphing
- disappearing geometry
- changing object proportions.

==================================================
SUBJECT PLACEMENT
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses make subject placement especially important.

A subject near the centre generally experiences less edge-related distortion than a subject near the extreme frame boundary, depending on the lens.

Place important faces and geometry intentionally.

Do not allow critical facial features to sit against extreme frame edges without narrative justification.

==================================================
SPATIAL DEPTH
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses can reveal strong foreground/midground/background separation.

Use genuine:

- foreground
- midground
- background
- occlusion
- parallax
- atmospheric perspective.

Do not manufacture depth using digital layers.

DEPTH OF FIELD
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses often permit substantial depth of field, particularly when:

- aperture is stopped down
- focus distance is moderate/far
- subjects are not extremely close.

However:

ultra-wide does NOT automatically mean deep focus.

DOF depends on:

- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject distance
- background distance
- circle of confusion.

==================================================
APERTURE
==================================================

Select aperture based on:

- desired DOF
- subject priority
- lighting
- movement
- focus tolerance
- optical character.

Do not automatically stop down ultra-wide lenses.

Do not automatically shoot wide open.

==================================================
FOCUS
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses can make focus less visually sensitive at normal distances, but close subjects can become highly sensitive.

Focus must remain physically valid.

For close foreground subjects:

- focus distance must be precise
- MFD must be respected
- DOF must be physically derived.

==================================================
HYPERFOCAL / DEEP FOCUS
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses can be useful for deep-focus compositions.

If deep focus is required:

derive it through:

- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor
- circle of confusion.

Do not use artificial sharpening to simulate deep focus.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITIONS
==================================================

Rack focus on ultra-wide lenses must remain optical.

During the transition:

- focal plane moves continuously
- foreground/background sharpness changes naturally
- bokeh changes according to optical conditions
- breathing follows the lens profile.

Do not use digital depth masks.

- camera position

==================================================
BOKEH
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses can produce visible bokeh when:

- the subject is very close
- aperture is wide
- background is sufficiently distant
- focus distance is short.

Do not assume ultra-wide lenses always produce strong bokeh.

Bokeh must be derived from:

- lens design
- aperture shape
- aperture setting
- focus distance
- subject/background distances.

==================================================
FOREGROUND BOKEH
==================================================

Close foreground objects may fall dramatically out of focus on an ultra-wide lens when the focus distance is very short.

This can be used for:

- framing
- immersion
- environmental transitions
- visual depth.

Do not use artificial blur overlays.

==================================================
ANAMORPHIC VS SPHERICAL

- spherical

- anamorphic squeeze

==================================================
FLARE
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses may encounter strong flare because their large FOV can include bright sources near the frame.

Possible sources:

- sun
- bright sky
- fire
- torches
- lamps
- reflections.

Flare depends on:

- source position
- lens coatings
- aperture
- optical construction
- filters.

Do not add flare simply because the lens is ultra-wide.

==================================================
GHOSTING
==================================================

Strong light sources may produce internal reflections.

Ghosting must remain:

- lens-dependent
- source-dependent
- physically positioned
- temporally stable.

Never create floating screen-space artifacts.

==================================================

==================================================

An ultra-wide lens can include the sun while retaining a large environmental field.

Possible optical effects:

- veiling flare
- subtle ghosting
- reduced contrast
- highlight wash.

- giant rainbow circles
- blue streaks

==================================================
CHROMATIC ABERRATION
==================================================

Possible conditions:

- high-contrast branches against sky
- bright architecture against dark background
- reflective surfaces
- strong highlights.

Keep CA:

- subtle
- edge-dependent
- lens-dependent.

Never create obvious RGB outlines.

==================================================
FIELD CURVATURE
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses may exhibit field curvature depending on optical design.

This can influence edge sharpness relative to the centre.

==================================================
VIGNETTING

- optical design
- image circle
- aperture
- sensor coverage

- natural mountain proportions.

Do not artificially shrink or enlarge mountains.

==================================================
ATMOSPHERIC PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Ultra-wide images may contain more environmental atmosphere.

Represent:

- haze
- dust
- mist
- smoke
- humidity

only when physically supported.

Do not add uniform atmospheric haze.

==================================================
OPTICAL CHARACTER
==================================================

Ultra-wide lenses may be:

- highly corrected
- neutral
- vintage
- soft
- high contrast
- low contrast
- character-oriented.

Do not automatically make ultra-wide lenses:

- distorted
- soft
- dreamy
- vintage
- exaggerated.

Use the actual lens profile.

==================================================
MODERN ULTRA-WIDE
==================================================

Modern cinema ultra-wide lenses may provide:

- strong correction
- high resolution
- controlled CA
- controlled flare
- restrained distortion.

Do not add historical lens imperfections unless the selected lens supports them.

==================================================
CHARACTER ULTRA-WIDE
==================================================

A character ultra-wide lens may exhibit:

- stronger distortion
- edge softness
- more flare
- greater CA
- unusual bokeh
- stronger field curvature.

These properties must remain:

- lens-specific
- spatially coherent
- temporally stable.

==================================================
TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Ultra-wide optical behaviour must remain stable across frames.

NEVER allow:

- FOV flicker
- distortion flicker
- edge stretching changes
- architecture morphing
- face geometry changes
- bokeh popping
- flare popping
- ghosting flicker
- vignetting flicker
- focus hunting
- parallax discontinuity
- object-scale jumps.

Every change must have a physical cause.

==================================================
LENS CONTINUITY
==================================================

The same ultra-wide lens must preserve:

- focal length
- optical format
- distortion profile
- bokeh
- flare
- ghosting
- CA
- breathing
- vignetting
- image coverage.

If the lens changes:

recalculate the optical configuration.

Same focal length does not necessarily mean the same lens.

==================================================
SEQUENCE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Ultra-wide usage should be motivated by:

- environmental importance
- spatial geography
- movement
- architecture
- immersion
- scale
- subject/environment relationship.

Do not randomly alternate between ultra-wide and normal lenses.

A transition to ultra-wide should have a clear shot purpose.
