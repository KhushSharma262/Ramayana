# LENS CONTINUITY BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control optical and photographic continuity when a shot changes lens, when multiple shots belong to the same sequence, or when a lens characteristic must remain stable across time.

Lens continuity ensures that every shot behaves as though it was photographed by a coherent physical camera and lens system.

The system must preserve continuity of:

- lens type
- focal length
- field of view
- perspective
- optical character
- distortion
- depth-of-field behaviour
- bokeh
- flare
- ghosting
- chromatic aberration
- compression
- focus behaviour
- lens breathing
- image geometry
- framing relationships

Lens continuity must be derived from the actual camera and lens configuration.

It must never be simulated through arbitrary post-processing.

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PHOTOREALISM
==================================================

The final video must look like professionally captured live-action footage using a real camera system and real physical lenses.

It must NEVER look AI-generated.

Every lens characteristic must remain:

- physically plausible
- temporally consistent
- spatially consistent
- optically consistent
- internally coherent
- consistent with the selected lens profile

NEVER allow:

- random lens-character changes
- spontaneous distortion changes
- inconsistent focal length
- unstable perspective
- changing bokeh style
- random flare
- random ghosting
- fluctuating chromatic aberration
- inconsistent depth of field
- artificial sharpening
- artificial blur
- digital lens overlays
- inconsistent focus breathing
- geometry morphing
- frame-to-frame optical character changes
- lens effects appearing without physical cause

When uncertain, preserve the established optical behaviour.

PHYSICAL REALISM ALWAYS TAKES PRIORITY OVER CINEMATIC EFFECT.

==================================================
WHAT LENS CONTINUITY MEANS
==================================================

Lens continuity means that the optical appearance of a shot remains consistent with the lens configuration assigned to that shot and consistent with surrounding shots when continuity requires it.

A lens is not merely:

"24mm"

or:

"85mm."

It is a complete optical system with characteristics affecting:

- FOV
- perspective interaction
- distortion
- focus rendering
- bokeh
- flare
- ghosting
- chromatic aberration
- breathing
- contrast
- edge behaviour
- compression perception
- focus falloff

Therefore:

FOCAL LENGTH CONTINUITY
does NOT automatically guarantee
LENS CHARACTER CONTINUITY.

==================================================
LENS IDENTITY
==================================================

Each selected lens configuration should maintain a stable identity throughout the shot.

A lens identity may include:

- lens family
- optical type
- spherical / anamorphic
- focal length
- maximum aperture
- optical character
- distortion profile
- flare profile
- ghosting profile
- chromatic aberration profile
- bokeh profile
- breathing profile
- focus behaviour
- coverage
- sensor compatibility

Once selected:

do not silently replace its optical behaviour with another lens profile.

==================================================
LENS CHANGE
==================================================

A lens may change between shots when cinematographically justified.

Examples:

24mm
→ 50mm
→ 85mm

This is acceptable if the shot design requires it.

However, the system must explicitly recognize the lens change.

Do not allow a new focal length to appear while retaining the previous lens's optical behaviour.

==================================================
CUT-TO-CUT CONTINUITY
==================================================

When cutting between shots, evaluate:

- focal length
- camera position
- framing
- perspective
- subject distance
- background scale
- depth of field
- optical character

A focal-length change may be intentional.

It must not create an accidental discontinuity.

Example:

Shot A:
50mm medium close-up

Shot B:
85mm close-up

The system should recognize that the tighter framing may result from both:

- longer focal length
- different camera position

Do not automatically reproduce the same perspective as the previous shot.

==================================================
SAME LENS CONTINUITY
==================================================

If the same lens is retained between shots:

preserve its optical character.

Maintain:

- distortion behaviour
- bokeh character
- flare sensitivity
- ghosting character
- chromatic aberration
- contrast character
- edge rendering
- breathing
- focus behaviour

Do not randomly change these characteristics between shots.

==================================================
SAME FOCAL LENGTH, DIFFERENT LENS
==================================================

Two lenses with identical focal lengths may still render differently.

For example:

50mm Lens A
≠
50mm Lens B

They may differ in:

- distortion
- bokeh
- flare
- contrast
- focus breathing
- chromatic aberration
- edge sharpness
- rendering character

Therefore:

do not treat focal length as complete lens identity.

The selected lens profile has priority.

==================================================
FOCAL LENGTH CONTINUITY
==================================================

Maintain the intended focal length throughout a fixed-lens shot.

Do not allow:

- spontaneous focal-length changes
- accidental zooming
- FOV drift
- framing drift

unless the shot explicitly specifies a zoom.

If a zoom is specified:

focal length must change continuously and plausibly.

==================================================
ZOOM CONTINUITY
==================================================

A zoom is a continuous focal-length transition.

During a zoom:

- FOV changes continuously
- magnification changes continuously
- optical characteristics remain consistent with the lens
- perspective does NOT change merely because focal length changes

Do not simulate zoom through digital scaling.

==================================================
DOLLY VS ZOOM CONTINUITY
==================================================

Maintain the distinction:

DOLLY:
camera position changes.

ZOOM:
focal length changes.

A dolly changes perspective.

A zoom changes FOV and framing without physically moving the camera.

Do not allow one to silently replace the other.

==================================================
PERSPECTIVE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Perspective is primarily determined by camera position relative to the scene.

When maintaining visual continuity:

track:

- camera position
- subject distance
- background distance
- focal length
- framing

Do not attribute perspective entirely to focal length.

If the camera position changes:

the spatial relationship may change.

This is physically correct.

==================================================
FIELD OF VIEW CONTINUITY
==================================================

FOV must remain consistent with:

- focal length
- sensor dimensions
- aspect ratio
- lens type
- anamorphic squeeze where applicable

Master format:

16:9.

Do not allow unexplained FOV changes between frames or shots.

==================================================
ANAMORPHIC CONTINUITY
==================================================

If a sequence establishes anamorphic capture:

maintain consistent anamorphic behaviour unless the shot explicitly changes to another lens system.

Maintain:

- squeeze characteristics
- horizontal optical rendering
- bokeh geometry
- flare behaviour
- edge behaviour
- breathing
- distortion
- spatial rendering

Do not allow one shot to suddenly behave like a spherical lens without an explicit lens change.

==================================================
SPHERICAL CONTINUITY
==================================================

If spherical lenses are established:

maintain spherical optical behaviour.

Do not introduce:

- anamorphic oval bokeh
- horizontal streaks
- anamorphic distortion
- anamorphic squeeze

unless the shot explicitly changes lens system.

==================================================
CROP FACTOR CONTINUITY
==================================================

Sensor format must remain consistent unless the camera system changes.

Crop factor affects:

- FOV
- framing
- equivalent focal length

It does not directly change:

- physical focal length
- perspective at the same camera position

Do not allow unexplained sensor-format changes.

==================================================
LENS COVERAGE
==================================================

The selected lens must physically cover the selected sensor.

Do not introduce:

- artificial vignetting
- black corners
- edge falloff
- cropping
- image-circle artifacts

unless the lens/sensor combination physically warrants them.

==================================================
DISTORTION CONTINUITY
==================================================

Lens distortion must remain consistent with the selected lens.

Do not allow:

- barrel distortion to appear randomly
- pincushion distortion to disappear randomly
- moustache distortion to change between frames
- architecture to bend differently without lens or camera change

For architecture:

preserve stable geometry.

For faces:

avoid unstable facial proportions.

==================================================
DEPTH-OF-FIELD CONTINUITY
==================================================

DOF must remain physically consistent with:

- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor
- camera position
- subject distance
- background distance

Do not maintain identical background blur across shots if the physical camera configuration changes substantially.

Likewise:

do not change blur arbitrarily when the configuration remains the same.

==================================================
BOKEH CONTINUITY
==================================================

Bokeh must remain consistent with the selected lens.

Maintain:

- aperture shape
- optical rendering
- cat-eye behaviour where applicable
- anamorphic oval characteristics where applicable
- highlight rendering
- edge behaviour

Do not randomly switch between:

- circular
- polygonal
- oval
- swirling

bokeh patterns.

The lens determines the behaviour.

==================================================
FLARE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Flare must depend on:

- lens
- coatings
- light source
- source position
- camera orientation
- aperture
- filters

Do not add flare merely because another shot contains flare.

If the light source and camera relationship remain comparable:

flare behaviour should remain comparable.

If the source moves:

flare should change physically.

==================================================
GHOSTING CONTINUITY
==================================================

Ghosting must remain tied to the optical system and light source.

Do not allow:

- identical ghost patterns in every shot
- random ghost appearance
- ghost position locked to screen space
- ghosting without an appropriate source

If the lens remains unchanged but the source position changes:

ghosting should change accordingly.

==================================================
CHROMATIC ABERRATION CONTINUITY
==================================================

CA must remain consistent with the lens profile.

Modern corrected lens:

→ very subtle.

Vintage / character lens:

→ potentially more visible.

Do not randomly add RGB fringing to different shots.

Do not allow CA to change character independently of:

- lens
- aperture
- focus
- frame position
- contrast
- lighting

==================================================
LENS BREATHING CONTINUITY
==================================================

Breathing must remain tied to the selected lens.

If the same lens performs multiple focus pulls:

its breathing behaviour should remain consistent.

Do not make one focus pull heavily breathe and another show zero breathing without a physical reason.

==================================================
FOCUS CONTINUITY
==================================================

Focus transitions must remain consistent with:

- lens focus throw
- breathing
- depth of field
- focus distance
- subject movement
- camera movement

Do not allow focus behaviour to change arbitrarily between consecutive frames.

==================================================
EXPOSURE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Lens changes must not automatically cause exposure changes.

Exposure should be controlled independently through:

- aperture
- shutter
- ISO / gain
- lighting

If the lens changes to a different maximum aperture:

the cinematographic system may compensate exposure when appropriate.

Do not create unexplained brightness changes simply because the lens changed.

==================================================
APERTURE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Maintain aperture continuity unless the shot specifies an aperture change.

Aperture affects:

- exposure
- DOF
- bokeh
- optical aberrations
- flare behaviour
- highlight rendering

Therefore an unexplained aperture change can create multiple optical discontinuities.

==================================================
FILTER CONTINUITY
==================================================

Filters must remain consistent across shots when the same optical setup is maintained.

Track:

- diffusion filters
- ND filters
- polarizers
- specialty filters

Do not allow filter-specific optical behaviour to appear or disappear randomly.

==================================================
LIGHTING INTERACTION
==================================================

Lens continuity does not mean identical optical artefacts under different lighting.

The same lens may produce:

almost no flare
in one shot

and

visible flare
in another

because the light source changed.

Therefore:

PRESERVE LENS CHARACTER,
BUT ALLOW PHYSICAL CONDITIONS TO CHANGE THE RESULT.

==================================================
SEQUENCE CONTINUITY
==================================================

For shots belonging to the same continuous visual sequence:

maintain a coherent lens language.

Track:

- established lens family
- focal-length range
- optical character
- anamorphic/spherical state
- sensor format
- image geometry
- focus behaviour
- bokeh
- flare
- contrast character

A sequence should feel photographed by the same cinematographic system unless a deliberate visual transition occurs.

==================================================
SCENE-TO-SCENE CONTINUITY
==================================================

A new scene may intentionally introduce a new lens language.

For example:

INTIMATE SCENE:
moderate telephoto

GRAND ENVIRONMENT:
wide lens

ACTION:
wider dynamic lens

OBSERVATIONAL:
long lens

These changes are valid when story-motivated.

The transition should still be deliberate.

Do not randomly change lens characteristics between scenes.

==================================================
CHARACTER CONTINUITY
==================================================

Lens selection must not cause characters to change apparent identity.

Maintain stable:

- facial proportions
- body proportions
- eye spacing
- nose geometry
- jawline
- hair
- costume geometry

If a focal length changes:

allow only the physically expected perspective differences.

Do not allow lens changes to become character morphing.

==================================================
ARCHITECTURAL CONTINUITY
==================================================

Temples, palaces, forests, mountains, roads, structures and other environments must maintain stable geometry.

Lens changes may alter:

- FOV
- perspective
- apparent scale
- spatial compression

but must not cause:

- structure deformation
- random bending
- resizing of individual objects
- inconsistent horizon geometry

==================================================
SHOT-REVERSE-SHOT CONTINUITY
==================================================

For dialogue and conversational coverage:

maintain consistent lens relationships unless a deliberate visual strategy is specified.

Track:

- focal-length relationship
- camera distance
- subject size
- eye-line
- perspective
- background rendering
- depth of field

Do not allow one character to appear optically distorted while the reverse shot appears inexplicably normal.

==================================================
180-DEGREE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Lens continuity must support spatial orientation.

When coverage remains within a consistent screen direction:

preserve:

- eye-line
- camera-side relationship
- focal-length logic
- perspective
- subject placement

Lens changes must not accidentally create a spatially contradictory image.

==================================================
MATCH CUTS
==================================================

For visually matched cuts:

lens changes must not create accidental mismatch.

If matching:

- subject scale
- composition
- horizon
- architecture
- movement

evaluate lens and camera position together.

Do not digitally resize the image to hide an incorrect physical configuration.

==================================================
TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Within a shot, lens characteristics must remain stable unless a physical camera/lens parameter changes.

NEVER allow:

- optical character flicker
- changing distortion
- changing bokeh shape
- flare popping
- ghosting popping
- CA flickering
- breathing oscillation
- FOV drift
- focal-length drift
- perspective morphing
- inconsistent focus rendering
- changing lens softness

Every optical change must have a physical cause.

==================================================
LENS CHANGE TRANSITIONS
==================================================

When the script explicitly calls for a lens change:

treat it as a new optical configuration.

The new setup may have different:

- focal length
- FOV
- perspective
- DOF
- bokeh
- flare
- distortion
- breathing
- chromatic aberration

Do not blend two lens profiles continuously unless the camera is actually performing a physical optical transition such as a zoom.

A hard cut between lenses may produce an intentional visual discontinuity.

That discontinuity should be physically coherent.

==================================================
NO AUTOMATIC CINEMATIC CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Lens continuity does NOT mean every shot should look identical.

Preserve:

OPTICAL IDENTITY

not:

VISUAL REPETITION.

A lens can produce different results under different:

- lighting
- focus distance
- subject distance
- aperture
- composition
- camera position

This is physically correct.

==================================================
LENS SELECTION PRIORITY
==================================================

Lens continuity must never override shot requirements.

The correct order is:

STORY
→ SHOT PURPOSE
→ COMPOSITION
→ SUBJECT / ENVIRONMENT
→ REQUIRED FOV
→ CAMERA POSITION
→ LENS SELECTION
→ FOCAL LENGTH
→ APERTURE
→ FOCUS DISTANCE
→ OPTICAL CHARACTER
→ CONTINUITY CHECK

Do not retain an inappropriate lens merely because it was used in the previous shot.

Continuity supports cinematography.

It does not replace cinematographic intent.

==================================================
FINAL CONTINUITY CHECK
==================================================

Before generating each shot, compare the current optical configuration against the established sequence.

CHECK:

1. Is the lens family consistent?
2. Is the spherical/anamorphic state correct?
3. Is the focal length intentional?
4. Is the sensor format unchanged?
5. Is the FOV physically correct?
6. Is camera position physically plausible?
7. Is perspective consistent with camera position?
8. Is distortion consistent with the lens?
9. Is DOF physically consistent?
10. Is bokeh consistent with the lens?
11. Is flare justified by the light source?
12. Is ghosting justified by the optical geometry?
13. Is chromatic aberration consistent?
14. Is focus breathing consistent?
15. Is focus behaviour consistent?
16. Is aperture consistent with the shot?
17. Are filters consistent?
18. Is character geometry stable?
19. Is environmental geometry stable?
20. Is every optical change physically explainable?

If any optical characteristic changes without a physical or cinematographic reason:

CORRECT IT.

==================================================
FINAL DECISION HIERARCHY
==================================================

Determine lens continuity in this order:

1. STORY
2. SEQUENCE PURPOSE
3. SHOT PURPOSE
4. ESTABLISHED LENS LANGUAGE
5. CAMERA SYSTEM
6. SENSOR FORMAT
7. LENS TYPE
8. ANAMORPHIC / SPHERICAL STATE
9. FOCAL LENGTH
10. CAMERA POSITION
11. SUBJECT DISTANCE
12. APERTURE
13. FOCUS DISTANCE
14. DEPTH OF FIELD
15. BOKEH
16. DISTORTION
17. FLARE
18. GHOSTING
19. CHROMATIC ABERRATION
20. LENS BREATHING
21. FOCUS BEHAVIOUR
22. TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
23. CROSS-SHOT CONTINUITY
24. FINAL OPTICAL REALISM

Never begin with:

"Make the shots visually consistent."

Instead determine:

WHAT LENS SYSTEM IS ESTABLISHED?
→ WHAT PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS DOES IT HAVE?
→ WHAT HAS CHANGED BETWEEN SHOTS?
→ WHICH CHANGES ARE INTENTIONAL?
→ WHICH CHANGES SHOULD REMAIN CONSTANT?
→ ARE ALL OPTICAL DIFFERENCES PHYSICALLY EXPLAINABLE?

Lens continuity means maintaining a coherent physical camera system while allowing legitimate optical variation caused by:

- lens changes
- focus changes
- aperture changes
- camera movement
- subject movement
- lighting
- composition
- shot purpose

The final sequence must feel as though it was photographed by a real cinematographer using real physical lenses.

No arbitrary lens effects.
No optical character flicker.
No digital lens simulation.
No impossible perspective changes.
No AI-generated optical behaviour.

It must never look AI-generated.
