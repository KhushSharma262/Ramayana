# NORMAL LENS BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control the physical optical behaviour of normal focal-length lenses used to produce a natural, human-scale representation of space.

A normal lens should reproduce perspective and spatial relationships in a way that feels close to ordinary human visual experience when viewed from an appropriate camera position.

"Normal" does NOT mean:

- mathematically identical to human vision
- automatically 50 mm
- automatically distortion-free
- automatically cinematic
- automatically shallow depth of field
- automatically neutral-looking
- automatically the correct lens for every natural-looking shot

Normal-lens behaviour must be derived from:

- sensor format
- focal length
- camera position
- subject distance
- framing
- aperture
- focus distance
- subject/background distance
- lens design
- lens character
- shot purpose

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PHOTOREALISM
==================================================

The final video must look like professionally captured live-action footage through a real physical camera and lens.

The normal lens must produce believable optical behaviour.

NEVER create:

- artificial "cinematic" sharpness
- fake perspective
- digital depth simulation
- portrait-mode blur
- artificial background compression
- exaggerated edge distortion
- synthetic bokeh
- fake lens effects
- impossible focus
- artificial eye-level perspective
- digital focal-length changes
- unstable geometry
- AI-generated texture
- temporal optical inconsistencies

Avoid making the image look "too perfect."

Real lenses may have:

- subtle distortion
- subtle vignetting
- mild chromatic aberration
- finite resolving power
- natural contrast
- subtle flare
- focus breathing
- field curvature
- diffraction
- lens-dependent bokeh

These characteristics must appear only when physically justified.

When uncertain:

choose the physically plausible and less noticeable result.

==================================================
WHAT IS A NORMAL LENS?
==================================================

A normal lens is a focal-length configuration whose field of view and image rendering produce a relatively natural representation of spatial relationships for a given sensor format.

For a full-frame 35 mm reference system, a normal region is approximately:

40â€“60 mm

with approximately:

50 mm

often used as a conventional reference.

This is descriptive, not an absolute rule.

A normal lens on a different sensor format requires a different physical focal length to produce a similar field of view.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS VS HUMAN VISION
==================================================

Do not claim that a normal lens literally reproduces human vision.

Human visual perception involves:

- a wide visual field
- binocular vision
- eye movement
- peripheral awareness
- visual attention
- neural processing
- dynamic adaptation

A normal lens provides a photographic projection.

Its value is that, from an appropriate viewing distance and camera position, spatial relationships can appear naturally proportioned.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Perspective is primarily determined by:

CAMERA POSITION.

Not by focal length alone.

A normal lens does not inherently create a special "normal perspective."

Instead:

camera position
+
subject distance
+
background distance
+
focal length
=
final spatial appearance.

Do not use focal length as a substitute for camera placement.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS CAMERA POSITION
==================================================

For natural representation:

place the camera at a physically appropriate distance for the desired framing.

Do not force the camera unnaturally close merely because a normal lens is selected.

For example:

a medium shot may require the camera to be positioned several meters from the subject depending on:

- sensor format
- focal length
- subject size
- desired framing

The camera position must be derived from the shot.

NORMAL LENS FOV

For a full-frame 35 mm reference:

A 50 mm lens on full frame provides a moderate field of view that can produce a natural-looking representation.

The exact FOV depends on:

- sensor width
- sensor height
- aspect ratio
- focal length

Do not hard-code a universal FOV.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND DISTORTION
==================================================

Normal lenses generally have relatively restrained geometric distortion, but this is lens-design dependent.

Do not assume:

normal lens = zero distortion.

Possible behaviour:

- very low barrel distortion
- mild pincushion distortion
- complex/moustache distortion
- almost perfectly corrected geometry

Use the selected lens profile.

Distortion should normally be:

- subtle
- stable
- spatially coherent
- weakest near the image centre
- lens-dependent toward the edges

Never add obvious warping merely to make the shot look cinematic.

==================================================
FACES
==================================================

Normal lenses are particularly useful for natural facial representation.

For human faces:

maintain:

- natural proportions
- stable facial geometry
- realistic nose/ear relationships
- natural eye spacing
- stable jawline
- realistic head shape

Avoid placing a normal lens excessively close to a face.

Even without significant lens distortion, very close camera placement changes perspective.

The result must not make:

- nose unnaturally large
- ears unnaturally small
- forehead unnaturally expanded
- facial depth unnaturally compressed

unless the physical camera position genuinely produces that effect.

OPTICAL CAUSALITY / UNKNOWN-PARAMETER RULE
==================================================

Normal-lens behaviour must be derived from a physical camera/lens

UNKNOWN does not mean:

- default 50 mm
- default shallow DOF
- default natural perspective
- default bokeh
- default low distortion
- default spherical character
- default cinematic softness

If a parameter is unknown:

do not invent an exact value.

Use only explicitly established parameters and physically conservative
behaviour until the missing parameter is resolved.

Never infer:

FOCAL_LENGTH
from desired framing alone.

Never infer:

CAMERA_POSITION
from focal length alone.

Never infer:

PERSPECTIVE
from focal length alone.

Never infer:

OPTICAL_CHARACTER
from the word "normal" alone.

NORMAL LENS VALIDATION PRECEDENCE
==================================================

Normal-lens configuration must be resolved in this order:

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS CLASSIFICATION FIREWALL
==================================================

"Normal lens" is a spatial/cinematographic classification.

It does not authorize:

- a specific focal length
- a specific sensor format
- a specific perspective
- shallow DOF
- restrained bokeh
- low distortion
- spherical optics
- anamorphic optics

A normal lens must always be interpreted relative to the selected
sensor format and framing requirements.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / PERSPECTIVE FIREWALL
==================================================

Perspective is determined primarily by camera position relative to
the subject and environment.

Focal length controls:

- field of view
- framing
- magnification at a given camera position

Do not claim that focal length alone creates perspective compression
or expansion.

If camera position changes:

recalculate:

- perspective
- parallax
- subject scale
- background scale
- spatial relationships
- framing

Never preserve the previous perspective digitally.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / FRAMING FIREWALL
==================================================

Required framing must be achieved through a physically valid
combination of:

- sensor format
- focal length
- camera position
- subject distance

Do not achieve framing through:

- digital zoom
- synthetic cropping
- artificial perspective
- subject scaling
- image warping

If the desired framing is not physically achievable:

RECONFIGURE THE CAMERA/LENS SYSTEM.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / DOF FIREWALL
==================================================

Depth of field must be physically derived.

Required dependencies include:

- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject distance
- acceptable circle of confusion
- final image conditions

Do not assign shallow DOF merely because the shot is:

- cinematic
- emotional
- intimate
- a portrait
- a close-up

Do not use portrait-mode blur or depth-map simulation.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / OPTICAL CHARACTER AUTHORIZATION
==================================================

Optical effects may only appear when physically justified.

AUTHORIZATION STATES:

FORCED:
required by the selected physical lens configuration.

DERIVED:
produced naturally by lens parameters and scene conditions.

ALLOWED:
physically possible but not necessarily visible.

DISABLED:
physically unsupported or not justified.

This applies to:

- distortion
- chromatic aberration
- flare
- ghosting
- vignetting
- bokeh
- field curvature
- diffraction
- focus breathing

Never promote an ALLOWED characteristic into a visible effect merely
because it looks cinematic.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / SPHERICAL-ANAMORPHIC FIREWALL
==================================================

Normal focal length does not determine optical format.

First determine:

SPHERICAL OR ANAMORPHIC

Then derive the corresponding optical behaviour.

If SPHERICAL:

do not introduce:

- anamorphic squeeze
- oval bokeh
- horizontal streak flare
- anamorphic edge behaviour
- anamorphic distortion
- anamorphic breathing characteristics

If ANAMORPHIC:

apply only the selected anamorphic system's physically supported
characteristics.

Never mix incompatible optical signatures.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / FOCUS FIREWALL
==================================================

Focus must correspond to a physically achievable focal plane.

The system must not automatically focus on:

- centre of frame
- nearest object
- largest object
- highest-contrast object

unless that is the intended photographic focus.

Focus priority is story-driven but physically constrained.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / MOVEMENT FIREWALL
==================================================

Camera movement must produce its physical consequences.

DOLLY:

changes:

- camera position
- perspective
- parallax
- subject/background relationship
- subject scale

ZOOM:

changes:

- focal length
- field of view
- framing

but does not independently change perspective.

Never simulate either movement through digital scaling or digital
perspective warping.

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS / MOTION-BLUR FIREWALL
==================================================

Motion blur must emerge from:

- shutter behaviour
- exposure time
- subject velocity
- camera velocity
- camera movement

Do not apply uniform synthetic motion blur.

Different moving objects may exhibit different blur according to
their relative motion.

==================================================
TEMPORAL NORMAL-LENS VALIDATION
==================================================

Within a continuous shot, the following must remain stable unless
their physical cause changes:

- lens identity
- focal length
- sensor format
- optical format
- distortion profile
- bokeh behaviour
- optical character
- focus behaviour
- perspective logic

Natural changes may occur through:

- camera movement
- lens movement
- focus movement
- subject movement
- lighting changes
- aperture changes
- focal-length changes on a zoom lens

Every visible optical change must have a physical cause.

Never allow:

- focal-length flicker
- perspective jumping
- bokeh popping
- distortion flicker
- random flare
- random CA
- unstable focus
- changing facial proportions
- texture crawling
- optical character switching

==================================================
NORMAL-LENS CONTINUITY LOCK
==================================================

Within a continuous shot:

LENS_IDENTITY = CONSTANT

unless an actual physical lens change occurs.

Across shots:

normal-lens usage may change only when motivated by:

- story
- spatial relationship
- emotional intent
- composition
- sequence progression

Do not change between normal, wide, telephoto, spherical, or
anamorphic configurations without an intentional shot transition.

==================================================
VALIDATION FAILURE HANDLING
==================================================

If a normal-lens configuration fails validation:

1. identify the failed physical constraint
2. identify the earliest incorrect parameter
3. correct that parameter
4. recalculate all dependent optical parameters
5. recalculate framing
6. recalculate focus
7. recalculate DOF
8. recalculate optical character
9. validate temporal continuity
10. validate final physical realism

Never repair the result with a post-hoc visual effect.

==================================================
MINIMUM OPTICAL INTERVENTION
==================================================

When multiple physically valid normal-lens configurations can produce
the required shot:

prefer the configuration that introduces the fewest unnecessary
optical changes.

Do not:

- widen aperture unnecessarily
- move the camera unnecessarily
- exaggerate bokeh
- introduce visible aberrations
- introduce anamorphic behaviour
- add flare
- add distortion

simply to make the image appear more cinematic.

==================================================
FINAL NORMAL-LENS REALISM RULE
==================================================

Every normal-lens decision must be explainable as a real cinematographer's
physical camera and lens decision.

The system must establish:

- why a normal lens was selected
- what sensor format is being used
- what focal length is being used
- where the camera physically is
- what perspective results
- whether framing is achievable
- whether focus is physically valid
- whether DOF is physically achievable
- whether optical character is justified
- whether movement remains physically coherent
- whether the lens remains temporally consistent

If any requirement cannot be satisfied:

RECONFIGURE THE CAMERA/LENS SYSTEM.

Never fake the result digitally.

PORTRAITS

A normal lens can produce natural environmental portraits.

For tighter facial portraits:

consider whether a slightly longer focal length would provide a more appropriate camera distance.

Do not force a 50 mm lens into every portrait.

Lens selection remains subordinate to:

- shot purpose
- framing
- subject relationship
- camera position
- emotional intent

==================================================
HUMAN-SCALE REPRESENTATION
==================================================

Normal lenses are particularly useful when the audience should feel physically present within the environment.

Suitable applications include:

- dialogue
- walking
- interiors
- natural observation
- environmental portraits
- medium shots
- two-shots
- groups
- everyday interaction
- grounded dramatic scenes

The lens should preserve believable spatial relationships.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND ENVIRONMENT
==================================================

A normal lens should retain enough environmental information to establish spatial context without dramatically expanding or compressing the scene.

Use it when:

- environment matters
- subject/environment relationship matters
- natural perspective is desired
- extreme spatial exaggeration is undesirable

Do not blur the environment simply because the subject is important.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND ARCHITECTURE
==================================================

Normal lenses are useful for architecture when:

- natural spatial representation is desired
- extreme width is unnecessary
- geometric stability is important

Keep:

- verticals stable
- horizontal lines coherent
- perspective physically correct

Remember:

camera tilt causes perspective convergence.

Do not blame the lens for perspective changes caused by camera orientation.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND LANDSCAPES
==================================================

A normal lens can provide a grounded representation of landscapes.

Unlike a wide lens:

it does not automatically exaggerate foreground depth.

Unlike a telephoto:

it does not strongly compress distant layers.

Maintain:

- atmospheric perspective
- natural depth cues
- realistic scale
- coherent foreground/midground/background relationships

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND COMPRESSION
==================================================

Do not describe normal lenses as inherently compressing space.

Perceived compression depends primarily on:

- camera position
- subject distance
- background distance
- focal length used for framing

A normal lens positioned farther away can still produce spatial compression effects through camera placement.

Do not apply artificial "normal-lens compression."

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND DEPTH OF FIELD
==================================================

A normal lens does not automatically produce shallow DOF.

DOF depends on:

- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject distance
- background distance
- final image conditions

Use shallow DOF only when the physical configuration produces it.

Normal lenses may produce:

- moderate DOF
- shallow DOF
- deep DOF

depending on the shot.

==================================================
APERTURE
==================================================

Aperture must be selected according to:

- required DOF
- lighting
- subject priority
- motion
- exposure
- optical character
- diffraction

Do not default to the widest aperture.

Wide aperture may produce:

- shallower DOF
- stronger subject separation
- more visible bokeh
- more optical aberration
- reduced tolerance for focus error

Stopped-down aperture may produce:

- greater DOF
- increased environmental readability
- reduced aberrations
- potentially greater diffraction at very small apertures

==================================================
FOCUS DISTANCE
==================================================

Focus distance must correspond to the actual intended focal plane.

For a character:

normally prioritize the eyes when appropriate.

For dialogue:

focus may remain on the speaking subject or transition between subjects.

For environmental shots:

focus may be deeper.

For objects:

focus the story-critical detail.

Never automatically focus on:

- the closest object
- the centre of frame
- the largest object

Focus is story-driven but physically constrained.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND BOKEH
==================================================

Bokeh must arise naturally from:

- aperture
- focus distance
- subject/background distance
- lens design
- aperture shape

Normal lenses generally produce relatively restrained bokeh compared with some longer lenses under equivalent framing.

Do not create large glowing circles simply because the shot is cinematic.

If point sources are out of focus:

their appearance must follow the selected lens profile.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND FOREGROUND
==================================================

Foreground elements may become naturally defocused.

Examples:

- doorway edges
- foliage
- curtains
- pillars
- objects
- people

Foreground blur must correspond to physical distance from the focal plane.

Do not use foreground blur as an artificial framing effect.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND BACKGROUND
==================================================

The background should retain enough information to communicate spatial context unless the selected aperture and distances naturally produce significant defocus.

Do not destroy environmental geography with excessive blur.

==================================================
MINIMUM FOCUS DISTANCE
==================================================

Respect the selected lens's actual minimum focus distance.

If:

SUBJECT_DISTANCE < MFD

the lens cannot achieve valid focus.

Do not compensate with:

- digital zoom
- digital sharpening
- AI focus
- synthetic blur
- image scaling

Instead:

- move camera
- change lens
- use close-focus configuration
- change framing

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION
==================================================

Normal-lens rack focus should be physically optical.

During focus transition:

- previous subject progressively softens
- target subject progressively sharpens
- intermediate depth planes transition naturally
- bokeh changes continuously
- focus breathing follows lens characteristics

No digital depth-map transition.

No focus snapping.

No random focus hunting.

==================================================
FOCUS BREATHING
==================================================

Normal lenses may exhibit:

- minimal breathing
- moderate breathing
- noticeable breathing

depending on design.

Use the selected lens profile.

If focus changes:

any apparent framing change must be:

- continuous
- lens-dependent
- subtle unless the lens is known for stronger breathing

Never simulate breathing through digital zoom.

==================================================
CHROMATIC ABERRATION
==================================================

Normal lenses should generally have restrained CA unless a character lens is intentionally selected.

Potentially visible around:

- branches against bright sky
- high-contrast edges
- reflective metal
- bright practicals
- frame edges

Keep it:

- subtle
- lens-dependent
- physically localized

Never create obvious red/blue outlines.

==================================================
FLARE
==================================================

Normal lenses may produce flare when strong light enters the optical system.

Possible sources:

- sun
- bright sky
- fire
- lamps
- diyas
- candles
- reflections

Flare must depend on:

- source intensity
- source position
- lens coatings
- lens design
- aperture
- camera orientation

Do not automatically add cinematic flare.

==================================================
GHOSTING
==================================================

Ghosting may occur from internal reflections.

It must be:

- subtle
- lens-dependent
- source-dependent
- spatially coherent

Do not add floating ghost circles or colored overlays.

==================================================
VIGNETTING
==================================================

Normal lenses may exhibit subtle natural light falloff toward the edges.

Vignetting depends on:

- lens design
- aperture
- sensor coverage
- optical construction

Do not add a cinematic vignette in post.

If the selected lens is well corrected:

vignetting may be minimal.

==================================================
DIFFRACTION
==================================================

At sufficiently small apertures:

diffraction can reduce fine detail.

Do not compensate with artificial sharpening.

The image should maintain natural optical resolution.

==================================================
FIELD CURVATURE
==================================================

A normal lens may not focus a perfectly flat field uniformly depending on optical design.

For ordinary three-dimensional scenes:

this should generally remain subtle.

For flat subjects such as:

- manuscripts
- paintings
- inscriptions
- maps
- technical surfaces

use an appropriately corrected lens when uniform focus is important.

Do not digitally flatten field curvature.

==================================================
SPHERICAL VS ANAMORPHIC
==================================================

Normal focal length does not determine whether a lens is spherical or anamorphic.

Most normal-lens configurations will generally be spherical unless the selected lens system specifies otherwise.

If anamorphic:

apply the selected anamorphic optical profile.

If spherical:

do not introduce anamorphic:

- oval bokeh
- horizontal streaks
- squeeze behaviour
- characteristic edge rendering

Anamorphic selection remains shot-dependent.

==================================================
NORMAL ANAMORPHIC LENS
==================================================

If a normal focal-length anamorphic lens is selected:

- physical squeeze
- appropriate FOV
- lens-specific bokeh
- lens-specific flare
- lens-specific distortion
- lens-specific breathing
- natural optical character

Do not apply exaggerated anamorphic effects simply because the lens is anamorphic.

- dolly
- tracking
- handheld
- crane

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND DOLLY

- changing perspective
- changing parallax

Do not simulate dolly movement through digital scaling.

==================================================
NORMAL LENS AND ZOOM
==================================================

If using a normal-range zoom:

focal length may change continuously.

During a zoom:

- FOV changes
- framing changes
- perspective does not change merely because the lens zooms
- optical characteristics may vary with focal length

Do not digitally zoom a fixed normal lens.

==================================================

==================================================

A dolly zoom must only occur when explicitly motivated.

It requires:

simultaneous camera movement
+
focal-length change.

The resulting background size relationship must emerge physically.

Do not create a digital perspective warp.

- natural small camera movement

- physically plausible motion blur
