# FOCUS TRANSITION BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control how focus changes during a shot when the camera, subject, or cinematographer changes the active focal plane.

Focus transition must behave like a real physical lens being refocused.

It is NOT a digital blur transition.

The system must determine:

- where focus begins
- where focus ends
- why focus changes
- when the transition begins
- how quickly it occurs
- which subject gains priority
- how the previous focal plane loses sharpness
- how the new focal plane gains sharpness
- how the selected lens physically responds
- how focus breathing affects framing
- how camera and subject movement interact with focus

Focus transition must always serve the shot and story.

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PHOTOREALISM
==================================================

The final video must look like professionally captured live-action footage from a real camera and real physical lens.

It must NEVER look AI-generated.

Focus transitions must never appear digitally generated, composited, masked, or artificially blurred.

NEVER use:

- digital blur wipes
- AI depth-map transitions
- portrait-mode background blur
- artificial subject cutouts
- selective sharpening
- blur masks
- instantaneous focus switching
- arbitrary sharpness changes
- face-specific focus manipulation
- background-only blur transitions
- impossible simultaneous focus planes
- focus flicker
- focus pumping
- random focus hunting
- unstable facial detail
- shimmering hair
- changing object geometry
- temporal depth-map artifacts

The entire optical image must respond coherently to a change in the physical focal plane.

If uncertain, choose the physically plausible and less noticeable result.

REAL OPTICAL BEHAVIOUR ALWAYS TAKES PRIORITY OVER VISUAL EFFECT.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION DEFINITION
==================================================

A focus transition is a controlled movement of the lens's focal plane from one distance to another.

Example:

FOCUS A
→ intermediate focal distances
→ FOCUS B

It is NOT:

FOCUS A
→ instant digital sharpness switch
→ FOCUS B

During a real focus pull:

- the original subject gradually leaves critical focus
- intermediate depth planes pass through focus
- the new subject gradually becomes sharp
- defocus changes continuously
- bokeh changes continuously
- focus breathing may occur according to the lens
- the entire image remains optically coherent

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION CAUSE
==================================================

Every focus transition must have a physical or narrative reason.

Valid causes include:

- deliberate rack focus
- subject movement
- camera movement
- cinematographer focus adjustment
- focus tracking
- change in narrative attention
- reveal of a previously secondary subject
- foreground-to-background transition
- background-to-foreground transition

Do not introduce focus changes without cause.

==================================================
STORY FIRST
==================================================

Determine the narrative purpose before determining transition behaviour.

Possible purposes:

### ATTENTION SHIFT

Move the audience's attention from one subject to another.

### REVELATION

Reveal an important subject or object that was previously soft.

### EMOTIONAL EMPHASIS

Move focus toward the character or detail carrying emotional importance.

### SPATIAL RECONTEXTUALIZATION

Shift attention between foreground and background to clarify spatial relationships.

### SUBJECT HANDOFF

Transfer visual priority from one moving character to another.

### ENVIRONMENTAL REVEAL

Transition from a character to an important environmental element.

Never use a focus pull simply because it "looks cinematic."

==================================================
STARTING FOCUS
==================================================

Determine the initial focal plane from the shot's opening composition.

The initial subject should be naturally sharp according to:

- focus distance
- focal length
- aperture
- sensor format
- subject distance
- lens characteristics

Do not artificially preserve sharpness on the starting subject after the focal plane has moved away from it.

==================================================
ENDING FOCUS
==================================================

Determine the final focal plane from the intended narrative target.

The final subject should become progressively sharper as the focal plane approaches it.

Do not artificially sharpen the target before the physical focus transition reaches it.

==================================================
FOCUS PATH
==================================================

Focus should transition continuously between the starting and ending focus distances.

For:

A = starting focus distance
B = ending focus distance

the lens should physically move through the intermediate focus range.

Do not create an impossible discontinuity between A and B.

Objects located between A and B should naturally pass through intermediate states of sharpness as the focal plane moves.

==================================================
LINEARITY
==================================================

Do NOT assume every focus pull should be mechanically linear.

Transition speed may vary according to:

- narrative intention
- focus-pull distance
- subject movement
- cinematographer technique
- lens characteristics
- shot duration
- emotional intensity

Possible behaviours:

### LINEAR

Steady focus movement from start to finish.

Useful for:

- calm transitions
- controlled observation
- technical clarity

### EASE-IN

Slow initial movement followed by faster transition.

Useful for:

- subtle attention shift
- restrained cinematic transitions

### EASE-OUT

Faster initial movement followed by gradual settling.

Useful for:

- dramatic reveal
- urgent attention shift

### SMOOTH EASE-IN / EASE-OUT

Gradual acceleration followed by gradual deceleration.

Useful as the default for natural controlled focus pulls.

### VARIABLE

Focus speed changes during the pull because of deliberate cinematographic intent.

Use only when motivated.

The transition must never feel like a digital animation curve.

==================================================
FOCUS PULL SPEED
==================================================

Focus speed should correspond to the dramatic requirement.

### SLOW

Use for:

- realization
- contemplation
- emotional intimacy
- subtle revelation
- quiet observation

### MODERATE

Use for:

- normal attention shifts
- dialogue
- environmental discovery
- character interaction

### FAST

Use for:

- sudden danger
- shock
- immediate realization
- rapid subject handoff
- action

Even a fast focus pull must contain a physically plausible transition.

Do not use instantaneous switching unless the cinematographic system explicitly calls for a cut rather than a focus transition.

==================================================
RACK FOCUS
==================================================

A rack focus is an intentional focus transition between subjects or depth planes.

Example:

Foreground character
→ background character

or:

Background character
→ foreground object

During the rack:

- starting subject gradually softens
- intermediate planes transition naturally
- target subject progressively sharpens
- bokeh evolves continuously
- focus breathing follows lens behaviour
- framing remains optically consistent

Do not blur the starting subject independently from the rest of the scene.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION BETWEEN PEOPLE
==================================================

When transferring focus between characters:

prioritize the relevant facial/eye plane.

Example:

Character A in foreground
→ Character B in background

The transition should cause:

Character A:
sharp → progressively softer

Character B:
soft → progressively sharper

Intermediate objects:
change according to their physical distance from the moving focal plane

Do not allow Character A and Character B to remain artificially sharp throughout a shallow-depth-of-field transition.

==================================================
EYE-TO-EYE TRANSITIONS
==================================================

For close character shots, an eye-plane transition must remain physically plausible.

If the camera is positioned at an angle:

- nearer eye may behave differently from farther eye
- facial planes may enter/leave focus naturally
- eyelashes, hair, ears and skin detail may soften according to depth

Do not create a digital "eye sharpening" effect.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION AND DEPTH OF FIELD
==================================================

Focus transition must always be interpreted together with depth of field.

The transition is not merely:

sharp → blur → sharp.

Instead:

focal plane movement
+
existing depth of field
+
subject distances
+
lens optical character

determine the changing image.

A subject close to the focal plane may remain acceptably sharp for part of the transition.

A subject far outside the focal plane may become strongly defocused.

==================================================
BOKEH DURING FOCUS TRANSITION
==================================================

Bokeh must evolve naturally as focus changes.

As a point source moves farther from the focal plane:

- its blur increases
- its shape follows the lens profile
- its apparent size changes naturally

As it approaches focus:

- the blur decreases
- the point becomes progressively sharper

For anamorphic lenses:

- oval character may remain consistent with the lens

For spherical lenses:

- bokeh remains consistent with the selected aperture/lens design

Do not make bokeh appear or disappear suddenly.

Do not create identical glowing circles throughout the transition.

==================================================
FOCUS BREATHING
==================================================

Focus breathing may alter apparent framing during a focus transition.

The amount depends on the selected lens.

### LOW-BREATHING LENS

Keep framing highly stable.

### MODERATE-BREATHING LENS

Allow subtle change in apparent magnification/FOV.

### STRONG-BREATHING CHARACTER LENS

Allow more noticeable but still physically plausible framing change.

Never introduce focus breathing simply to make a focus pull look cinematic.

Focus breathing must be a consequence of the selected lens.

==================================================
ANAMORPHIC FOCUS TRANSITIONS
==================================================

If an anamorphic lens is selected:

maintain its specific optical behaviour throughout the focus transition.

Possible characteristics include:

- focus breathing
- changing edge rendering
- anamorphic bokeh behaviour
- subtle spatial changes
- lens-specific close-focus characteristics

Do not suddenly introduce anamorphic artifacts during the focus pull.

Do not increase flare or distortion merely because focus is changing.

==================================================
SPHERICAL FOCUS TRANSITIONS
==================================================

For spherical lenses:

maintain spherical lens characteristics.

Do not introduce:

- anamorphic oval bokeh
- horizontal streaks
- artificial stretching
- anamorphic breathing

unless those behaviours are explicitly justified by the selected lens profile.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION WITH CAMERA MOVEMENT
==================================================

Focus behaviour must account for camera movement.

During:

- dolly
- tracking
- crane
- orbit
- handheld
- push-in
- pull-out

the focus distance may change continuously.

If the subject remains stationary while the camera moves:

camera-to-subject distance changes physically.

If the subject moves:

subject-to-camera distance changes physically.

If both move:

calculate the resulting relative distance.

Do not preserve a fixed focus distance when physical geometry clearly changes.

==================================================
FOCUS TRACKING
==================================================

When tracking a moving subject:

the focal plane should follow the intended subject.

However, tracking must remain physically believable.

Avoid:

- perfect computational sharpness
- instant corrections
- focus snapping
- random hunting
- oscillation around the target

Small natural focus variation may occur when appropriate.

The subject should remain acceptably sharp according to the selected depth of field.

==================================================
FOCUS PULL WITH MOVING SUBJECTS
==================================================

If both the camera and subjects move:

focus transition must account for changing relative distances.

Example:

Character A moves toward camera
while
focus transitions toward Character B.

The system must not treat either focus distance as static.

Focus behaviour should be derived from the evolving scene geometry.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION AND FOREGROUND OBJECTS
==================================================

Foreground objects may naturally pass through focus during a rack.

Example:

foreground foliage
→ character
→ distant temple

Each depth layer should behave according to its actual distance.

Do not force foreground elements to remain blurred simply because they are in front.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION AND BACKGROUND
==================================================

Background elements should progressively sharpen or soften according to their relationship to the moving focal plane.

When moving focus:

foreground
→ subject
→ background

the background must not remain uniformly blurred.

Different background objects at different distances may have different levels of sharpness.

==================================================
MULTIPLE IMPORTANT SUBJECTS
==================================================

If multiple subjects must remain readable:

do not automatically perform a focus pull.

Instead evaluate whether the shot should use:

- deeper depth of field
- smaller aperture
- different camera position
- different focal length
- increased subject alignment
- deliberate focus transition

Do not sacrifice physical realism simply to create selective focus.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION DURATION
==================================================

Transition duration should depend on:

- distance between focal planes
- desired emotional pacing
- shot duration
- lens/focus mechanism
- subject movement
- narrative urgency

A larger focus-distance change does not automatically require a slower transition.

The cinematographic intention determines the speed.

==================================================
NO AUTOFOCUS HUNTING
==================================================

Default cinematic behaviour:

NO UNCONTROLLED AUTOFOCUS HUNTING.

Never allow the focal plane to oscillate between:

- face and background
- foreground and subject
- one eye and another
- subject and practical light
- nearby object and distant object

unless explicitly motivated by the story or shooting style.

==================================================
TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Focus transition must remain stable frame-to-frame.

Never allow:

- sharpness flicker
- sudden focus jumps
- background popping into focus
- faces changing sharpness randomly
- hair flickering between sharp and blurred
- jewelry changing focus unpredictably
- bokeh popping
- focus breathing oscillation
- depth-map shimmer
- artificial blur boundaries

Every frame must represent a physically plausible intermediate focus state.

==================================================
LIGHT SOURCES DURING FOCUS TRANSITION
==================================================

Practical lights, diyas, torches, candles, fire, stars and distant lamps may change appearance as they move relative to the focal plane.

Their defocus must change progressively.

Do not automatically turn every light into a large glowing orb.

Do not create artificial halos solely because a focus pull occurs.

Lens flare remains a separate optical phenomenon.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION VS EXPOSURE
==================================================

Do not change exposure merely because focus changes.

Focus transition does not inherently require:

- brightness changes
- contrast changes
- saturation changes
- white-balance changes

Any such change must have a separate physical or cinematographic cause.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION VS ZOOM
==================================================

Do not confuse a focus pull with a zoom.

Focus pull:
changes focal plane.

Zoom:
changes focal length and FOV.

A focus pull should not automatically change composition significantly unless the selected lens exhibits focus breathing.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION VS DOLLY
==================================================

Do not confuse a focus pull with camera movement.

Dolly:
changes camera position and therefore perspective.

Focus pull:
changes the focal plane.

They may occur simultaneously, but they remain separate physical processes.

If both occur:

model both independently and maintain consistency between them.

==================================================
DOLLY + FOCUS
==================================================

When the camera physically approaches a subject:

focus may need to continuously adjust to maintain subject sharpness.

When the camera pulls away:

focus distance may increase accordingly.

Do not maintain focus using artificial sharpening.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION AND CUTS
==================================================

A cut is not a focus transition.

If the script specifies a shot cut:

do not simulate the transition through focus.

If the shot remains continuous:

focus transitions must be physically continuous.

==================================================
REAL LENS RESPONSE
==================================================

The system should behave as though a real cinematographer is operating a real focus mechanism.

Consider:

- focus throw
- focus distance
- lens gearing
- lens response
- focus breathing
- depth of field
- subject movement
- camera movement

Do not expose these as artificial visual effects.

They should manifest naturally in the image.

==================================================
DEFAULT TRANSITION PROFILE
==================================================

Unless the shot specifies otherwise:

FOCUS_TRANSITION:
controlled optical rack

START:
story-defined focal plane

END:
story-defined focal plane

SPEED:
moderate

CURVE:
smooth ease-in / ease-out

FOCUS_HUNTING:
none

DIGITAL_BLUR:
none

DIGITAL_SHARPENING:
none

FOCUS_BREATHING:
lens-dependent

BOKEH_CHANGE:
physically derived

TEMPORAL_STABILITY:
extremely high

OPTICAL_REALISM:
extremely high

AI_ARTIFACT_TOLERANCE:
none

==================================================
DECISION HIERARCHY
==================================================

Determine focus transition in this order:

1. STORY PURPOSE
2. SHOT PURPOSE
3. INITIAL SUBJECT / FOCAL PLANE
4. FINAL SUBJECT / FOCAL PLANE
5. SUBJECT MOVEMENT
6. CAMERA MOVEMENT
7. SENSOR FORMAT
8. LENS TYPE
9. FOCAL LENGTH
10. APERTURE
11. STARTING FOCUS DISTANCE
12. ENDING FOCUS DISTANCE
13. DEPTH OF FIELD
14. FOCUS TRANSITION SPEED
15. TRANSITION CURVE
16. FOCUS BREATHING
17. BOKEH RESPONSE
18. TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
19. FINAL OPTICAL REALISM

Never begin with:

"Add a cinematic focus pull."

Instead determine:

WHY DOES ATTENTION MOVE?
→ WHAT IS CURRENTLY SHARP?
→ WHAT SHOULD BECOME SHARP?
→ WHERE ARE BOTH SUBJECTS PHYSICALLY LOCATED?
→ HOW DOES THE FOCAL PLANE MOVE BETWEEN THEM?
→ HOW DOES THE SELECTED LENS RESPOND?
→ WHAT NATURAL OPTICAL RESULT OCCURS?

The final result must look like a real cinematographer physically changed focus during a continuous live-action shot.

No digital focus effect.
No artificial blur.
No AI depth-map behaviour.
No focus flicker.
No impossible sharpness.

The focus transition must be physically plausible, narratively motivated, temporally stable, and invisible as an artificial effect.

It must never look AI-generated.
