# GHOSTING BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control optical ghosting produced when strong light enters a real camera lens and reflects between optical surfaces, coatings, filters, and lens elements.

Ghosting must be treated as a physical optical consequence of the selected lens and lighting conditions.

It is NOT a generic cinematic effect.

It must never be added simply because a shot is intended to look cinematic.

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PHOTOREALISM
==================================================

The final video must look like professionally captured live-action footage through a real physical lens.

It must NEVER look AI-generated.

Ghosting must remain:

- physically motivated
- lens-dependent
- source-dependent
- spatially coherent
- temporally stable
- optically plausible
- restrained

NEVER create:

- random floating circles
- arbitrary translucent shapes
- decorative light artifacts
- identical ghost patterns in every shot
- perfectly symmetrical digital ghosts
- rainbow overlays
- artificial lens-effect stickers
- ghosts disconnected from light sources
- ghosts that remain fixed while the light source moves
- ghosts that appear without a sufficiently strong source
- excessive ghosting around ordinary objects
- digital compositing artifacts
- animated optical effects unrelated to camera geometry

When uncertain, reduce ghosting rather than exaggerating it.

PHYSICAL OPTICAL REALISM ALWAYS TAKES PRIORITY OVER VISUAL EFFECT.

==================================================
WHAT GHOSTING IS
==================================================

Ghosting occurs when strong light undergoes internal reflections between optical surfaces.

A bright source may therefore produce faint secondary images or shapes within the image.

These may appear as:

- faint disks
- polygons
- arcs
- reflections
- low-contrast copies
- rings
- soft patches
- faint streak-like structures

Their exact appearance depends on the optical system.

Ghosting is different from:

- veiling flare
- halation
- bloom
- bokeh
- chromatic aberration
- sensor glare
- anamorphic streak flare

Do not merge these phenomena into one generic "lens effect."

==================================================
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
==================================================

Ghosting requires a sufficiently bright or high-contrast source entering the lens.

Potential sources include:

- sun
- bright sky near the sun
- extremely bright practical lights
- headlights
- strong reflections
- torches
- fire
- explosions
- bright lamps
- concentrated artificial light sources
- intense specular reflections

Ordinary low-intensity objects should not automatically generate ghosts.

A candle or diya may produce ghosting only when its brightness, position, exposure, lens characteristics, and surrounding darkness make it optically plausible.

==================================================
LIGHT SOURCE POSITION
==================================================

Ghosting is strongly dependent on the position of the light source relative to the optical axis.

As the source moves:

the ghost structures should move accordingly.

Ghosts must remain geometrically related to the originating light source.

They should NOT behave as independent objects in the scene.

If the bright source exits the optical conditions required to produce ghosting:

the ghosting should naturally weaken or disappear.

==================================================
OPTICAL AXIS RELATIONSHIP
==================================================

Ghosting generally becomes more noticeable when a bright source lies within or near the camera's optical field.

The system must consider:

- source position
- source intensity
- angle relative to optical axis
- lens construction
- number and arrangement of optical elements
- coatings
- filters
- aperture
- focal length
- exposure
- camera orientation

Do not assume that every bright source creates visible ghost images.

==================================================
LENS DEPENDENCE
==================================================

Ghosting must be determined by the selected lens profile.

### MODERN HIGH-CONTRAST LENS

Generally:

- controlled ghosting
- weak secondary reflections
- high resistance to veiling flare
- restrained optical artifacts

### VINTAGE / CHARACTER LENS

May produce:

- stronger ghost images
- more visible internal reflections
- lower contrast
- more distinctive shapes

However:

vintage does NOT mean extreme ghosting.

Do not automatically increase ghosting simply because a lens is older.

==================================================
COATINGS
==================================================

Lens coatings strongly influence internal reflections.

Modern multi-coated lenses generally suppress ghosting.

Older or less effectively coated lenses may exhibit more visible internal reflections.

Respect the selected lens profile.

Do not apply identical ghosting behaviour to every lens.

==================================================
FILTERS
==================================================

If a filter is physically present in the optical path, it may alter internal reflections.

Consider:

- filter glass
- filter coatings
- number of optical surfaces
- filter quality
- filter type

Do not invent filter-related ghosting if no filter is present.

==================================================
APERTURE
==================================================

Aperture can influence the appearance and geometry of optical artifacts.

The aperture shape may influence visible ghost structures and flare characteristics.

Maintain consistency with the selected lens and aperture.

Do not force every ghost into a circular shape.

Do not create artificial aperture-shaped overlays.

==================================================
GHOST SHAPE
==================================================

Ghosts may inherit characteristics related to:

- aperture geometry
- optical element shape
- lens construction
- coatings
- internal reflections
- source shape

Possible appearances include:

- faint circular forms
- polygonal forms
- rings
- soft reflected shapes
- subtle colored forms
- irregular low-contrast reflections

The shape must be lens-dependent.

Never use a universal ghost shape.

==================================================
GHOST BRIGHTNESS
==================================================

Ghosts are normally much weaker than the originating light source.

Maintain a strong brightness relationship:

PRIMARY SOURCE
>
GHOST IMAGE

Do not allow the ghost to become brighter than or equally dominant as the source unless an unusual physical optical configuration explicitly justifies it.

Ghosting should generally remain subtle enough that the audience perceives it as an optical consequence rather than a visual effect.

==================================================
GHOST COLOUR
==================================================

Ghosts may exhibit subtle colour differences because different wavelengths can reflect differently through coated optical surfaces.

However:

do not automatically produce rainbow ghosts.

Avoid:

- exaggerated RGB rings
- neon colours
- saturated blue/red/green circles
- chromatic overlays

Colour should remain weak and lens-dependent.

==================================================
GHOST LOCATION
==================================================

Ghost images should appear in positions determined by the optical geometry of the source and lens.

They should not randomly appear:

- around the subject
- in empty corners
- behind characters
- directly over faces
- at fixed screen coordinates

Their movement should be tied to the source and camera orientation.

==================================================
CAMERA MOVEMENT
==================================================

During:

- pan
- tilt
- dolly
- tracking
- orbit
- crane
- handheld movement

ghosting must respond naturally to changes in the light source's relationship to the optical axis.

As the camera moves:

the ghost pattern may:

- shift
- change intensity
- change position
- become weaker
- become stronger
- disappear

This must occur smoothly.

Never allow ghost elements to remain unnaturally locked to the screen while the source moves.

==================================================
LIGHT SOURCE MOVEMENT
==================================================

If the light source itself moves:

ghosting must respond to its changing optical relationship with the lens.

Examples:

A moving torch:
ghost position changes with the torch.

A moving character carrying a lamp:
ghosting follows the changing lamp position relative to camera.

Moving sun due to camera movement:
ghosting changes as the sun enters or leaves the required optical position.

Do not treat ghosting as part of the physical environment.

It is an optical image generated inside the camera system.

==================================================
FOCUS CHANGES
==================================================

Ghosting is primarily governed by light entering the optical system, not by focus distance.

Therefore:

do not automatically intensify ghosting during a rack focus.

Focus may alter the appearance of some optical structures, but the effect must remain lens-dependent.

Do not confuse:

FOCUS CHANGE

with

GHOSTING GENERATION.

==================================================
ZOOM
==================================================

During a zoom:

the relationship between the light source and the optical system may change.

Ghost structures may change:

- position
- scale
- intensity
- visibility

according to the selected lens design and optical geometry.

Do not simply scale a ghost overlay with the image.

==================================================
DOLLY
==================================================

A dolly changes camera position.

This can change:

- source angle
- source intensity at the lens
- relative position of ghost structures
- visibility of internal reflections

Ghosting must respond to the physical camera movement.

==================================================
SUN GHOSTING
==================================================

The sun is one of the strongest potential sources of lens ghosting.

When directly or nearly directly photographed:

possible behaviour includes:

- faint internal reflections
- subtle circular or polygonal ghosts
- reduced contrast
- slight veiling flare
- occasional lens-specific streaks

Do not automatically create:

- giant rainbow rings
- multiple perfect circles
- large neon artifacts
- exaggerated cinematic flare patterns

The sun itself should remain the dominant source.

==================================================
FIRE, TORCHES, DIYAS AND LAMPS
==================================================

Ramayana environments may contain:

- diyas
- oil lamps
- torches
- fire pits
- ceremonial flames
- lamps
- reflected firelight

These may create optical ghosting when sufficiently bright and appropriately positioned.

However:

not every flame should produce visible ghosting.

Evaluate:

- brightness
- distance
- camera angle
- lens
- exposure
- optical axis
- surrounding darkness
- source size

Natural flame flicker may cause subtle ghost intensity changes, but these must remain temporally coherent.

==================================================
HEADLIGHTS / MODERN ARTIFICIAL SOURCES
==================================================

For scenes involving extremely bright artificial sources:

ghosting may appear when physically justified.

Do not automatically transfer modern cinematic headlight ghost behaviour to historical Ramayana scenes.

Use only sources that exist within the scene.

==================================================
ANAMORPHIC LENSES
==================================================

Anamorphic lenses may exhibit distinctive flare and ghost behaviour depending on their construction.

However:

anamorphic does NOT automatically mean heavy ghosting.

The system must respect the specific anamorphic optical profile.

Possible characteristics include:

- elongated internal reflections
- subtle streak-related reflections
- lens-specific ghost shapes
- increased flare sensitivity

Do not automatically create:

- blue horizontal ghost streaks
- repeated oval ghosts
- extreme anamorphic artifacts

Those require actual optical justification.

==================================================
SPHERICAL LENSES
==================================================

Spherical lenses should exhibit ghosting according to their own optical construction.

Do not give spherical lenses anamorphic ghost behaviour.

Avoid universal cinematic lens-flare templates.

==================================================
GHOSTING VS VEILING FLARE
==================================================

Keep these separate.

GHOSTING:
distinct or semi-distinct secondary reflections.

VEILING FLARE:
general reduction in contrast caused by stray light.

A scene may have:

- ghosting without strong veiling flare
- veiling flare without obvious ghosts
- both
- neither

Do not automatically generate both together.

==================================================
GHOSTING VS BOKEH
==================================================

Ghost images are not bokeh.

Bokeh results from out-of-focus rendering of light sources.

Ghosting results from internal optical reflections.

A shot may contain both.

Their behaviour must remain independent.

==================================================
GHOSTING VS HALATION
==================================================

Do not confuse ghosting with halation.

Halation is associated with light scattering/reflection around bright areas in the imaging process.

Ghosting is associated with internal optical reflections.

Do not create red/orange halos around bright objects and label them as ghosting.

==================================================
GHOSTING VS CHROMATIC ABERRATION
==================================================

Chromatic aberration creates wavelength-dependent image displacement or colour fringing.

Ghosting creates secondary reflected images.

Do not add RGB edges to ghosting unless the selected optical system physically produces subtle wavelength-dependent reflections.

==================================================
CONTRAST
==================================================

Strong ghosting may be accompanied by reduced contrast.

However, contrast reduction should remain physically localized and source-dependent.

Do not apply a global washed-out look simply because ghosting is present.

==================================================
EXPOSURE
==================================================

Ghosting should respond to exposure conditions naturally.

An overexposed bright source may increase visibility of optical artifacts.

However:

do not use exposure changes as an excuse to generate stronger ghosting.

Exposure, flare, clipping, bloom, and ghosting remain separate phenomena.

==================================================
TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Ghosting must remain stable across frames unless a physical condition changes.

NEVER allow:

- ghost flickering without source movement
- random ghost appearance
- random ghost disappearance
- ghost shape morphing
- ghost colour cycling
- ghost position jumping
- ghost intensity pulsing independently
- duplicated ghosts appearing between frames
- screen-space artifacts

Every change must have a physical cause.

==================================================
FACE AND CHARACTER PROTECTION
==================================================

Ghosting must never be used to obscure character identity.

Do not place artificial ghost images over:

- eyes
- faces
- hands
- important costume details
- weapons
- jewelry
- narrative objects

If real optical geometry places a faint ghost over a face, it must remain subtle and physically plausible.

Never use ghosting as an intentional beauty or stylization effect.

==================================================
ARCHITECTURE AND ENVIRONMENT
==================================================

Ghosting must not distort actual geometry.

Buildings, temples, forests, mountains, roads, structures and objects must retain stable geometry.

Ghost images may pass over them as optical reflections, but the underlying environment must remain unchanged.

==================================================
NIGHT SCENES
==================================================

Night scenes may contain strong point sources against dark backgrounds.

Potential sources:

- torches
- lamps
- diyas
- fire
- stars
- bright reflections

Ghosting may therefore become more visible.

Still:

not every point light should produce a ghost.

The optical conditions must justify it.

==================================================
DAYLIGHT SCENES
==================================================

Bright sunlight can produce stronger ghosting, especially when the sun enters or approaches the lens field.

Maintain realistic interaction with:

- sky brightness
- atmospheric haze
- lens coatings
- lens angle
- aperture
- exposure

Do not make every outdoor shot visibly flare.

==================================================

==================================================

Unless the shot or lens profile specifies otherwise:

subtle / condition-dependent

SOURCE REQUIREMENT:
strong bright source

LENS DEPENDENCE:

high

GHOST BRIGHTNESS:
low

GHOST SATURATION:
very low

GHOST COUNT:

GHOST SHAPE:

==================================================
DECISION HIERARCHY
==================================================

Determine ghosting in this order:

1. STORY PURPOSE
2. SCENE LIGHTING
3. BRIGHT LIGHT SOURCE
4. SOURCE INTENSITY
5. SOURCE POSITION
6. CAMERA ORIENTATION
7. LENS TYPE
8. LENS OPTICAL CHARACTER
9. COATINGS
10. FILTERS
11. APERTURE
12. FOCAL LENGTH
13. CAMERA MOVEMENT
14. SOURCE MOVEMENT
15. RESULTING INTERNAL REFLECTIONS
16. GHOST INTENSITY
17. GHOST SHAPE
18. GHOST POSITION
19. TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
20. FINAL OPTICAL REALISM

Never begin with:

"Add lens ghosts."

Instead determine:

IS THERE A STRONG LIGHT SOURCE?
→ IS IT ENTERING THE LENS UNDER SUITABLE CONDITIONS?
→ WHAT LENS IS BEING USED?
→ HOW WELL IS IT COATED?
→ WHAT OPTICAL CHARACTER DOES THAT LENS HAVE?
→ WHERE WOULD INTERNAL REFLECTIONS PHYSICALLY APPEAR?
→ HOW STRONG WOULD THEY ACTUALLY BE?
→ HOW WOULD THEY MOVE WITH THE CAMERA AND SOURCE?

Ghosting must be a consequence of the camera system, not a visual effect applied to the image.

The final result must look as though a real physical lens produced the ghosting during real photography.

It must never look AI-generated.
