# MACRO LENS BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control the physical optical behaviour of extreme close-up and macro photography.

Macro imagery must be treated as photography performed with a real close-focus or macro-capable optical system.

The system must determine:

- whether macro photography is actually required
- the appropriate lens or close-focus configuration
- subject distance
- focus distance
- magnification
- working distance
- aperture
- depth of field
- focus behaviour
- focus transition
- background rendering
- foreground rendering
- bokeh
- diffraction
- optical aberrations
- perspective
- camera movement
- focus breathing
- temporal continuity

Macro is NOT simply:

"make the object very large."

It is a specific optical configuration in which the subject is photographed at very close distances and potentially high magnification.

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PHOTOREALISM
==================================================

The final video must look like professionally captured live-action macro footage through a real camera and real physical macro-capable lens.

It must NEVER look AI-generated.

Maintain:

- physically plausible magnification
- realistic focus distance
- realistic working distance
- realistic depth of field
- realistic focus falloff
- realistic lens rendering
- realistic surface texture
- realistic reflections
- realistic motion
- realistic optical blur
- realistic diffraction
- stable geometry

NEVER create:

- artificial macro zoom
- digital enlargement disguised as macro
- AI-generated surface detail
- fake microscopic texture
- impossible focus planes
- uniform artificial blur
- depth-map cutouts
- floating objects
- unstable fine detail
- texture crawling
- changing surface patterns
- synthetic sharpening halos
- impossible reflections
- exaggerated bokeh
- artificial focus stacking
- frame-to-frame magnification changes without cause

When uncertain:

choose the physically plausible and less exaggerated result.

REAL MACRO OPTICS ALWAYS TAKE PRIORITY OVER VISUAL IMPACT.

==================================================
OPTICAL CAUSALITY / UNKNOWN-PARAMETER RULE
==================================================

Macro behaviour must be derived from physical close-focus optics.

UNKNOWN does not mean:

- maximum magnification
- extreme close-up
- extreme shallow DOF
- maximum aperture
- maximum sharpness
- exaggerated bokeh
- artificial detail

If the shot does not specify a macro requirement:

do not assume macro.

If macro is required but magnification is unspecified:

select the least extreme physically valid magnification that satisfies
the shot purpose.

Never manufacture macro characteristics for visual impact.

==================================================
MACRO MAGNIFICATION VALIDATION
==================================================

Magnification must remain physically consistent with:

- lens design
- focal length
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject distance
- working distance
- optical accessories
- image-circle coverage

Digital cropping does not increase optical magnification.

Digital enlargement must never be represented as true macro capture.

If the requested magnification cannot be achieved:

RESELECT THE OPTICAL CONFIGURATION.

Do not digitally compensate.

==================================================
WORKING-DISTANCE FIREWALL
==================================================

Working distance must remain physically compatible with the selected lens.

Do not simultaneously require:

- extreme magnification
- extremely long working distance
- extremely wide FOV
- impossible minimum focus distance

unless a physically valid optical configuration supports all requirements.

For living subjects, delicate subjects, flames, reflective objects,
or obstructed subjects:

preserve physically plausible clearance between the front of the lens
and the subject.

==================================================
MACRO FOCUS-PLANE FIREWALL
==================================================

At high magnification, the focus plane must remain physically coherent.

Only surfaces within the resulting depth of field may be sharply resolved.

Do not independently sharpen:

- foreground detail
- subject detail
- background detail

as separate image regions.

Do not use segmentation to maintain artificial sharpness.

If multiple details lie at different depths:

the system must respect the actual focal plane and resulting DOF.

==================================================
MACRO DEPTH-OF-FIELD RULE
==================================================

High magnification may produce extremely shallow DOF, but shallow DOF
must never be assumed merely because a shot is called "macro."

DOF must be derived from:

- magnification
- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject geometry
- acceptable circle of confusion
- viewing conditions

If the required subject cannot remain sufficiently readable at the
selected configuration:

adjust the physical configuration.

Do not add computational blur or computational sharpness.

==================================================
MACRO DETAIL REALISM FIREWALL
==================================================

Macro capture reveals detail but does not create information that the
optical system could not resolve.

Visible detail must remain consistent with:

- lens resolving power
- sensor resolution
- focus accuracy
- lighting
- exposure
- material
- subject identity
- motion
- depth
- viewing scale

Prohibited:

- invented pores
- invented fibers
- invented scratches
- invented engraving
- invented insect anatomy
- invented gemstone facets
- invented plant veins
- synthetic micro-texture
- sharpening halos
- hallucinated surface detail

REAL CAPTURED DETAIL > GENERATED DETAIL.

==================================================
MACRO OPTICAL CHARACTER AUTHORIZATION
==================================================

Macro mode does not automatically authorize:

- strong flare
- strong ghosting
- visible CA
- strong distortion
- extreme bokeh
- anamorphic artifacts
- strong breathing
- exaggerated softness

Each characteristic requires its own physical cause.

Authorization states:

FORCED:
Physically required by the selected configuration.

DERIVED:
Naturally produced by the optical configuration and scene.

ALLOWED:
Physically possible but not required to be prominent.

DISABLED:
Must not be intentionally emphasized or simulated.

==================================================
MACRO MOVEMENT VALIDATION
==================================================

At high magnification, small camera or subject movements can create large
image changes.

Movement must therefore remain consistent with:

- magnification
- camera distance
- subject distance
- focus tolerance
- lens breathing
- parallax
- available stabilization
- intended cinematographic movement

No movement may be added merely to make macro footage feel cinematic.

==================================================
MACRO TEMPORAL VALIDATION
==================================================

Across consecutive frames, maintain:

- magnification
- subject geometry
- focus-plane behaviour
- texture
- fine detail
- bokeh
- lens character
- distortion
- reflections
- refractions
- focus breathing

A change is valid only when caused by:

- camera movement
- subject movement
- focus movement
- lens movement
- lighting change
- exposure change
- actual environmental change

No spontaneous optical or geometric changes.

==================================================
MACRO REFLECTION / REFRACTION FIREWALL
==================================================

For reflective or refractive macro subjects:

reflections and refractions must be determined by:

- material properties
- surface geometry
- camera position
- light-source position
- environment
- focus
- lens optics

Do not generate arbitrary highlights, reflections, gemstone facets,
water-droplet images, or glass distortions.

==================================================
VALIDATION FAILURE HANDLING
==================================================

If the macro configuration is physically invalid:

1. identify the failed physical constraint
2. locate the earliest incorrect parameter
3. correct that parameter
4. recalculate dependent parameters
5. revalidate the complete macro configuration

Never conceal an invalid macro setup through post-processing.

==================================================
MINIMUM OPTICAL INTERVENTION
==================================================

When several physically valid macro configurations satisfy the shot:

prefer the configuration with the least unnecessary optical intervention.

This does not mean choosing the least capable lens.

It means avoiding optical extremes that are not required by the shot.

==================================================
FINAL MACRO REALISM RULE
==================================================

The macro configuration must be explainable as a real cinematographer's
physical camera and lens decision.

The system must be able to explain:

- why macro is required
- why this magnification is required
- why this lens can achieve it
- why this working distance is possible
- why this focus distance is correct
- why this aperture is appropriate
- why this DOF results
- why this movement is possible
- why the visible optical characteristics occur
- why the result remains stable through time

If the explanation is only:

"because it looks cinematic,"

the macro configuration is invalid.

WHAT MACRO MEANS
==================================================

Macro photography is characterized by close focusing and high image magnification.

A traditional macro lens is commonly capable of approximately 1:1 reproduction at its maximum magnification.

Do not assume every close-up is macro.

A close-up can be produced with:

- a normal lens

- close-focus lens

Macro specifically concerns the optical magnification and close-focus capability.

==================================================
MACRO VS EXTREME CLOSE-UP
==================================================

These are related but not identical.

### EXTREME CLOSE-UP

A cinematographic shot category.

It may use:

- macro lens
- telephoto lens
- close-focus lens
- other optical configurations

### MACRO

An optical configuration involving close focus and high magnification.

The shot may be an extreme close-up without being true macro.

Do not force macro optics when the shot only requires a conventional close-up.

==================================================
MACRO SELECTION RULE
==================================================

Select macro behaviour only when the shot requires:

- very small subject detail
- high magnification
- extremely close observation
- fine material detail
- tiny objects
- insects
- jewelry
- eyes
- texture
- small ritual objects
- weapons/details
- plants
- flowers
- inscriptions
- miniature objects

Do not use macro merely because the subject is visually interesting.

==================================================
MAGNIFICATION
==================================================

Magnification must be physically determined.

Possible configurations include:

- sub-1:1 close focus
- approximately 1:1 macro
- greater-than-1:1 specialized macro
- moderate close-focus

Do not automatically use maximum magnification.

The required magnification depends on the shot.

Higher magnification generally makes:

- framing more sensitive
- focus more sensitive
- camera movement more noticeable
- depth of field shallower
- subject motion more significant

==================================================
WORKING DISTANCE
==================================================

Working distance is the physical space between the front of the lens and the subject.

It is distinct from focus distance.

A macro lens may provide a useful working distance while maintaining high magnification.

Consider working distance when the subject is:

- delicate
- moving
- illuminated
- reflective
- alive
- obstructed by the lens
- sensitive to camera proximity

Do not place the lens unrealistically close to the subject.

==================================================
MINIMUM FOCUS DISTANCE
==================================================

Respect the lens's minimum focusing distance.

If the subject is closer than the lens can physically focus:

the system must not create perfect focus.

Instead:

- move the camera
- change lens
- change magnification
- alter framing
- use an appropriate close-focus configuration

Never violate the physical focusing capability of the selected lens.

==================================================
FOCUS DISTANCE
==================================================

Macro focus distance must be treated as a physical parameter.

Small changes in subject distance can produce large changes in focus.

Therefore:

focus distance must remain extremely stable unless a deliberate movement occurs.

For a static macro subject:

maintain stable focus.

For a moving subject:

track the changing subject distance.

==================================================
DEPTH OF FIELD
==================================================

Macro photography commonly produces extremely shallow depth of field.

At high magnification:

very small changes in subject depth can cause substantial changes in sharpness.

Do not compensate with artificial blur.

The depth of field must emerge naturally from:

- magnification
- aperture
- focal length
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject geometry
- final viewing conditions

==================================================
MACRO FOCUS TRANSITION
==================================================

Focus transitions must be extremely controlled.

A small focus change may cause a significant change in what is sharp.

During a macro rack focus:

- focal plane moves continuously
- sharp region shifts naturally
- previous detail softens
- new detail sharpens
- bokeh evolves
- focus breathing follows lens behaviour

Do not use digital focus masks.

==================================================
MACRO FOCUS TRACKING
==================================================

Focus tracking becomes more difficult at high magnification.

If tracking a moving macro subject:

allow realistic focus limitations.

Do not maintain mathematically perfect focus on every frame.

However:

avoid uncontrolled focus hunting.

The focus system should remain cinematographer-controlled.

==================================================
FOCUS STACKING
==================================================

Do not automatically use focus stacking.

Focus stacking is a computational or photographic technique combining multiple focus planes.

For natural cinematic video:

default:

FOCUS STACKING = OFF.

If a shot explicitly requires a special focus-stacked visual:

it must be treated as a deliberate imaging technique, not normal macro cinematography.

Never create invisible AI focus stacking.

==================================================
APERTURE
==================================================

Macro aperture selection must account for:

- extremely shallow DOF
- required subject sharpness
- light level
- diffraction
- bokeh
- exposure

Do not automatically select maximum aperture.

Do not automatically stop down excessively.

The chosen aperture must balance:

SUBJECT SHARPNESS
+
DEPTH OF FIELD
+
DIFFRACTION
+
LIGHT
+
OPTICAL CHARACTER.

==================================================
DIFFRACTION
==================================================

At high magnification and small apertures, diffraction can significantly affect fine detail.

Do not compensate with excessive digital sharpening.

Macro imagery should retain natural micro-detail.

Avoid:

- sharpening halos
- artificial edge enhancement
- hyper-detailed synthetic textures

Fine detail must remain physically plausible.

==================================================
MACRO SHARPNESS
==================================================

Sharpness should be optical, not computational.

Maintain:

- natural microcontrast
- realistic fine detail
- physically plausible resolution
- smooth transition from sharp to soft

Do not make every surface appear unnaturally razor-sharp.

==================================================
BOKEH
==================================================

Macro scenes can produce strong background blur.

Bokeh must depend on:

- lens design
- aperture
- focus distance
- magnification
- background distance
- point-source geometry

Do not turn every background light into an oversized glowing orb.

For anamorphic macro systems:

use oval bokeh only if the selected optical system supports it.

==================================================
BACKGROUND DISTANCE
==================================================

At high magnification, background rendering is highly sensitive to physical distance.

A background very close behind the subject may remain more recognizable.

A distant background may become strongly defocused.

Do not use a fixed blur amount.

==================================================
FOREGROUND
==================================================

Foreground objects may also become strongly defocused.

Examples:

- leaves
- fabric
- jewelry
- vegetation
- decorative objects

The amount of blur must follow their physical distance from the focal plane.

==================================================
PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Macro photography can produce distinctive spatial relationships because the camera is physically close to the subject.

Do not exaggerate perspective artificially.

For example:

a close macro view of a flower may make nearby petals appear much larger than petals farther away.

That difference should emerge from real camera position and geometry.

==================================================
LENS DISTORTION
==================================================

Macro lenses may be highly corrected, particularly for flat-field reproduction.

However, distortion remains lens-dependent.

For:

- inscriptions
- artwork
- jewelry
- architecture details
- flat objects

prefer appropriately corrected macro optics when geometric accuracy matters.

Do not introduce wide-angle barrel distortion into macro imagery unless physically justified.

==================================================
FLAT-FIELD BEHAVIOUR
==================================================

Some macro lenses are designed for high edge-to-edge performance on relatively flat subjects.

When photographing:

- manuscripts
- inscriptions
- paintings
- coins
- engravings
- technical surfaces

preserve consistent sharpness across the intended subject plane when the selected lens supports it.

Do not artificially flatten the depth of field.

==================================================
CHROMATIC ABERRATION
==================================================

Macro imagery can make CA visible around:

- high-contrast edges
- reflective objects
- metal
- glass
- bright highlights
- fine branches
- small specular details

Keep CA:

- lens-dependent
- subtle
- physically localized

Never create obvious RGB outlines.

==================================================
FLARE
==================================================

Macro scenes may contain strong light sources close to the optical axis.

Potential sources:

- flame
- diya
- lamp
- sunlight
- reflective metal
- jewelry
- polished surfaces

Flare must remain lens-dependent and source-dependent.

Do not add flare merely because the shot is a macro.

==================================================
GHOSTING
==================================================

Strong point sources may produce internal reflections.

Ghosting must remain:

- optically plausible
- lens-dependent
- source-dependent
- subtle

Do not create floating circles around every reflective object.

==================================================
MACRO LIGHTING
==================================================

Macro lighting must account for the physical proximity of the lens to the subject.

Consider:

- light direction
- lens obstruction
- working distance
- subject reflectivity
- surface texture
- specular highlights
- shadow scale

Do not create impossible lighting around tiny objects.

==================================================
REFLECTIVE SUBJECTS
==================================================

For:

- gold
- silver
- jewelry
- polished metal
- gemstones
- glass
- water droplets

reflections must obey:

- surface geometry
- light-source position
- camera position
- material properties

Do not generate arbitrary highlights.

==================================================
TRANSLUCENT SUBJECTS
==================================================

For:

- leaves
- petals
- skin
- thin fabric
- insects' wings

maintain physically plausible:

- transmission
- scattering
- translucency
- backlighting

Do not make translucent materials glow artificially.

==================================================
WATER DROPLETS
==================================================

Water droplets may act as small optical elements.

Their appearance should depend on:

- shape
- surface tension
- surrounding environment
- focus
- lighting
- reflection
- refraction

Do not place identical artificial droplets across the scene.

==================================================
INSECTS / SMALL ANIMALS
==================================================

If the macro subject is alive:

preserve biological motion.

Maintain:

- natural movement
- realistic anatomy
- stable body geometry
- stable texture
- coherent limb movement

Do not freeze the subject unnaturally simply because macro focus is shallow.

==================================================
PLANTS / FLOWERS
==================================================

For macro vegetation:

maintain:

- natural surface texture
- realistic veins
- realistic edges
- natural movement
- coherent depth relationships

Wind may move foreground and background elements differently according to their physical position.

Do not create synchronized artificial movement.

==================================================
TEXTILES / MATERIALS
==================================================

For fabric macro shots:

surface detail must remain physically plausible.

Do not generate:

- repeating patterns
- texture crawling
- impossible fibers
- changing embroidery
- unstable weave geometry

Fine details must remain temporally stable.

==================================================
JEWELRY / ORNAMENTS
==================================================

For jewelry:

maintain:

- stable geometry
- consistent gemstones
- realistic reflections
- realistic metal response
- natural focus falloff

Do not allow gemstones to change shape or colour between frames.

==================================================
MACRO AND CAMERA MOVEMENT
==================================================

At high magnification, even tiny camera movements can cause significant image movement.

Therefore:

macro movement should generally be:

- controlled
- deliberate
- smooth
- physically plausible

Possible movements:

- micro-dolly
- controlled slider movement
- slow push-in
- slow lateral movement
- controlled focus pull

Avoid excessive handheld movement unless explicitly intended.

==================================================
MACRO PARALLAX
==================================================

Because the camera is close to the subject:

small camera movements can create strong parallax.

Foreground and background elements may shift relative to each other significantly.

This must emerge naturally.

Do not create artificial parallax through digital image warping.

==================================================
MACRO HANDHELD
==================================================

If handheld macro is explicitly required:

allow realistic micro-movement.

However:

avoid:

- random frame jumps
- digital stabilization artifacts
- geometry warping
- subject morphing
- impossible focus compensation

==================================================
LENS BREATHING
==================================================

Macro focus pulls can make lens breathing particularly noticeable.

Use the selected lens profile.

If breathing is low:

maintain stable framing.

If breathing is stronger:

allow subtle optical framing changes.

Never simulate breathing through digital zoom.

==================================================
ANAMORPHIC MACRO
==================================================

Anamorphic macro is a specialized configuration.

Use only when explicitly justified.

Maintain:

- appropriate close-focus capability
- realistic squeeze
- anamorphic bokeh
- lens-specific distortion
- lens-specific breathing
- physically plausible working distance

Do not force anamorphic behaviour onto a conventional macro lens.

==================================================
SENSOR AND CROP FACTOR
==================================================

Sensor format affects:

- FOV
- framing
- magnification relationships
- DOF

Crop factor does not magically increase optical magnification.

Do not digitally crop the image and call it macro.

==================================================
DIGITAL ZOOM PROHIBITION
==================================================

Digital zoom must not be used as a substitute for macro optics.

If the shot requires greater magnification:

select a physically appropriate:

- macro lens
- close-focus lens
- extension configuration
- specialized macro optical system

rather than enlarging the image digitally.

==================================================
MINIMUM FOCUS AND MAGNIFICATION TRADE-OFF
==================================================

Higher magnification and closer focus can reduce:

- working distance
- depth of field
- focus tolerance
- lighting flexibility

The system must account for these trade-offs.

Do not simultaneously demand:

- extreme magnification
- huge DOF
- distant camera position
- impossible working distance

without a physically valid optical configuration.

==================================================
MACRO CONTINUITY
==================================================

Within a macro shot:

maintain stable:

- magnification
- focus distance
- lens identity
- optical character
- surface geometry
- bokeh
- distortion
- focus behaviour

Unless the camera, subject, lens, or focus physically changes.

==================================================
TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Macro imagery is extremely sensitive to temporal artifacts.

NEVER allow:

- texture crawling
- focus flicker
- surface morphing
- unstable fine detail
- changing hair/fibers
- changing insect anatomy
- changing jewelry geometry
- bokeh popping
- focus snapping
- magnification flicker
- lens-character flicker
- random micro-movement

Every visible change must have a physical cause.

==================================================
MACRO VS AI DETAIL
==================================================

Macro photography reveals fine detail.

Do NOT interpret this as permission to invent detail.

The system must not hallucinate:

- pores
- fibers
- scratches
- engraving
- veins
- insect structures
- gemstone facets
- skin texture

Fine detail must be consistent with:

- subject identity
- lighting
- resolution
- lens resolving power
- sensor capability
- focus
- material
