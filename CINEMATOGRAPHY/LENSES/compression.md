### LENS COMPRESSION BEHAVIOUR

Treat lens compression as a natural consequence of focal length, camera-to-subject distance, subject-to-background distance, and perspective — NOT as a visual effect.

The final video must look like professionally photographed live-action footage captured through a real physical camera and lens.

It must NOT look AI-generated, digitally manipulated, artificially stylized, or visually "enhanced."

==================================================
CORE PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Do not describe or generate "lens compression" as though a lens independently compresses space.

Perspective is primarily determined by CAMERA POSITION relative to the subject.

Longer focal lengths allow the camera to be positioned farther from the subject while maintaining similar framing. This change in camera position produces the familiar compressed-perspective appearance.

Therefore:

CAMERA POSITION
+
FOCAL LENGTH
+
SUBJECT DISTANCE
+
BACKGROUND DISTANCE
=
PERCEIVED SPATIAL COMPRESSION

Do not incorrectly imply that simply changing focal length from the same camera position produces the same perspective-compression effect.

==================================================
PHOTOREALISM — NON-NEGOTIABLE
==================================================

The final video must look indistinguishable from professionally captured live-action footage.

Maintain:

- physically plausible perspective
- realistic spatial relationships
- realistic facial proportions
- realistic body proportions
- realistic object dimensions
- physically consistent reflections
- realistic atmospheric depth
- realistic focus behaviour
- realistic motion
- realistic motion blur
- consistent lens behaviour
- consistent lighting
- stable geometry
- stable textures
- stable faces
- stable hair
- stable clothing
- stable accessories
- stable environmental details

Do NOT introduce AI-generated visual characteristics such as:

- plastic or wax-like skin
- artificial facial structure
- distorted faces
- changing body proportions
- warped architecture
- unstable straight lines
- texture crawling
- flickering details
- morphing objects
- duplicated objects
- unstable hair
- unstable clothing
- floating objects
- impossible reflections
- inconsistent shadows
- artificial depth-of-field cutouts
- excessive background blur
- unrealistic perspective
- exaggerated compression
- digitally flattened depth
- unnatural scale relationships

When uncertain, choose the physically plausible result.

PHYSICAL REALISM ALWAYS TAKES PRIORITY OVER A VISUALLY IMPRESSIVE EFFECT.

==================================================
LONG-FOCAL-LENGTH COMPRESSION
==================================================

When using a longer focal length with increased camera-to-subject distance, reproduce the naturally compressed spatial appearance that results from the changed camera position.

This may produce:

- reduced apparent separation between foreground and background
- background elements appearing larger relative to the subject
- visually flattened spatial relationships
- reduced apparent depth
- tighter layering of distant elements
- stronger visual stacking of subjects
- more isolated framing

Keep these effects realistic.

Do not make distant objects unnaturally huge.

Do not flatten the scene into a 2D image.

Do not remove all depth cues.

==================================================
WIDE-ANGLE PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

When using a wide-angle lens from a relatively close camera position, allow natural perspective expansion.

This may produce:

- stronger foreground-background size differences
- increased apparent depth
- greater spatial separation
- larger near objects relative to distant objects
- stronger edge perspective
- greater sense of environmental scale

Keep facial and anatomical proportions realistic.

Do not exaggerate wide-angle distortion unless the selected lens actually has significant distortion.

==================================================
FOCAL LENGTH SELECTION
==================================================

Choose focal length according to the desired framing and spatial relationship.

Shorter focal lengths generally allow the camera to be closer for the same framing.

Longer focal lengths generally require the camera to move farther away for the same framing.

Do not use focal length alone to describe perspective.

The final perspective must result from the actual camera position implied by the shot.

==================================================
PORTRAITS AND FACES
==================================================

Prioritize accurate human anatomy.

For intimate portraits:

- avoid excessive wide-angle proximity
- avoid unnaturally enlarged noses
- avoid distorted facial proportions
- avoid stretched ears
- avoid warped jawlines
- avoid unnatural facial depth

For longer-lens portraits:

- maintain realistic facial geometry
- allow natural background enlargement
- preserve realistic facial depth
- avoid making the face unnaturally flat

The character must remain visually consistent with their established identity.

==================================================
BACKGROUND RELATIONSHIPS
==================================================

Compression should affect the entire spatial relationship between subject and environment.

When using longer focal lengths and increased camera distance:

- distant mountains may appear visually larger
- architectural layers may appear closer together
- crowds may appear more densely stacked
- trees may appear more tightly layered
- background characters may appear closer to foreground characters

However, preserve physically plausible scale.

Do not make distant objects appear physically larger than they could plausibly be.

==================================================
MOUNTAINS AND LANDSCAPES
==================================================

Longer focal lengths may be used to visually layer:

- mountains
- hills
- forests
- valleys
- architectural structures
- distant settlements
- atmospheric layers

Use this naturally to create spatial density and scale.

Do not artificially enlarge mountains or collapse the landscape into a flat background.

Atmospheric perspective should still provide appropriate depth cues.

==================================================
CROWDS
==================================================

Longer lenses can visually compress crowds and make people appear densely layered.

When appropriate:

- maintain realistic human scale
- preserve individual body proportions
- maintain correct depth ordering
- allow realistic overlap
- maintain consistent motion between people at different distances

Do not allow crowd members to merge, duplicate, morph, or occupy impossible spatial positions.

==================================================
ARCHITECTURE
==================================================

Compression may be used to visually layer:

- palace courtyards
- temple structures
- pillars
- corridors
- city streets
- gateways
- armies
- architectural depth

Maintain physically plausible geometry.

Do not allow:

- bending architecture
- impossible perspective
- inconsistent pillar spacing
- changing building dimensions
- warped straight lines

If architectural accuracy is critical, prefer a lens and camera position that minimize unwanted distortion.

==================================================
TELEPHOTO COMPRESSION
==================================================

For long-lens shots, create natural spatial layering rather than an artificial "compressed background."

Possible applications:

- observing a character from a distance
- large landscapes
- armies
- distant interactions
- architectural layering
- emotionally detached observation
- isolating a subject within a large environment

The effect should remain subtle unless the shot specifically requires strong spatial flattening.

==================================================
DEPTH CUES
==================================================

Even when spatial compression is strong, retain physically plausible depth cues through:

- atmospheric perspective
- lighting differences
- scale relationships
- occlusion
- focus falloff
- texture gradients
- motion parallax
- shadow relationships

Do not remove all depth merely because a long lens is being used.

==================================================
CAMERA MOVEMENT
==================================================

Perspective relationships must remain consistent during:

- dolly movement
- tracking movement
- crane movement
- orbit movement
- pan
- tilt
- handheld movement

Physical camera movement must produce appropriate changes in perspective.

For example:

A dolly toward a subject should produce changing spatial relationships between foreground, subject, and background.

A zoom should primarily change framing and magnification without producing the same perspective change as a physical dolly.

Do not confuse:

DOLLY
with
ZOOM.

==================================================
DOLLY VS ZOOM
==================================================

If the shot requires perspective transformation:

use physical camera movement.

If the shot requires magnification/framing change without changing camera position:

use zoom.

If both are specified:

maintain the distinct optical and spatial consequences of each.

Do not create an artificial "dolly zoom" unless the shot explicitly calls for one.

==================================================
DOLLY ZOOM
==================================================

When explicitly requested, reproduce the physical effect correctly:

- camera moves toward or away from subject
- focal length changes simultaneously
- subject framing remains approximately constant
- background perspective changes dramatically
- spatial relationships appear to expand or contract

Do not simulate the effect using digital scaling.

The result must resemble footage captured using a real camera performing a real dolly zoom.

==================================================
FOCUS AND COMPRESSION
==================================================

Do not confuse perspective compression with depth of field.

Compression describes spatial relationships.

Depth of field describes focus.

A long lens may produce shallow depth of field under certain conditions, but compression and background blur are not the same phenomenon.

Do not automatically blur the background whenever compression is present.

==================================================
ANAMORPHIC INTERACTION
==================================================

If an anamorphic lens is selected, preserve its anamorphic optical behaviour separately from perspective compression.

Do not attribute all spatial flattening to anamorphic optics.

Anamorphic characteristics may include:

- horizontal optical compression
- anamorphic bokeh
- specific flare behaviour
- optical distortion

Perspective compression remains primarily dependent on camera position and subject geometry.

==================================================
SHOT-SPECIFIC USE
==================================================

Use stronger perceived compression when the shot benefits from:

- visual layering
- distant observation
- dense crowds
- mountains
- architectural repetition
- compressed landscapes
- emotional distance
- telephoto intimacy
- battlefield scale
- visual density

Use weaker compression when the shot benefits from:

- environmental depth
- spatial openness
- strong foreground-background separation
- immersive movement
- proximity to the character
- expansive perspective

Do not choose compression simply because the shot should look "cinematic."

==================================================
NATURALISM
==================================================

The audience should perceive a believable camera perspective rather than consciously notice "lens compression."

Do NOT:

- artificially flatten the scene
- enlarge distant objects excessively
- shrink foreground objects unnaturally
- create fake background scaling
- digitally compress spatial layers
- use exaggerated telephoto effects
- make all long-lens shots look identical
- remove atmospheric depth
- create impossible spatial relationships

The result should look like a real lens and real camera position were used.

==================================================
VIDEO TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Because this is AI-generated VIDEO, spatial compression must remain temporally consistent.

During movement:

- perspective must change continuously
- background scale must remain physically coherent
- foreground-background relationships must remain stable
- objects must not suddenly change apparent size
- architectural geometry must remain stable
- faces must remain consistent
- depth relationships must remain consistent

Do NOT allow:

- perspective flickering
- background resizing between frames
- sudden changes in focal length appearance
- objects jumping between depth planes
- unstable spatial geometry
- artificial warping during camera movement

==================================================
STORY-FIRST RULE
==================================================

Compression must serve:

- story
- character relationship
- environment
- spatial geography
- emotional distance
- visual scale
- composition

Never select a long lens simply because it creates a "cinematic compressed look."

The decision must originate from the shot's narrative and spatial requirements.

==================================================
FINAL DECISION ORDER
==================================================

Determine perspective in this order:

1. STORY PURPOSE
2. SHOT TYPE
3. SUBJECT-ENVIRONMENT RELATIONSHIP
4. REQUIRED FRAMING
5. CAMERA POSITION
6. FOCAL LENGTH
7. SENSOR / FORMAT
8. SUBJECT DISTANCE
9. BACKGROUND DISTANCE
10. RESULTING PERSPECTIVE
11. PERCEIVED COMPRESSION

Never start with:

"Add lens compression."

Instead determine the physical camera placement and lens required for the shot, then allow the resulting perspective to emerge naturally.

The final video must look like real professionally photographed live-action footage captured with a real physical camera and lens.

It must never look AI-generated.
