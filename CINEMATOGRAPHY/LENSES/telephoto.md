# TELEPHOTO LENS BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control the physical optical behaviour of telephoto lenses.

A telephoto lens provides a narrower field of view and higher angular magnification than normal or wide lenses. Its characteristic spatial appearance must be produced through the actual relationship between:

- focal length
- sensor format
- camera position
- subject distance
- background distance
- framing
- focus distance
- aperture
- lens design
- camera movement

A telephoto lens is NOT a digital zoom, artificial compression filter, background-scaling tool, or automatic shallow-depth-of-field effect.

The telephoto image must emerge from physically plausible camera and lens geometry.

==================================================
FIELD OF VIEW
==================================================

Telephoto lenses have narrower FOV.

FOV must be physically calculated from:

- focal length
- sensor dimensions
- active sensor area
- aspect ratio.

Do not digitally crop a normal lens and label it telephoto.

Do not digitally magnify the image.

Do not dynamically narrow FOV without a physical focal-length change.

==================================================
SENSOR FORMAT
==================================================

Sensor format affects the captured FOV.

For a smaller sensor:

the same physical focal length produces a narrower FOV.

Crop factor is a framing/FOV relationship only.

It does NOT:

- change physical focal length
- independently create compression
- independently change perspective
- turn a lens into a telephoto lens
- create optical magnification.

The system must use the actual active sensor area.

==================================================
CAMERA POSITION
==================================================

Telephoto shots often place the camera farther from the subject.

Camera position must be explicitly determined.

Consider:

- subject distance
- background distance
- foreground distance
- available physical space
- desired framing
- desired perspective
- desired compression
- subject movement
- camera movement.

Never assume that every telephoto shot requires extreme distance.

==================================================
SPATIAL COMPRESSION
==================================================

When the camera is positioned far from the subject:

relative depth differences may appear reduced.

Possible visual consequences:

- background appears larger relative to the subject
- distant mountains appear visually closer
- crowds appear denser
- layers of warriors appear packed together
- architectural planes appear closer
- forest layers appear visually condensed
- foreground/background scale differences become less dramatic.

This must remain a genuine 3D scene.

Maintain:

- real depth
- real occlusion
- real parallax
- real atmospheric perspective
- real subject/background relationships.

Never create compression by:

- scaling the background
- moving background layers independently
- manipulating depth maps
- flattening the scene into 2D.

==================================================
TELEPHOTO PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Telephoto lenses do not intrinsically flatten perspective.

The perceived flattening comes from the camera being farther away.

Therefore:

DO NOT SAY:

"Use telephoto to flatten space."

Instead:

"Move the camera to an appropriate distance and select the required telephoto focal length to achieve the desired framing and resulting spatial relationship."

- breathing
- vignetting
- coverage.

If the lens changes:

explicitly recalculate the optical system.

Same focal length does not necessarily mean same lens.

==================================================
SEQUENCE CONTINUITY
==================================================

Telephoto usage should remain cinematographically motivated across a sequence.

Do not alternate randomly between:

- wide
- normal
- telephoto

without a shot-purpose reason.

Telephoto may progressively increase as:

- characters become distant
- observation becomes important
- emotional distance increases
- battlefield density increases
- environmental layers become narratively important.

Any such progression must still be physically grounded.

==================================================
COMPOSITION
==================================================

Telephoto framing can emphasize:

- isolated subjects
- layered backgrounds
- symmetry
- repeating architectural elements
- distant figures
- environmental details
- compressed formations.

Maintain:

- headroom
- lead room
- rule of thirds when appropriate
- visual balance
- foreground/midground/background relationships.

Do not sacrifice composition merely to create compression.

==================================================
SUBJECT MOVEMENT
==================================================

Moving subjects must remain correctly scaled relative to the environment.

During movement:

- parallax must remain physically correct
- focus tracking must remain coherent
- motion blur must follow shutter/exposure
- subject/background relationships must remain stable.

No frame-to-frame subject resizing.

==================================================
TELEPHOTO AND HUMAN MOTION
==================================================

Running, walking, fighting, riding and gesturing subjects must retain:

- consistent scale
- anatomy
- limb proportions
- perspective
- motion blur.

Telephoto should not produce unnatural "floating" movement.

==================================================
OPTICAL CHARACTER
==================================================

A telephoto lens may be:

- highly corrected
- neutral
- vintage
- character-oriented
- soft
- high-contrast
- low-contrast.

The selected lens profile determines the character.

Do not automatically make telephoto lenses:

- dreamy
- hazy
- soft
- vintage
- high-contrast.

==================================================
MODERN TELEPHOTO
==================================================

Modern telephoto lenses generally prioritize:

- high optical correction
- controlled CA
- controlled distortion
- high contrast
- high resolution
- controlled flare.

Use restrained optical artifacts.

==================================================
CHARACTER TELEPHOTO
==================================================

Character telephoto lenses may exhibit:

- stronger flare
- more CA
- softer edges
- stronger field curvature
- distinctive bokeh
- more breathing
- lower contrast.

Only use these properties when the selected lens profile supports them.
