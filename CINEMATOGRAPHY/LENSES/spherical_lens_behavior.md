# SPHERICAL LENS BEHAVIOUR

## PURPOSE

Control the physical optical behaviour of spherical lenses.

A spherical lens forms the image through conventional rotationally symmetric optical projection without the horizontal cylindrical squeeze characteristic of an anamorphic optical system.

Spherical lenses are the default optical format for this cinematography system unless anamorphic use is explicitly justified by the shot.

Spherical lens behaviour must be derived from:

- lens design
- focal length
- sensor format
- image circle
- camera position
- subject distance
- focus distance
- aperture
- subject/background distances
- lens coatings
- optical construction
- camera movement
- lighting
- lens continuity

A spherical lens is NOT:

- a neutral digital image
- a completely distortion-free lens
- a generic "clean cinematic" filter
- an automatic shallow-DOF lens
- an automatic wide-angle lens
- a substitute for anamorphic optics

==================================================
SPHERICAL / ANAMORPHIC FIREWALL
==================================================

Spherical and anamorphic systems must remain explicitly distinct.

A spherical lens must NOT inherit anamorphic characteristics merely because they are considered cinematic.

Unless the selected spherical lens profile physically supports the behaviour, do not introduce:

- horizontal anamorphic streaks
- anamorphic oval bokeh
- anamorphic squeeze
- anamorphic edge behaviour
- anamorphic distortion
- anamorphic flare geometry.

SPHERICAL ≠ ANAMORPHIC.

==================================================
SPHERICAL / CAMERA-POSITION FIREWALL
==================================================

Perspective and spatial relationships must emerge from actual camera position.

Focal length alone must never be used to manufacture perspective.

If focal length changes while camera position remains constant:

- perspective geometry remains fundamentally unchanged
- FOV changes
- framing changes
- magnification changes.

If equivalent framing requires the camera to move:

the resulting perspective change must come from the camera movement.

==================================================
SPHERICAL / ABERRATION FIREWALL
==================================================

Chromatic aberration, coma, astigmatism, spherical aberration, and field curvature must remain:

- aperture-dependent where applicable

- restrained.

Never generate:

- obvious RGB outlines
- universal edge fringing
- identical aberration patterns
- exaggerated point-light comets
- arbitrary edge softness.

==================================================
SPHERICAL / DISTORTION FIREWALL
==================================================

Lens distortion must remain independent from perspective.

Do not use barrel, pincushion, moustache, or other distortion to manufacture:

- wide-angle perspective
- compression
- depth
- cinematic character.

Distortion must follow the selected lens profile and frame position.

==================================================
PERSPECTIVE
==================================================

Spherical lenses do not have a unique perspective.

Perspective is primarily determined by:

CAMERA POSITION.

A spherical wide lens can produce strong perspective when placed close to the subject.

A spherical telephoto can produce compressed spatial relationships when used from a greater distance.

These effects must emerge naturally.

==================================================
LENS DISTORTION
==================================================

Spherical lenses may exhibit:

- barrel distortion
- pincushion distortion
- moustache distortion
- complex/asymmetric distortion
- extremely low distortion

depending on lens design.

Do not assume:

SPHERICAL = ZERO DISTORTION.

Do not assume:

WIDE SPHERICAL = HEAVY DISTORTION.

A highly corrected rectilinear wide spherical lens can maintain excellent geometric accuracy.

==================================================
RECTILINEAR PROJECTION
==================================================

Most conventional spherical cinema lenses use rectilinear projection.

Straight lines generally remain straight, aside from lens distortion.

However:

perspective convergence remains.

Therefore:

straight architectural lines
+
strong perspective

can coexist.

Do not remove perspective simply because the lens is rectilinear.

==================================================
FISHEYE
==================================================

Fisheye lenses are a specialized projection type.

Do not classify all fisheye behaviour as normal spherical-lens behaviour.

If a fisheye lens is explicitly selected:

its characteristic curved projection must be used.

Otherwise:

NO FISHEYE DISTORTION.

==================================================
DEPTH OF FIELD
==================================================

DOF must be physically derived.

Inputs include:

- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- sensor format
- subject distance
- background distance
- circle of confusion
- final viewing conditions.

Spherical lenses do not automatically create shallow DOF.

Do not use artificial portrait blur.

==================================================
FOCUS DISTANCE
==================================================

Focus must be placed on the story-critical focal plane.

For human close-ups:

typically prioritize the eyes.

For groups:

select a physically appropriate DOF rather than forcing every subject into perfect sharpness.

For environments:

preserve sufficient depth when spatial geography matters.

Focus must respect:

- minimum focus distance
- focus range
- lens design.

==================================================
FOCUS TRANSITION
==================================================

Focus pulls must be optical.

During a rack focus:

- focal plane moves continuously
- previous subject softens
- target subject sharpens
- bokeh changes naturally
- breathing follows the lens profile.

Never use:

- depth-map blur
- digital sharpening
- focus masks
- focus snapping.

==================================================
FOCUS BREATHING
==================================================

Spherical lenses may have:

- very low breathing
- moderate breathing
- noticeable breathing

depending on design.

Breathing must remain:

- lens-specific
- continuous
- subtle unless intentionally characteristic.

Never simulate breathing through digital zoom.

==================================================
BOKEH
==================================================

Spherical lens bokeh is generally determined by:

- aperture shape
- aperture setting
- lens design
- focus distance
- subject/background distance
- optical construction.

Typical spherical bokeh may appear:

- circular
- polygonal
- cat-eye toward edges
- softly irregular
- characteristically rendered by a particular lens.

Do not make all spherical bokeh perfectly circular.

Do not apply anamorphic oval bokeh.

==================================================
APERTURE SHAPE
==================================================

Out-of-focus highlights may reflect the physical shape of the aperture.

If the lens has:

- 6 blades
- 8 blades
- 11 blades
- another geometry

the resulting highlight shape should be consistent with the lens profile.

Do not create arbitrary bokeh shapes.

==================================================
CAT-EYE BOKEH
==================================================

Edge bokeh may become partially clipped due to optical vignetting and pupil geometry.

This can create:

- oval/cat-eye highlights toward the frame edges.

This is not anamorphic bokeh.

If present:

it must remain:

- lens-dependent
- edge-dependent
- physically coherent.

Do not create uniform oval bokeh across the entire frame.

==================================================
FLARE
==================================================

Spherical lenses can flare when strong light enters the optical system.

Possible sources:

- sun
- bright sky
- fire
- torches
- diyas
- candles
- practical lamps
- headlights
- bright reflections.

Flare depends on:

- lens coatings
- optical construction
- source position
- source intensity
- aperture
- lens condition
- filters.

Do not add anamorphic horizontal streaks to spherical lenses.

==================================================
SPHERICAL FLARE
==================================================

Possible spherical-lens flare includes:

- veiling flare
- ghost images
- halos
- glare
- contrast reduction
- subtle internal reflections.

The exact pattern must depend on the selected lens.

Avoid generic "cinematic flare."

==================================================
HORIZONTAL STREAKS
==================================================

Horizontal streaking must NOT be applied automatically to spherical lenses.

Strong horizontal anamorphic streaks are characteristic of some anamorphic optical systems.

If a spherical lens produces some directional flare:

it must come from its actual optical construction or filter.

Do not manufacture anamorphic-looking streaks.

==================================================
GHOSTING
==================================================

Ghosting is produced by internal reflections.

For spherical lenses:

ghosting may appear as:

- faint discs
- polygons
- arcs
- reflected source shapes
- low-contrast internal images.

It must be:

- source-dependent
- lens-dependent
- temporally stable.

Do not create identical ghost patterns across shots.

==================================================
CHROMATIC ABERRATION
==================================================

Spherical lenses may exhibit:

### LONGITUDINAL / AXIAL CA

Different wavelengths focus at slightly different distances.

May become visible with:

- wide apertures
- high-contrast highlights
- shallow DOF.

### LATERAL / TRANSVERSE CA

Different wavelengths have slightly different magnification.

Often more visible toward:

- frame edges
- high-contrast boundaries.

Keep both:

- subtle
- lens-dependent
- spatially coherent.

Never create obvious RGB outlines.

==================================================
SPHERICAL ABERRATION
==================================================

Spherical aberration can affect:

- sharpness
- contrast
- highlight rendering
- focus character.

Modern lenses may be highly corrected.

Character lenses may retain more.

Do not automatically introduce spherical aberration.

==================================================
COMA
==================================================

Coma can affect point-source rendering, particularly toward the frame edges.

It may be visible around:

- stars
- distant lights
- bright point sources.

The effect must depend on:

- lens design
- aperture
- frame position.

Do not turn every point light into a comet-like shape.

==================================================
ASTIGMATISM
==================================================

Astigmatism can produce different sharpness behaviour in different image directions.

It may become more visible:

- toward edges
- at wider apertures
- with certain lens designs.

Keep it subtle unless the selected lens intentionally has stronger character.

==================================================
FIELD CURVATURE
==================================================

Spherical lenses may have a curved focal surface.

This can cause:

- centre-focused subjects to differ in edge sharpness
- edge-focused subjects to differ from centre sharpness.

Do not use field curvature as an artificial blur effect.

Use the selected lens profile.

==================================================
VIGNETTING
==================================================

Spherical lenses may exhibit natural light falloff toward the edges.

Possible causes:

- optical design
- mechanical obstruction
- aperture
- sensor coverage
- lens image circle.

Vignetting must be lens-dependent.

Do not add a generic cinematic vignette.

==================================================
IMAGE CIRCLE
==================================================

The spherical lens must physically cover the selected sensor.

If:

LENS_IMAGE_CIRCLE < SENSOR_ACTIVE_AREA

then:

coverage is invalid.

Possible consequences may include:

- mechanical vignetting
- dark corners
- incomplete image.

Do not digitally hide an incompatible lens.

==================================================
SENSOR INTERACTION
==================================================

Sensor format affects how much of the spherical lens's image circle is captured.

A smaller sensor may exclude:

- outer distortion
- edge softness
- edge CA
- cat-eye bokeh
- vignetting.

Therefore:

lens character must be evaluated together with sensor format.

==================================================
CROP FACTOR
==================================================

Crop factor changes FOV/framing comparison.

It does not:

- change physical focal length
- change MFD
- create compression
- create perspective
- create anamorphic behaviour.

Use crop factor only as a sensor-format relationship.

Spherical lenses should generally form the neutral physical foundation of the Ramayana cinematography system.

They are especially appropriate for:

- grounded character interactions
- palace interiors
- forest dialogue
- natural environments
- architectural details
- emotionally intimate scenes
- realistic observation
- scenes where facial and spatial accuracy are critical.

Anamorphic should be introduced selectively when its optical character provides a genuine cinematographic advantage.

==================================================
CONFLICT RESOLUTION
==================================================

When requirements conflict, prioritize:

1. PHYSICAL POSSIBILITY
2. SENSOR/LENS COMPATIBILITY
3. CAMERA/LENS GEOMETRY
4. STORY PURPOSE
5. SHOT PURPOSE
6. SUBJECT READABILITY
7. COMPOSITION
8. OPTICAL CONSISTENCY
9. SEQUENCE CONTINUITY
10. STYLISTIC PREFERENCE
11. VISIBLE OPTICAL EFFECT

Never sacrifice physical plausibility to produce a stronger lens effect.

==================================================
VALIDATION
==================================================

Before generating a spherical-lens shot, verify:

1. Is spherical optics appropriate for the shot?
2. Is anamorphic actually unnecessary?
3. Is the selected lens genuinely spherical?
4. Is the focal length appropriate?
5. Is the sensor format correct?
6. Does the lens cover the sensor?
7. Is the FOV physically correct?
8. Is camera position physically correct?
9. Is perspective derived from camera position?
10. Is spatial compression derived from camera distance?
11. Is MFD respected?
12. Is focus distance valid?
13. Is DOF physically derived?
14. Is aperture appropriate?
15. Is bokeh lens-dependent?
16. Is distortion lens-dependent?
17. Is CA restrained and physically localized?
18. Is flare caused by an actual light source?
19. Is ghosting physically justified?
20. Is vignetting physically justified?
21. Is breathing consistent with the lens?
22. Is diffraction physically plausible?
23. Is camera movement physically represented?
24. Is zoom distinguished from dolly?
25. Is lens identity stable?
26. Is sensor configuration stable?
27. Are faces geometrically stable?
28. Is architecture geometrically stable?
29. Is optical behaviour temporally stable?
30. Does the final result look like genuine spherical-lens cinematography?

If any answer is NO:

RECALCULATE THE CAMERA/LENS CONFIGURATION.
