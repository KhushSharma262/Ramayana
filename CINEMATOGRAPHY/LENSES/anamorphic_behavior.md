LENS SELECTION HAS PRIORITY OVER LENS EFFECTS:
First determine whether the shot requires anamorphic or spherical optics. Only after selecting anamorphic should anamorphic optical behaviour be applied.
### ANAMORPHIC LENS BEHAVIOUR

Use anamorphic optical behaviour only when it is cinematographically justified by the shot. Do not treat anamorphic as the default visual style.

When an anamorphic lens is selected, reproduce its behaviour as a physically plausible optical characteristic rather than as an exaggerated "cinematic look."

Apply the following behaviour naturally:

- horizontal optical squeeze characteristic of the selected anamorphic optical design; this changes the image's horizontal sampling/projection and does NOT create, increase, or alter perspective or spatial compression
- natural spatial rendering produced by the selected anamorphic optical design while preserving perspective determined by physical camera position and scene geometry; do not invent additional depth, scale, compression, or spatial distortion
- gently elongated oval out-of-focus highlights only when the selected anamorphic lens design, aperture, focus distance, subject/background distances, and visible out-of-focus highlights physically produce them
- restrained horizontal flare only when strong point light sources and the selected anamorphic lens design physically support it; blue coloration is not automatic
- natural highlight bloom and veiling flare only when supported by the selected lens profile, coatings, light-source intensity, source position, and exposure conditions
- mild lens-specific optical distortion toward the edges only when supported by the selected anamorphic lens profile; do not introduce distortion merely because the lens is anamorphic
- natural edge softness or lens character only to the extent supported by the selected lens profile; never use optical character to hide or compensate for geometry errors
- realistic focus falloff
- subtle focus breathing during rack focus only when supported by the selected lens profile; breathing must remain physically consistent with the lens and focus movement
- realistic depth and separation resulting from physical camera position, subject/background distances, focal length, aperture, focus distance, and depth of field; anamorphic selection alone must not manufacture depth or separation
- physically faithful rendering of skin, faces, architecture and environmental details; anamorphic optical character must not alter identity, anatomy, geometry, texture structure, or object topology

Do NOT automatically add:
- blue horizontal lens flares
- extreme oval bokeh
- heavy distortion
- exaggerated edge stretching
- excessive softness
- strong vintage imperfections
- artificial glow
- excessive contrast loss
- obvious "anamorphic" visual effects

Anamorphic characteristics must depend on the shot's:
- selected anamorphic lens design and optical profile
- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- subject distance
- subject/background spatial relationship
- depth of field
- sensor format and active sensor area
- lens coatings and optical construction
- filters and optical accessories
- exposure settings
- light-source intensity
- light-source size
- light-source angular position
- lighting conditions
- subject position within the frame
- camera position and orientation
- camera movement
- focus movement
- established lens continuity
Maintain optical consistency throughout the entire video. Anamorphic characteristics must not suddenly appear, disappear, change intensity, or change geometry between frames unless caused by an actual change in focus, lighting, aperture, camera position, or lens behaviour.

Prioritize natural optical realism over recognizable anamorphic clichÃ©s.

If the shot does not benefit from anamorphic characteristics, do not force them. Use neutral/spherical optical behaviour instead.

### ANAMORPHIC DECISION RULE

Choose anamorphic optics only when the physical characteristics of the selected anamorphic lens are appropriate for the shot.

Valid reasons include:
- required anamorphic image projection and aspect-ratio workflow
- wide compositions physically compatible with the selected anamorphic lens
- the depth-of-field behaviour physically produced by the selected lens at the required framing
- specific optical character physically supported by the selected lens
- physically supported highlight rendering or flare behaviour when appropriate light sources are present
- a motivated visual language established by surrounding shots
- specific anamorphic optical behaviour required by the shot

Anamorphic selection must NOT be justified by claims that anamorphic optics inherently create:
- greater perspective
- greater spatial depth
- greater environmental scale
- greater subject/environment separation
- stronger dimensionality
- a more cinematic image

Perspective, spatial depth, environmental scale, and subject/environment separation must be determined by physical camera position, scene geometry, subject/background distances, focal length, framing, lighting, and composition.

Avoid or reduce anamorphic selection when its physically supported characteristics could interfere with:
- accurate facial proportions
- intimate close-ups
- precise architectural geometry
- detailed object rendering
- required optical clarity
- stable focus behaviour
- shots where lens-specific distortion, flare, softness, or other optical characteristics would materially interfere with the shot's purpose

The lens behaviour must always serve the shot's story, composition, physical camera configuration, and visual intent.
### INTENSITY

Default anamorphic optical character: NATURAL / SUBTLE.

Anamorphic optical character must be derived from the selected physical
anamorphic lens profile and the actual shot conditions.

Physical causality has priority over cinematic intent. A shot-level
justification can authorize selection of anamorphic optics but cannot
create, strengthen, or override an optical characteristic that is not
physically supported.

Do NOT increase optical character merely to make the image appear more
cinematic.

If a specific anamorphic characteristic has no physical trigger:
    that characteristic = NONE.

If a physical trigger exists but is weak:
    render the characteristic only at physically appropriate subtle strength.

If a physical trigger is strong:
    render the characteristic only to the degree physically supported
    by the selected lens, camera configuration, lighting, exposure,
    and scene geometry.

Never increase an optical characteristic solely because the shot is
dramatic, epic, mythological, emotional, atmospheric, or visually important.

Never use anamorphic effects simply because the scene is epic, mythological, dramatic, or cinematic.

The final image should look like it was genuinely captured through an
appropriate real anamorphic lens, not like a spherical image with
artificial anamorphic effects added afterward.

"Appropriate" means physically appropriate for the selected shot,
composition, focal length, camera position, subject distance, lighting,
focus requirements, and established lens continuity.

Do not select anamorphic merely because anamorphic characteristics
would make the shot appear more cinematic.
