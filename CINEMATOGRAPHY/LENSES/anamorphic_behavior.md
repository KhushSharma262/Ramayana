LENS SELECTION HAS PRIORITY OVER LENS EFFECTS:
First determine whether the shot requires anamorphic or spherical optics. Only after selecting anamorphic should anamorphic optical behaviour be applied.
### ANAMORPHIC LENS BEHAVIOUR

Use anamorphic optical behaviour only when it is cinematographically justified by the shot. Do not treat anamorphic as the default visual style.

When an anamorphic lens is selected, reproduce its behaviour as a physically plausible optical characteristic rather than as an exaggerated "cinematic look."

Apply the following behaviour naturally:

- subtle horizontal optical compression consistent with anamorphic optics
- natural anamorphic perspective and spatial rendering
- gently elongated oval out-of-focus highlights when the depth of field produces visible bokeh
- restrained horizontal flare from strong point light sources when physically appropriate
- natural highlight bloom and controlled veiling flare
- mild optical distortion toward the edges where appropriate
- natural edge softness or character without making the image visibly warped
- realistic focus falloff
- subtle focus breathing during rack focus
- realistic depth and separation between foreground, subject and background
- natural rendering of skin, faces, architecture and environmental details

Do NOT automatically add:
- blue horizontal lens flares
- extreme oval bokeh
- heavy distortion
- exaggerated edge stretching
- excessive softness
- strong vintage imperfections
- artificial glow
- excessive contrast loss
- obvious "anamorphic" visual effects

Anamorphic characteristics must depend on the shot's:
- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- subject distance
- lighting
- position of light sources
- subject position within the frame
- camera movement
- depth of field

Maintain optical consistency throughout the entire video. Anamorphic characteristics must not suddenly appear, disappear, change intensity, or change geometry between frames unless caused by an actual change in focus, lighting, aperture, camera position, or lens behaviour.

Prioritize natural optical realism over recognizable anamorphic clichés.

If the shot does not benefit from anamorphic characteristics, do not force them. Use neutral/spherical optical behaviour instead.

### ANAMORPHIC DECISION RULE

Choose anamorphic behaviour when it improves one or more of the following:
- environmental scale
- spatial depth
- separation between character and environment
- controlled atmospheric rendering
- visual grandeur
- wide environmental compositions
- motivated cinematic movement
- specific optical character required by the shot

Avoid or reduce anamorphic characteristics when they could interfere with:
- accurate facial proportions
- intimate close-ups
- precise architectural geometry
- detailed object rendering
- shots requiring extremely clean optical clarity
- shots where optical distortion would distract from the subject

The lens behaviour must always serve the shot's story, composition and visual intent.

### INTENSITY

Default anamorphic intensity: NATURAL / SUBTLE.

Only increase anamorphic optical character when explicitly justified by the shot.

Never use anamorphic effects simply because the scene is epic, mythological, dramatic, or cinematic.

The final image should look like it was genuinely captured through an appropriate real anamorphic lens, not like a spherical image with artificial anamorphic effects added afterward.
