# LENS SELECTION RULES

## PURPOSE

Determine which physical lens configuration should be selected for a shot before any lens-specific optical behaviour is applied.

Lens selection is the first cinematographic decision within the lens system.

The system must select a lens based on:

- story purpose
- shot purpose
- subject
- environment
- spatial relationship
- composition
- desired field of view
- camera position
- subject distance
- perspective requirements
- depth-of-field requirements
- movement
- optical character
- continuity
- practical shooting constraints

Only after lens selection is complete should the system apply:

- focal length behaviour
- field of view
- depth of field
- focus distance
- bokeh
- distortion
- flare
- ghosting
- chromatic aberration
- compression
- breathing
- anamorphic behaviour
- other optical characteristics

The system must never choose a lens by selecting an optical effect first.

==================================================
NON-NEGOTIABLE PHOTOREALISM
==================================================

The final video must look like professionally captured live-action footage using a real camera and real physical lens.

It must NEVER look AI-generated.

Lens selection must produce physically plausible:

- framing
- perspective
- field of view
- subject proportions
- depth relationships
- optical character
- focus behaviour
- background rendering
- movement

NEVER select a lens merely because it produces a desirable "cinematic effect."

NEVER compensate for an incorrect lens using:

- digital zoom
- artificial blur
- artificial perspective
- fake compression
- digital lens distortion
- fake anamorphic effects
- artificial bokeh
- post-generated flare
- synthetic depth maps

When uncertain:

choose the physically plausible lens that produces the least distracting optical behaviour.

REAL CAMERA LOGIC ALWAYS TAKES PRIORITY OVER VISUAL EFFECT.

==================================================
CORE SELECTION HIERARCHY
==================================================

Always evaluate lens selection in this order:

1. STORY PURPOSE
2. SHOT PURPOSE
3. SUBJECT PRIORITY
4. ENVIRONMENT
5. SPATIAL RELATIONSHIP
6. REQUIRED COMPOSITION
7. REQUIRED FIELD OF VIEW
8. CAMERA POSITION
9. SUBJECT DISTANCE
10. PERSPECTIVE REQUIREMENT
11. FOCAL LENGTH
12. SPHERICAL / ANAMORPHIC DECISION
13. DEPTH-OF-FIELD REQUIREMENT
14. FOCUS REQUIREMENT
15. CAMERA MOVEMENT
16. OPTICAL CHARACTER
17. LENS CONTINUITY
18. PRACTICAL CAMERA CONSTRAINTS
19. FINAL OPTICAL REALISM

Never reverse this order.

==================================================
RULE 1 — STORY BEFORE LENS
==================================================

The story determines the visual requirement.

Ask:

- What does the audience need to notice?
- What is emotionally important?
- How intimate should the audience feel?
- How much environment matters?
- Should the audience feel physically close or distant?
- Is the shot observational, intimate, monumental, dynamic, threatening, calm, sacred, or expansive?
- Does spatial geography need to be understood?
- Is the environment itself part of the narrative?

Only then select the lens.

==================================================
RULE 2 — SHOT PURPOSE
==================================================

Identify the shot before selecting the lens.

Possible shot purposes:

- extreme wide establishing
- wide environmental
- full shot
- medium full
- medium
- medium close-up
- close-up
- extreme close-up
- two-shot
- group shot
- detail shot
- object insert
- over-the-shoulder
- POV
- tracking shot
- observation shot
- architectural shot
- landscape shot
- action shot

Shot size alone does NOT determine focal length.

Camera distance and focal length must be considered together.

==================================================
RULE 3 — SUBJECT PRIORITY
==================================================

Determine what must receive visual priority.

Possible priorities:

- face
- eyes
- full body
- multiple characters
- object
- environment
- architecture
- landscape
- action
- spatial relationship

The selected lens must support that priority.

For example:

FACE PRIORITY
→ avoid unnecessary extreme wide-angle proximity.

ENVIRONMENT PRIORITY
→ consider wider focal lengths when spatial context is important.

DISTANT OBSERVATION
→ consider longer focal lengths.

MULTIPLE CHARACTERS
→ select a lens and camera position that preserve natural relationships.

==================================================
RULE 4 — ENVIRONMENT MATTERS
==================================================

Evaluate the physical environment before choosing focal length.

Consider:

- room size
- corridor width
- palace interior
- forest
- battlefield
- mountain
- village
- temple
- river
- open landscape
- crowd
- narrow passage
- large ceremonial space

A lens must be physically appropriate for the available camera position.

Do not select a focal length that requires an impossible camera position.

==================================================
RULE 5 — FIELD OF VIEW
==================================================

Select focal length according to the required FOV.

General full-frame reference:

### ULTRA-WIDE
Approximately 14–24mm

Use when:

- very large environment must be captured
- camera is physically close
- strong spatial immersion is required
- architecture or landscape scale matters

### WIDE
Approximately 24–35mm

Use when:

- environment remains important
- characters and surroundings must coexist
- spatial depth should remain visible

### MODERATE-WIDE
Approximately 35–40mm

Use when:

- natural environmental context is desired
- moderate perspective is useful
- subject and environment should remain balanced

### NORMAL
Approximately 40–60mm

Use when:

- natural human-scale representation is preferred
- perspective should feel relatively neutral
- character and environment need balanced rendering

### SHORT TELEPHOTO
Approximately 70–100mm

Use when:

- character isolation is useful
- flattering facial rendering is important
- background relationships should tighten somewhat

### TELEPHOTO
Approximately 100–200mm

Use when:

- distant subjects must be observed
- spatial layers should feel denser
- strong isolation is useful
- camera access is limited

### SUPER TELEPHOTO
200mm+

Use only when strongly justified by:

- extreme distance
- observational perspective
- specific action coverage
- inaccessible subjects
- deliberate compression

These ranges are descriptive, not rigid.

The actual lens must be selected according to the complete shot.

==================================================
RULE 6 — FOCAL LENGTH IS NOT CAMERA POSITION
==================================================

Never treat focal length as an independent perspective control.

Focal length determines FOV and framing characteristics.

Camera position determines perspective.

Example:

A 35mm lens close to a subject
and
an 85mm lens farther away

may produce similar framing but different spatial relationships.

Therefore:

If the shot requires a specific perspective:

select camera position first,
then choose focal length that achieves the required framing.

==================================================
RULE 7 — SAME FRAMING TEST
==================================================

When comparing lenses:

ask whether the comparison is:

### SAME CAMERA POSITION

Changing focal length changes:

- FOV
- framing
- magnification

but camera perspective remains based on the same position.

### SAME FRAMING

Changing focal length usually requires camera repositioning.

That changes:

- perspective
- subject/background relationship
- spatial compression

Never confuse these two situations.

==================================================
RULE 8 — FACE PROTECTION
==================================================

For important character faces:

avoid unnecessary extreme wide-angle proximity.

Prioritize:

- stable facial proportions
- natural nose projection
- stable eye spacing
- realistic jaw geometry
- natural body proportions

A wide lens may be used for a face if:

- the environment is important
- proximity is narratively motivated
- the perspective effect is intentional

But do not create exaggerated facial distortion merely because the lens is wide.

==================================================
RULE 9 — ARCHITECTURE PROTECTION
==================================================

For architecture:

prefer lenses capable of maintaining appropriate geometric rendering.

Consider:

- rectilinear behaviour
- edge distortion
- camera height
- camera position
- required FOV

Do not select a heavily distorted lens for precise architectural representation unless intentionally motivated.

==================================================
RULE 10 — ENVIRONMENTAL SCALE
==================================================

For monumental environments:

wide lenses may be appropriate when the camera can physically be placed close enough to create strong foreground-to-background relationships.

Use wider lenses to communicate:

- scale
- immersion
- proximity
- spatial depth

Do not create scale merely by digitally widening the image.

==================================================
RULE 11 — SPATIAL DEPTH
==================================================

If foreground, midground, and background relationships are important:

consider a lens and camera position that preserve those layers.

Wide lens + close camera:

may increase apparent spatial separation.

Long lens + distant camera:

may create denser spatial layering.

Choose based on the desired physical relationship.

==================================================
RULE 12 — COMPRESSION
==================================================

If the shot requires tighter spatial layering:

consider a longer focal length combined with an appropriate camera position.

Potential results:

- denser background
- reduced apparent foreground/background separation
- larger apparent background
- stronger layering
- observational feel

Do not create compression digitally.

Remember:

compression is primarily produced by camera position relative to scene depth.

==================================================
RULE 13 — DEPTH OF FIELD
==================================================

Do not choose a lens solely because it can produce shallow depth of field.

Determine:

- number of important subjects
- subject distance
- background distance
- required environmental information
- aperture
- focus distance

A longer lens does not automatically mean shallow DOF.

A wider lens does not automatically mean deep DOF.

DOF must be derived from the entire camera configuration.

==================================================
RULE 14 — MULTIPLE SUBJECTS
==================================================

When several characters matter:

avoid automatically selecting an extremely long lens with an extremely shallow focus plane.

Evaluate:

- subject spacing
- camera distance
- aperture
- focal length
- focus distance
- required simultaneous sharpness

If multiple characters occupy different depths:

choose a configuration that can physically support the intended readability.

==================================================
RULE 15 — ANAMORPHIC VS SPHERICAL
==================================================

This decision must occur BEFORE applying lens effects.

### SELECT ANAMORPHIC WHEN:

The shot benefits from:

- broad environmental scale
- specific anamorphic spatial rendering
- selective horizontal optical character
- motivated cinematic grandeur
- particular anamorphic bokeh
- controlled anamorphic flare behaviour
- lens-specific character required by the visual language

### SELECT SPHERICAL WHEN:

The shot requires:

- natural optical clarity
- precise facial representation
- architectural precision
- restrained optical character
- clean environmental geometry
- minimal lens artifacts
- intimate or technically precise rendering

Anamorphic is NOT the default.

Do not choose anamorphic simply because the shot should look cinematic.

==================================================
RULE 16 — ANAMORPHIC IS A LENS CHOICE
==================================================

Once anamorphic is selected:

apply its optical behaviour naturally.

Do not add anamorphic characteristics to a spherical lens.

Do not create:

- artificial horizontal flares
- artificial oval bokeh
- digital squeeze
- exaggerated edge stretching

unless supported by the selected physical lens.

==================================================
RULE 17 — NATURAL OPTICAL CHARACTER
==================================================

Select lenses according to the optical character required by the shot.

Possible character dimensions:

- modern / corrected
- neutral
- vintage
- soft
- high contrast
- low contrast
- clean
- character-rich
- anamorphic
- spherical

Do not choose "character" merely to create visible imperfections.

Optical character should support the story.

==================================================
RULE 18 — MODERN VS VINTAGE
==================================================

### MODERN CORRECTED

Prefer when:

- facial accuracy is critical
- architecture is important
- clean geometry is required
- optical consistency is essential
- controlled flare is desired

### VINTAGE / CHARACTER

Consider when:

- softer rendering supports the scene
- historical atmosphere is desired
- distinctive optical character supports the visual language
- controlled imperfections contribute meaningfully

Do not assume vintage means:

- heavy distortion
- heavy flare
- strong CA
- strong softness
- heavy breathing

Evaluate each property independently.

==================================================
RULE 19 — LIGHTING INTERACTION
==================================================

Consider the scene's light sources before finalizing lens choice.

Evaluate:

- sun
- fire
- torches
- diyas
- lamps
- reflective surfaces
- bright sky
- practical lights

A flare-sensitive lens may produce significantly different results under these conditions.

If optical cleanliness is important:

choose an appropriate lens profile.

Do not choose a flare-prone lens simply because flares look cinematic.

==================================================
RULE 20 — FOCUS REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

Before selecting a lens, determine:

- starting focus distance
- ending focus distance
- need for rack focus
- subject movement
- camera movement
- focus tracking
- required focus throw
- acceptable breathing

If a critical rack focus is required:

prefer a lens with appropriate focus behaviour.

If composition must remain extremely stable during focus:

prefer a low-breathing lens.

==================================================
RULE 21 — LENS BREATHING
==================================================

If the shot contains a major focus pull:

evaluate lens breathing before selecting the lens.

For composition-sensitive focus pulls:

prefer low-breathing lenses.

For shots where subtle optical breathing is acceptable:

moderate breathing may be allowed.

Strong breathing should only be selected when intentionally useful.

Do not add digital zoom to compensate for lens breathing.

==================================================
RULE 22 — DISTORTION
==================================================

Choose lens distortion characteristics according to the shot.

For:

- faces
- architecture
- precise geometry

prefer controlled distortion.

For:

- immersive wide shots
- subjective perspective
- deliberately unusual spatial rendering

more visible distortion may be acceptable.

Do not confuse:

wide FOV

with

strong distortion.

==================================================
RULE 23 — BOKEH
==================================================

Bokeh should be a consequence of the selected lens and aperture.

If the shot contains:

- candles
- diyas
- lamps
- distant fire
- stars
- practical lights

consider how the selected lens renders them when out of focus.

Do not choose a lens solely because it produces attractive bokeh.

The background still needs to serve the story.

==================================================
RULE 24 — FLARE
==================================================

Lens flare must be considered only when the lighting makes it relevant.

Choose a flare-resistant lens when:

- contrast must remain clean
- strong sources enter the frame
- facial detail must remain controlled

Choose a more flare-prone lens only when its optical behaviour is intentionally useful.

Do not select a lens based on a desired flare effect alone.

==================================================
RULE 25 — GHOSTING
==================================================

Evaluate internal reflection behaviour when the shot contains strong light sources.

Ghosting must remain:

- lens-dependent
- source-dependent
- subtle
- physically connected to the source

Do not select a lens merely because it produces recognizable ghost patterns.

==================================================
RULE 26 — CHROMATIC ABERRATION
==================================================

For high-contrast imagery:

evaluate the selected lens's CA behaviour.

For:

- bright sky against dark branches
- reflective metal
- strong backlight
- bright practicals
- high-contrast edges

prefer controlled CA unless a character lens is intentionally required.

Never use visible RGB fringing as a default cinematic characteristic.

==================================================
RULE 27 — FOCAL LENGTH STABILITY
==================================================

For a fixed-lens shot:

focal length must remain constant.

If a zoom is specified:

focal length changes continuously.

Do not create spontaneous focal-length changes.

==================================================
RULE 28 — ZOOM VS DOLLY
==================================================

If the shot requires:

### ZOOM

change focal length.

### DOLLY

change camera position.

### DOLLY ZOOM

change both simultaneously.

Never replace a physical dolly with a digital zoom.

Never replace a physical zoom with artificial camera movement.

==================================================
RULE 29 — CAMERA ACCESS
==================================================

Lens selection must respect the physical space available to the camera.

If the camera cannot physically move far enough:

a longer focal length may be required.

If the camera cannot move close enough:

a wider focal length may be required.

Do not assume unlimited camera movement.

==================================================
RULE 30 — SUBJECT DISTANCE
==================================================

Always evaluate subject distance together with focal length.

A 24mm lens at 1m is not equivalent to a 24mm lens at 5m.

A 85mm lens at 1m is not equivalent to an 85mm lens at 5m.

Subject distance affects:

- perspective
- framing
- facial rendering
- spatial relationships
- depth of field
- background scale

==================================================
RULE 31 — MOVEMENT
==================================================

For moving shots, choose a lens that supports:

- required FOV
- subject tracking
- camera speed
- spatial awareness
- focus distance
- operator/gimbal constraints

Wide lenses may provide more tolerance for subject movement within the frame.

Long lenses may require more precise framing and focus.

Do not choose solely from static composition.

==================================================
RULE 32 — ACTION
==================================================

For action sequences:

consider:

- subject speed
- camera speed
- available space
- required environmental context
- focus difficulty
- motion blur
- camera movement

A wider lens may be appropriate for immersive proximity.

A longer lens may be appropriate for distant observation.

Do not use long lenses simply to make action look cinematic.

==================================================
RULE 33 — ESTABLISHING SHOTS
==================================================

For establishing shots:

first determine how much geography the audience needs.

Use:

- wide
- moderate-wide
- normal
- telephoto

according to the required spatial information.

A wide establishing shot is not automatically superior.

A telephoto establishing shot may be appropriate when distant layers or monumental compression are important.

==================================================
RULE 34 — INTIMATE CHARACTER SHOTS
==================================================

For intimate close-ups:

prefer lenses that preserve natural facial proportions and allow appropriate camera distance.

Common useful region:

approximately 70–100mm full-frame equivalent.

But this is not mandatory.

The correct lens depends on:

- camera position
- subject distance
- environment
- emotional intention
- desired perspective

==================================================
RULE 35 — EXTREME CLOSE-UPS
==================================================

For eyes, jewelry, hands, weapons, textures, or other small details:

consider:

- minimum focus distance
- close-focus capability
- required magnification
- DOF
- lens sharpness
- focus breathing
- subject movement

Do not select a lens that cannot physically achieve the required focus.

==================================================
RULE 36 — ENVIRONMENTAL PORTRAIT
==================================================

When both character and environment matter:

choose a lens that balances:

- facial representation
- environmental context
- spatial depth
- subject scale

Avoid excessive telephoto isolation when the environment is narratively important.

Avoid extreme wide-angle proximity when facial naturalism is critical.

==================================================
RULE 37 — GROUP SHOTS
==================================================

For groups:

consider:

- number of subjects
- depth spacing
- camera distance
- required FOV
- edge distortion
- focus requirements

Avoid placing important faces at extreme wide-angle edges if that creates unwanted distortion.

==================================================
RULE 38 — SUBJECTIVE / POV SHOTS
==================================================

POV lens selection should reflect the intended human or subjective perspective.

Do not automatically use an ultra-wide lens for POV.

Consider:

- expected field of view
- camera-to-subject distance
- movement
- emotional intensity
- environmental context

The lens should feel physically plausible for the camera position.

==================================================
RULE 39 — SACRED / DIVINE MOMENTS
==================================================

For spiritually significant scenes:

do not equate divinity with optical effects.

Select the lens based on:

- spatial scale
- emotional intimacy
- character importance
- environment
- composition

Divine atmosphere should primarily emerge from:

- lighting
- performance
- production design
- composition
- movement

not artificial lens artifacts.

==================================================
RULE 40 — CONTINUITY
==================================================

When multiple shots belong to the same sequence:

maintain a coherent lens language.

Track:

- lens family
- focal-length range
- spherical/anamorphic state
- optical character
- distortion
- bokeh
- flare
- breathing
- focus behaviour

Do not change lenses randomly between adjacent shots.

However:

continuity must never force an inappropriate lens.

Story and shot requirements remain higher priority.

==================================================
RULE 41 — LENS CHANGES MUST BE INTENTIONAL
==================================================

A lens change should occur because of a meaningful change in:

- shot purpose
- spatial relationship
- emotional perspective
- subject distance
- environment
- movement
- composition
- visual language

Do not change lenses merely to create visual variation.

==================================================
RULE 42 — NO EFFECT-FIRST SELECTION
==================================================

NEVER reason:

"I want oval bokeh, therefore choose anamorphic."

Instead:

"The shot requires this spatial and emotional representation."

Then:

"Which lens physically provides that representation?"

Only afterward:

"Which optical characteristics naturally result?"

This rule is mandatory.

==================================================
RULE 43 — NO ONE-LENS-FITS-ALL
==================================================

Do not use one default focal length for every shot.

Do not use:

- 35mm everywhere
- 50mm everywhere
- 85mm everywhere
- anamorphic everywhere
- shallow DOF everywhere

Lens selection must be shot-specific.

==================================================
RULE 44 — NO EXTREME DEFAULTS
==================================================

Avoid automatically selecting:

- ultra-wide
- super telephoto
- maximum aperture
- extreme shallow DOF
- extreme distortion
- strong flare
- strong anamorphic character

Use extremes only when the story or physical environment requires them.

==================================================
RULE 45 — PHYSICAL PLAUSIBILITY
==================================================

Every selected lens must be physically plausible for:

- camera format
- sensor coverage
- focus distance
- subject distance
- required framing
- environment
- camera movement

Do not allow impossible camera placements.

==================================================
FINAL LENS SELECTION VALIDATION
==================================================

Before committing to a lens, verify:

1. Does the lens serve the story?
2. Does it serve the shot purpose?
3. Is the subject priority clear?
4. Is the environment important?
5. Is the required FOV correct?
6. Is the camera physically able to occupy the required position?
7. Is the perspective appropriate?
8. Is the focal length appropriate?
9. Is spherical or anamorphic correctly selected?
10. Is the lens physically compatible with the sensor?
11. Is the focus distance achievable?
12. Is the depth of field appropriate?
13. Are multiple subjects sufficiently readable?
14. Is facial geometry protected?
15. Is architectural geometry protected?
16. Is camera movement practical?
17. Is focus tracking practical?
18. Is breathing acceptable?
19. Is distortion acceptable?
20. Is flare behaviour appropriate for the lighting?
21. Is ghosting appropriate?
22. Is bokeh appropriate?
23. Is optical character appropriate?
24. Does the lens maintain sequence continuity?
25. Does the result remain physically plausible?

If the answer to the physical-plausibility test is no:

RESELECT THE LENS.

Do not fix an incorrect lens through post-processing.

==================================================
FINAL RULE
==================================================

The system must never ask:

"Which lens will make this shot look cinematic?"

It must ask:

"What lens would a real cinematographer physically choose to photograph this exact shot for this exact story purpose?"

Then determine:

- where the camera should be
- what the camera should see
- what focal length is required
- whether spherical or anamorphic is appropriate
- how the lens will naturally render the scene
- how focus will behave
- how depth of field will form
- how optical characteristics will emerge
- how the lens will remain consistent throughout the sequence

Lens selection comes BEFORE lens effects.

Physical camera logic comes BEFORE cinematic styling.

The final result must look as though a real cinematographer selected and operated a real physical lens.

No effect-first lens selection.
No arbitrary optical effects.
No digital lens simulation.
No impossible camera placement.
No artificial perspective.
No AI-generated optical behaviour.

It must never look AI-generated.
