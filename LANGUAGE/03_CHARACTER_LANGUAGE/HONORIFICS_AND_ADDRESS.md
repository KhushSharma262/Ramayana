# HONORIFICS AND ADDRESS SYSTEM

## 1. PURPOSE

This file defines the project's system for:
- names
- titles
- epithets
- kinship terms
- pronouns
- vocatives
- honorifics
- forms of address
- relationship-dependent speech
- hierarchy-sensitive language
- intimacy and distance
- public and private address
- changes in address caused by emotion or circumstance

This file is the authority for HOW ONE CHARACTER ADDRESSES ANOTHER.

It does not define the complete language style of a character.
Character-wide speech behavior belongs in CHARACTER_LANGUAGE.md.

---

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

Address is contextual.

A character must not have one permanently fixed form of address for every person and every situation.

Address depends on:

speaker:
listener:
relationship:
relative_age:
social_status:
spiritual_status:
authority:
kinship:
intimacy:
public_or_private:
ritual_or_ordinary:
emotional_state:
canonical_context:
source_evidence:

The same two characters may change forms of address when the relationship, situation, or emotional state changes.

---

## 3. SOURCE AUTHORITY

Forms of address must be derived in this order:

1. Direct canonical usage
2. Repeated canonical usage
3. Canonical narration describing the relationship
4. Canonical kinship/social relationship
5. Source-language linguistic evidence
6. Traditional interpretation
7. Historical/linguistic reconstruction
8. Project adaptation decision

Unsupported address forms must not be presented as canonical.

## 5. PRONOUN SYSTEM

Pronouns are relationship-sensitive.

Potential Hindi forms:

first person:
मैं
हम

second person:
तू
तुम
आप

Third-person references and demonstratives must also be selected contextually.

No pronoun may be assigned merely because a character is:
- male
- female
- royal
- divine
- elderly
- young
- powerful
- subordinate

The relationship and source context determine usage.

---

## 6. PRONOUN SELECTION

Pronoun selection may reflect:

respect:
तुम → आप

intimacy:
आप → तुम

extreme intimacy:
contextually supported use of तू

authority:
तुम / तू / आप depending on relationship and evidence

devotion:
often elevated respectful forms where supported

anger:
possible reduction of formality where justified

public ceremony:
formal respectful usage

private conversation:
potentially more intimate usage

These are possibilities, not automatic rules.

---

## 7. "तू"

तू must never be treated as simply equivalent to "rude."

Depending on source and relationship, it may express:
- extreme intimacy
- affection
- devotional intimacy
- equality
- emotional intensity
- anger
- contempt
- command
- traditional poetic usage

The exact meaning must be determined from context.

Do not modernize every occurrence into contemporary social assumptions.

---

## 8. "तुम"

तुम may express:
- familiarity
- closeness
- ordinary respect
- equality
- affection
- plural reference
- relationship-dependent familiarity

Its meaning must be determined contextually.

---

## 9. "आप"

आप generally marks elevated respect or formal distance in modern Hindi adaptation.

Its use must be controlled carefully because modern Hindi honorific behavior cannot automatically be projected backward onto every historical or canonical relationship.

When adapting source dialogue, preserve the underlying relationship rather than mechanically applying modern etiquette.

---

## 10. FIRST-PERSON "हम"

हम may occur because of:
- plural reference
- royal/ceremonial usage
- literary convention
- source-language structure
- character-specific usage

Do not automatically interpret every हम as modern singular "royal we."

Source context must determine its function.

## 14. EPITHET CONTROL

An epithet should have a reason for appearing.

Possible reasons:
- canonical quotation
- poetic metre
- devotional context
- lineage emphasis
- emotional emphasis
- identity clarification
- ritual context
- formal declaration
- traditional usage

Avoid excessive epithet switching that makes dialogue artificial.

## 16. ROYAL ADDRESS

Royal relationships require contextual differentiation.

A king may be addressed through:
- personal name
- royal title
- lineage title
- respectful kinship term
- epithet

depending on:
- speaker
- private/public setting
- relative status
- intimacy
- ceremony
- source evidence

A royal character should not be addressed as "महाराज" in every line merely to signal royalty.

---

## 17. KING TO SUBJECT

A king addressing a subject may use:

- formal respectful address
- direct name
- occupational/social designation
- compassionate address
- command
- request
- blessing

The king's authority should be expressed through context and syntax, not constant honorific repetition.

---

## 18. SUBJECT TO KING

A subject may use:

- royal title
- respectful epithet
- formal pronoun
- appropriate vocative
- lineage-based address

The exact form must be determined from source evidence and relationship.

Do not assume every subject must use the same address form.

---

## 19. ELDER TO YOUNGER

An elder may use:
- personal name
- kinship term
- affectionate form
- blessing
- instruction
- command

Respect toward the younger person is not always expressed through honorific pronouns.

---

## 20. YOUNGER TO ELDER

A younger character may use:
- kinship title
- respectful name form
- honorific
- formal pronoun
- deferential verb form
- affectionate respectful address

The combination depends on relationship.

---

## 21. PARENT AND CHILD

Parent-child address must distinguish:

father → child
mother → child
child → father
child → mother

It must also account for:
- affection
- discipline
- grief
- blessing
- public setting
- private setting
- crisis

A parent speaking lovingly to a child should not sound identical to a king addressing a subject.

---

## 22. HUSBAND AND WIFE

Spousal address must not be reduced to generic romantic language.

Track:

husband → wife:
- name
- epithet
- kinship/relational term
- pronoun
- affectionate form
- formal form

wife → husband:
- name
- title
- epithet
- relational term
- pronoun
- respectful form
- affectionate form

Forms must be grounded in canonical or justified traditional evidence.

---

## 23. BROTHER AND SISTER

Sibling address must distinguish:

elder brother → younger sibling
younger brother → elder brother
sister → brother
brother → sister

Relevant variables:
- age hierarchy
- affection
- respect
- authority
- public/private setting
- emotional state

---

## 24. GURU AND DISCIPLE

Guru-disciple language has a strong hierarchy.

Disciple → guru may involve:
- reverential address
- title
- respectful pronoun
- formal request
- devotional vocabulary

Guru → disciple may vary between:
- instruction
- affection
- correction
- blessing
- command

A guru need not sound harsh to establish authority.

---

## 25. SAGE AND KING

Neither side should automatically use the other's social register.

King → sage:
- respect
- reverence
- formal request
- appropriate title

Sage → king:
- dignity
- appropriate respect
- authority where spiritually/socially justified
- instruction or admonition when canonical context supports it

Spiritual authority and political authority must be distinguished.

---

## 26. DEITY AND DEVOTEE

Devotee → deity may use:
- reverential titles
- epithets
- respectful pronouns
- prayer language
- surrender language

Deity → devotee may use:
- compassion
- blessing
- instruction
- affection
- reassurance
- authority

Divine speech must not become uniformly grandiose.

---

## 27. DEITY TO DEITY

Divine relationships must still preserve relationship-specific behavior.

Do not assume every deity addresses every other deity with identical:
- pronouns
- titles
- epithets
- formality
- vocabulary

The specific relationship and source tradition govern the form.

---

## 28. DEVOTIONAL ADDRESS

Devotional intimacy may produce forms that appear linguistically less formal while remaining deeply reverential.

Therefore:

linguistic informality ≠ disrespect

formal language ≠ devotion

Both must be interpreted through context.

---

## 29. AFFECTIONATE ADDRESS

Affection may appear through:
- personal names
- kinship terms
- epithets
- softened imperatives
- reassurance
- repetition
- diminutive forms where genuinely supported

Do not invent modern pet names.

Avoid modern romantic expressions unless explicitly justified by the adaptation.

---

## 30. ANGER AND ADDRESS

Anger can change:

- pronoun
- title
- epithet
- directness
- imperative
- sentence length
- politeness

However, anger must not automatically produce:
- तू
- insults
- profanity
- vulgarity
- modern abusive language

Every such change must be contextually justified.

---

## 31. GRIEF AND ADDRESS

During grief, characters may:

- repeat names
- shift between titles and personal names
- use kinship terms
- speak incompletely
- invoke epithets
- become less formally structured

This should emerge naturally from emotional context.

---

The same relationship may have different address behavior in:

public court:
formal

ritual:
ceremonial

battle:
direct and functional

private chamber:
more intimate where supported

family gathering:
familial

forest/travel:
less ceremonial where appropriate

divine encounter:
reverential

Address must therefore be scene-aware.

---

## 33. CEREMONIAL ADDRESS

Ceremonial address may involve:

- titles
- lineage
- formal epithets
- respectful plural forms
- ritual vocabulary
- blessings
- formal declarations

Ceremonial language must not leak into ordinary conversation without justification.

## 35. NAME VS TITLE

Choosing a name instead of a title may signal:

- intimacy
- equality
- affection
- urgency
- anger
- private context
- emotional vulnerability

Choosing a title may signal:

- respect
- hierarchy
- ceremony
- distance
- public identity
- authority

These meanings are contextual and must not be assumed automatically.

---

## 36. ADDRESS TRANSITIONS

A change in address is itself a narrative event.

Track transitions such as:

formal → intimate
intimate → formal
respectful → direct
title → personal name
name → epithet
epithet → kinship term

A meaningful transition should be preserved when it contributes to characterization or emotion.

Once an address pattern is approved, maintain it across scenes unless:

- relationship changes
- context changes
- emotional state changes
- source evidence changes
- a deliberate adaptation decision changes it

Unexplained switching is a continuity error.

## 40. RAMCHARITMANAS-FIRST RULE

For this project, Ramcharitmanas is the primary textual foundation.

Where Ramcharitmanas provides direct address evidence, that evidence receives priority within the project's established source hierarchy.

Other Sanskrit texts, commentaries, regional traditions, translations, and scholarly sources may provide supporting evidence.

They must not silently override the project's Ramcharitmanas-first hierarchy.

---

## 41. TRANSLATION RULE

When an address form is translated from Awadhi or Sanskrit into Hindi:

preserve:
- relationship
- respect
- emotional force
- intimacy
- hierarchy
- identity
- intended address function

Do not translate only the literal dictionary meaning if doing so destroys the relationship encoded by the original.

---

## 42. MODERNIZATION CONTROL

Do not automatically replace traditional forms with contemporary colloquial Hindi.

Avoid introducing:

- bro
- yaar
- dude
- तुम लोग
- casual slang
- modern romantic pet names
- internet language
- modern corporate language

unless an exceptional production decision explicitly requires it.

Modern accessibility must not erase cultural register.

---

## 43. GENDER AND ADDRESS

Gender must not be used as a shortcut for respect level.

Do not assume:

woman → softer pronoun
man → stronger pronoun
wife → submissive address
husband → dominant address

Address is determined by relationship, source, status, emotion, and context.

---

## 44. STATUS AND ADDRESS

Social status and spiritual status are separate variables.

A king may possess political authority.

A sage may possess spiritual authority.

A deity may possess divine authority.

A parent may possess familial authority.

A younger character may still possess spiritual or moral authority in a particular context.

Do not collapse all authority into social rank.

## 47. AI GENERATION SAFETY

AI must never infer an address form solely from stereotypes.

AI-generated suggestions must be treated as candidates.

Before approval, verify:
- source
- relationship
- language
- register
- pronoun
- title
- epithet
- emotional context
- continuity

AI must not fabricate:
- canonical vocatives
- scriptural quotations
- Sanskrit forms
- Awadhi forms
- historical titles
- source references

---

## 48. EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION

Each address decision should use:

A — direct canonical evidence
B — repeated canonical pattern
C — strong contextual inference
D — linguistic/historical reconstruction
E — traditional interpretation
F — adaptation/directorial decision
U — unknown

Unknown must remain unknown until sufficiently researched.

---

## 49. CONFLICT HANDLING

When sources disagree:

1. Preserve both forms.
2. Identify the exact disagreement.
3. Identify the source and authority level.
4. Apply the project source hierarchy.
5. Record the selected project form.
6. Record the reason.
7. Preserve remaining uncertainty.

Never silently normalize conflicting traditions.

---

## 50. FINAL RULE

A form of address is never merely a word.

It communicates:

relationship,
respect,
distance,
intimacy,
authority,
affection,
emotion,
social identity,
spiritual position,
and narrative context.

Therefore every important address choice must answer:

WHO is speaking?
TO WHOM?
WHAT is their relationship?
WHERE are they?
WHO is socially/spiritually above or below whom in this context?
WHAT are they feeling?
WHAT does the source actually support?
IS this canonical, derived, reconstructed, traditional, or adapted?

If these questions cannot be answered, the address form must not be treated as established canon.

# 51. ADDRESS SYSTEM HARDENING — MASTER OVERRIDES
# ============================================================

This section is normative and overrides any earlier statement in this
file that conflicts with it.

The address system is not a vocabulary list.
It is a relationship-controlled linguistic system.

The minimum address unit is:

SPEAKER + LISTENER + RELATIONSHIP + CONTEXT + EMOTION + SOURCE

No address form is considered fully approved without evaluating all six.

## 52. ADDRESS VS REFERENCE

The system must distinguish:

DIRECT_ADDRESS:
How the speaker addresses the listener directly.

THIRD_PERSON_REFERENCE:
How the speaker refers to the listener when speaking about them.

NARRATIVE_REFERENCE:
How narration identifies the character.

SOURCE_REFERENCE:
How the original source identifies the character.

These four may legitimately use different forms.

Example:

speaker:
Sita

listener:
Rama

direct_address:
नाथ / स्वामी / other source-supported form

third_person_reference:
राम / रघुनाथ / other context-supported form

The existence of an epithet in third-person reference does NOT automatically
authorize that epithet as a vocative.

Likewise, a vocative does NOT automatically become the default third-person
reference.

---

## 53. FIVE DISTINCT ADDRESS CLASSES

Every address/reference form must be classified as one or more of:

1. PERSONAL_NAME
2. TITLE
3. EPITHET
4. KINSHIP_OR_RELATIONAL_TERM
5. VOCATIVE

These categories must never be silently merged.

A word may belong to multiple classes only when the source/context supports
each function independently.

---

## 54. HONORIFIC IS A FUNCTION, NOT A WORD CLASS

Honorificity is not itself a grammatical category.

The following may contribute to honor:

- pronoun
- title
- epithet
- kinship term
- verb form
- imperative construction
- lexical choice
- sentence structure
- ritual language
- deferential phrasing
- indirectness
- blessing/request structure

Therefore:

title ≠ honorific

epithet ≠ honorific

आप ≠ automatic proof of reverence

तुम ≠ automatic proof of equality

तू ≠ automatic proof of disrespect

Honor must be interpreted as a complete construction.
