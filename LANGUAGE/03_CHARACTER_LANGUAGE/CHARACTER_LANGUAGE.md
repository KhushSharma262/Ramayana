# CHARACTER LANGUAGE SYSTEM

## 1. PURPOSE

This file defines the character-specific language system for RAMAYAN.

It governs how language changes according to:
- character identity
- social position
- spiritual status
- age
- relationship
- education
- occupation
- authority
- emotional state
- audience
- situation
- source tradition
- canonical dialogue evidence

This file does not replace:
- LANGUAGE_SYSTEM.md
- HINDI.md
- AWADHI.md
- SANSKRIT.md
- PRONUNCIATION.md
- HONORIFICS_AND_ADDRESS.md

Those files define system-wide language rules.
This file defines their character-specific application.

---

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

Character language must be derived from evidence rather than stereotype.

A character must not be assigned a speech style merely because they are:
- a king
- a woman
- a deity
- a sage
- a warrior
- a servant
- elderly
- powerful
- divine

These identities may influence language, but they do not automatically determine it.

Where canonical dialogue exists, the character's actual textual language takes priority over assumptions.

Where direct dialogue evidence does not exist, the project must distinguish:
- canonical evidence
- derived characterization
- traditional interpretation
- linguistic reconstruction
- adaptation
- directorial choice
- production choice
- uncertainty

Unknown language behavior must remain unknown until researched.

---

## 3. CHARACTER LANGUAGE HIERARCHY

Character language is determined in the following order:

1. Direct canonical dialogue
2. Direct canonical narration describing the character's speech
3. Repeated canonical linguistic behavior
4. Canonical relationship and social context
5. Canonical titles and forms of address
6. Source-language evidence
7. Historical/linguistic evidence
8. Carefully justified traditional interpretation
9. Project adaptation decision

Lower-level decisions must not contradict higher-level evidence without an explicitly recorded reason.

## 5. LANGUAGE IS NOT PERSONALITY

Personality and language must remain separate systems.

A character described as:
- compassionate
- intelligent
- powerful
- proud
- devotional
- gentle
- angry
- philosophical

must not automatically receive a stereotyped vocabulary.

Personality may influence:
- sentence directness
- emotional intensity
- degree of explanation
- rhetorical behavior
- politeness
- questioning
- command style
- silence
- repetition

but these must be supported by character evidence wherever possible.

## 6. CHARACTER-SPECIFIC VARIABLES

Every major character language profile should evaluate:

### 6.1 Vocabulary
- everyday vocabulary
- formal vocabulary
- philosophical vocabulary
- ritual vocabulary
- royal vocabulary
- martial vocabulary
- devotional vocabulary
- familial vocabulary
- technical vocabulary

### 6.2 Sentence Structure
- short / medium / long sentences
- simple / compound / complex constructions
- direct statements
- conditional constructions
- explanatory constructions
- rhetorical constructions

### 6.3 Register
- intimate
- familiar
- respectful
- formal
- ceremonial
- royal
- philosophical
- devotional
- ritual
- authoritative

Register must change according to listener and situation.

### 6.4 Pronouns
Pronoun selection must be relationship-sensitive.

Possible forms include:
- मैं
- हम
- तू
- तुम
- आप

The selected form must never be assigned permanently to a character without contextual justification.

### 6.5 Verb Forms
Track:
- imperative strength
- politeness
- tense
- aspect
- modality
- obligation
- permission
- command
- request
- blessing
- prohibition

### 6.6 Sanskrit Density

Sanskrit vocabulary may increase because of:
- ritual context
- philosophical discussion
- theological discussion
- divine speech
- formal declaration
- quotation
- mantra
- traditional terminology

Sanskrit density must not be increased merely to make a character appear intelligent or divine.

### 6.7 Awadhi Usage

Awadhi usage must be governed by source evidence.

Possible uses:
- direct Ramcharitmanas quotation
- source-preserving poetic passage
- deliberately retained canonical phrase
- traditional performance requirement
- approved adaptation

Modern Awadhi must not be substituted for Ramcharitmanas language without an explicit project decision.

## 10. AUTHORITY AND POWER

Power must not automatically produce rudeness.

A powerful character may use:
- commands
- requests
- blessings
- formal statements
- affectionate speech
- philosophical instruction
- restrained authority

depending on the listener and context.

Likewise, a subordinate character may speak with:
- devotion
- respect
- affection
- fear
- confidence
- disagreement
- emotional urgency

without violating social dignity.

---

## 11. ROYAL SPEECH

Royal characters should not automatically speak in excessively Sanskritized Hindi.

Royal speech may involve:
- controlled syntax
- formal vocabulary
- measured delivery
- respectful address
- authoritative statements
- ceremonial language when appropriate

Private family conversation may be considerably more natural and intimate.

Public royal speech and private royal speech must therefore be distinguished.

---

## 12. SAGE AND SPIRITUAL SPEECH

Sages and spiritually advanced characters must not be written as permanently philosophical.

Their language should vary according to context:

ordinary conversation:
natural speech

instruction:
structured explanatory speech

blessing:
ceremonial language

ritual:
ritual terminology

spiritual teaching:
philosophically precise language

anger:
emotionally intensified but linguistically controlled speech

humor:
only where canon/tradition supports it

---

## 13. DIVINE CHARACTER SPEECH

Divine status does not mean every sentence should sound artificially grand.

Divine characters may use:
- ordinary dialogue
- affectionate speech
- instruction
- blessing
- philosophical speech
- cosmic/theological language
- ritual language

according to context.

Avoid:
- permanently elevated vocabulary
- excessive metaphors
- artificial Sanskritization
- repetitive declarations of divinity
- unnatural philosophical monologues

The audience should perceive divinity through characterization, situation, action, and context rather than through every sentence being artificially grand.

---

## 14. EMOTIONAL MODULATION

Character language must change with emotional state.

Track:

calm:
- measured
- complete
- controlled

joy:
- warmer
- more expressive
- potentially more affectionate

grief:
- reduced sentence length
- hesitation
- repetition where natural
- incomplete speech where justified

anger:
- increased directness
- stronger imperatives
- reduced politeness where canonically appropriate

fear:
- shorter constructions
- uncertainty
- questioning
- hesitation

devotion:
- reverential vocabulary
- prayerful constructions
- appropriate epithets

compassion:
- softer address
- reassurance
- reduced command intensity

Emotional modulation must remain human and performable.

---

## 15. HUMAN SPEECH BEHAVIOR

Characters must not speak like generated text.

Permitted natural behaviors include:
- pauses
- hesitation
- incomplete sentences
- self-correction
- repetition
- silence
- interrupted speech
- emotional breaks
- changes in sentence length

These must be used selectively.

Do not add modern fillers merely to create realism.

Avoid artificial patterns such as:
- "देखिए..."
- "मतलब..."
- "असल में..."
- "आप समझ रहे हैं..."
- repetitive explanatory transitions

unless genuinely appropriate to the character and context.

---

## 16. CHARACTER DIFFERENTIATION

Two characters in the same scene must not become linguistically interchangeable.

Differentiate characters through combinations of:

- vocabulary
- sentence structure
- directness
- politeness
- pronouns
- honorifics
- Sanskrit density
- poetic density
- emotional restraint
- rhetorical behavior
- question patterns
- command patterns
- silence
- relationship behavior

Differentiation must remain subtle.

Do not create artificial accents or exaggerated dialects merely to distinguish characters.

---

## 17. NO STEREOTYPING

Do not automatically write:

king = Sanskritized

warrior = aggressive

woman = soft

sage = philosophical

deity = grandiose

servant = submissive

elder = archaic

young person = casual

villain = harsh

These are unacceptable shortcuts.

Character language must come from evidence and context.

## 19. DIRECT QUOTATION VS ADAPTATION

Every important character line must be classifiable as:

translated_canon

---

## 20. NARRATION AND CHARACTER SPEECH

Narration and dialogue are separate language systems.

Narrator language must not automatically become the character's language.

A character speaking in dialogue must retain their own:
- register
- relationship behavior
- vocabulary
- emotional behavior
- address conventions

Narrative language may be more descriptive than spoken dialogue.

---

## 21. CHARACTER-SPECIFIC LANGUAGE SWITCHING

A character may change language/register when:

- entering a ritual
- addressing a deity
- addressing a guru
- addressing a king
- addressing a spouse
- addressing a parent
- speaking privately
- speaking publicly
- becoming emotionally overwhelmed
- reciting a canonical verse
- quoting scripture
- giving a blessing
- delivering a vow
- participating in a sacred ceremony

Every significant switch must have a reason.

---

## 22. SACRED LANGUAGE

Characters must not casually use sacred terminology.

Words referring to:
- deities
- divine names
- sacred places
- rituals
- mantras
- vows
- religious concepts

must follow the project's terminology and pronunciation authorities.

---

## 23. CHARACTER LANGUAGE CONTINUITY

Once a character's approved language profile is established, it must remain stable unless:

- relationship changes
- emotional state changes
- social position changes
- narrative development justifies change
- source evidence requires change
- project decision explicitly supersedes it

Changes must be recorded rather than silently introduced.

## 26. CHARACTER LANGUAGE AND PERFORMANCE

Written language must remain performable.

Avoid constructions that are technically grammatical but:
- difficult to speak
- unnaturally long
- rhythmically awkward
- unclear when spoken
- overloaded with Sanskrit compounds
- dependent on written punctuation

Final dialogue must be tested aloud.

---

## 27. CHARACTER LANGUAGE AND AI GENERATION

AI may generate candidate dialogue.

AI-generated language is never automatically canonical.

The model must not:
- invent quotations
- invent Sanskrit
- invent Awadhi
- invent historical dialect
- assign unsupported speech traits
- fabricate source references
- modernize source language silently
- convert stereotypes into character rules

Generated dialogue requires validation before approval.

---

## 28. EVIDENCE LEVEL

Every character-specific language decision must have an evidence level:

A — direct canonical evidence
B — repeated canonical pattern
C — strong contextual inference
D — linguistic/historical reconstruction
E — traditional interpretation
F — adaptation/directorial choice
U — unknown

A lower evidence level must never be presented as established canon.

---

## 29. UNCERTAINTY

Where evidence is insufficient:

do not invent.

Record:

unknown:
uncertain:
possible_interpretations:
research_required:
temporary_production_decision:

Uncertainty is preferable to false precision.

---

## 30. CONFLICTS

If different sources produce different character-language conclusions:

record:
- source A
- source B
- exact disagreement
- authority level
- evidence
- project hierarchy
- selected interpretation
- reason
- remaining uncertainty

Never silently merge incompatible traditions.

---

---

## 32. QUALITY STANDARD

A character's language is approved only when it is:

- linguistically correct
- source-traceable where applicable
- culturally appropriate
- relationship-appropriate
- emotionally appropriate
- performable
- internally consistent
- distinguishable from other characters
- free of modern slang unless explicitly justified
- free of unsupported Sanskrit/Awadhi
- free of fabricated canonical claims
- compatible with pronunciation rules
- compatible with honorific/address rules

---

## 33. FINAL CHARACTER LANGUAGE RULE

The goal is not to make every character sound "ancient."

The goal is to make every character sound like:

THAT CHARACTER
speaking
to THAT PERSON
in THAT RELATIONSHIP
in THAT SITUATION
with THAT EMOTION
under THAT SOURCE/TRADITIONAL CONTEXT.

Character language must therefore be specific without becoming theatrical, elevated without becoming artificial, and historically/canonically grounded without pretending that unsupported details are known.
