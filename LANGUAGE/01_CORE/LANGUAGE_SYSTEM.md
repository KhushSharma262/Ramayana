## 2. PRIMARY TEXTUAL FOUNDATION

primary_textual_foundation:
  source: Ramcharitmanas
  author: Goswami Tulsidas
  original_language: Awadhi
  textual_role: PRIMARY PROJECT TEXTUAL FOUNDATION

verified_linguistic_basis:
  - Ramcharitmanas is an Awadhi literary work.
  - Awadhi belongs to the Eastern Hindi linguistic group.
  - The original text must be treated as Awadhi rather than automatically normalized into modern Standard Hindi.
  - Modern Hindi is the principal spoken adaptation language of this production.
  - Original Awadhi passages retain their source identity when preserved.

project_source_policy:
  Ramcharitmanas_first

IMPORTANT:
Ramcharitmanas-first is a PROJECT DECISION.
It must not be described as a universal scholarly hierarchy.

Other approved primary canonical sources remain usable where required.

## 3. SOURCE AUTHORITY HIERARCHY

authority_level_01:
  source_class: direct_canon
  priority: highest
  examples:
    - Ramcharitmanas original text
    - approved primary canonical Sanskrit texts
  treatment: preserve and trace exactly where applicable

authority_level_02:
  source_class: derived_canon
  priority: high
  examples:
    - established textual/commentarial material directly derived from canonical sources
  treatment: distinguish from direct textual wording

authority_level_03:
  source_class: traditional_interpretation
  priority: contextual
  examples:
    - established traditional explanations
    - accepted interpretive traditions
  treatment: never silently convert into direct canon

authority_level_04:
  source_class: linguistic_reconstruction
  priority: supporting
  examples:
    - historical linguistic research
    - pronunciation research
    - dialect research
  treatment: use only when relevant and adequately supported

authority_level_05:
  source_class: translation
  priority: supporting
  treatment: never replace the original source

authority_level_06:
  source_class: adaptation
  priority: production
  treatment: identify as adaptation

authority_level_07:
  source_class: directorial_choice
  priority: project-specific
  treatment: valid only when explicitly approved

authority_level_08:
  source_class: production_choice
  priority: implementation
  treatment: must not be presented as canon

authority_level_09:
  source_class: unknown
  treatment: research required

authority_level_10:
  source_class: uncertain
  treatment: preserve uncertainty until resolved

Default project rule:

Ramcharitmanas takes priority for the project's primary textual adaptation unless an explicitly approved project decision states otherwise.

A different source may still be preferred for:
  - Sanskrit linguistic facts
  - historical phonology
  - pronunciation
  - textual comparison

## 5. LANGUAGE LAYERS

The project has three principal linguistic layers.

LAYER_01:
  language: Modern Hindi
  role: primary spoken screenplay language

LAYER_02:
  language: Awadhi
  role:
    - original Ramcharitmanas passages
    - selected source-specific quotations
    - passages whose identity should remain tied to the Manas

LAYER_03:
  language: Sanskrit
  role:
    - source quotations
    - shlokas
    - mantras
    - ritual language
    - spiritually significant speech
    - divine or traditionally Sanskritic contexts
    - terminology where source-supported

RULE:
Do not insert Sanskrit merely to create an artificial "mythological" sound.

RULE:
Do not convert Awadhi source material into generic Hindi when preservation is required.

RULE:
Do not force Awadhi into every spoken exchange.

## 6. HINDI STANDARD

The production Hindi should be:

  refined: true
  grammatically_correct: true
  respectful: true
  natural: true
  emotionally_precise: true
  accessible: true
  culturally_dignified: true
  human_performable: true

Avoid:

  - Gen-Z expressions
  - internet slang
  - meme language
  - texting language
  - profanity
  - obscene language
  - crude insults
  - unnecessary English
  - unnecessary Hinglish
  - corporate terminology
  - contemporary relationship slang
  - modern social-media expressions
  - artificial Sanskritization
  - artificial archaic speech

Important distinction:

sophisticated != complicated

The preferred standard is:

natural dignity > unnecessary complexity

## 14. RAMCHARITMANAS SOURCE PRESERVATION

When exact source wording exists:

  preserve_source_identity: REQUIRED
  fabricate_source_wording: FORBIDDEN
  false_attribution: FORBIDDEN

The system must distinguish:

  direct_source_text
  translated_source
  adapted_source
  new_connective_dialogue
  directorial_dialogue
  production_created_dialogue

Newly written dialogue must never be represented as a quotation from Tulsidas.

---

## 15. TRANSLATION POLICY

Translation stages:

1. original_source
2. literal_translation
3. contextual_translation
4. performance_translation
5. final_screenplay_version

Priority:

meaning
>
theological_accuracy
>
narrative_accuracy
>
character_context
>
natural_performance

A literal translation may be grammatically correct but unsuitable for performance.

A performance translation may change surface wording while preserving meaning.

## 16. PRONUNCIATION POLICY

Pronunciation accuracy is mandatory for:

  - character names
  - divine names
  - place names
  - Sanskrit terms
  - Awadhi passages
  - ritual terminology
  - mantras
  - shlokas
  - difficult Hindi words
  - source quotations

Track where necessary:

  original_script:
  transliteration:
  phonetic_representation:
  syllable_breakdown:
  pronunciation:
  pronunciation_notes:
  source:
  confidence:

Important Sanskrit phonetic distinctions include:

  - short vs long vowels
  - dental vs retroflex consonants
  - aspirated vs unaspirated consonants
  - voiced vs voiceless consonants
  - anusvara
  - chandrabindu
  - visarga
  - conjunct consonants

The pronunciation system must not infer pronunciation solely from English spelling.

## 18. HUMAN SPEECH RULE

Dialogue should be performable by a human actor.

Allowed where natural:

  - pauses
  - hesitation
  - incomplete sentences
  - interruptions
  - breath points
  - emotional silence
  - responsive listening

Avoid artificial filler words.

Avoid modern conversational filler unless explicitly justified by adaptation.

---

## 19. DIALOGUE REALISM

Avoid:

  - exposition dumps
  - characters explaining information they already know
  - repetitive declarations
  - constant philosophical monologues
  - excessive metaphors
  - every sentence being profound

  - identical speech patterns for every character

Language must serve:

  character
  scene
  emotion
  narrative
  relationship
  source
  performance

## 20. PROJECT NARRATION ARCHITECTURE

PROJECT DECISION:

The project uses layered narration.

NARRATION_LAYER_01:
  narrator: Tulsidas
  role: narration of the Shiva-Sati-Parvati story in the project's opening architecture

NARRATION_LAYER_02:
  narrator: Shiva
  listener: Parvati
  role: in-world narration of the Rama-katha

The project deliberately distinguishes:

  external_narrator:
    Tulsidas

  in_world_narrator:
    Shiva

  listener:
    Parvati

This is a PROJECT ADAPTATION DECISION.

It must not be falsely described as a literal statement that Tulsidas himself appears as an in-world narrator in the canonical text.

## 22. RITUAL LANGUAGE

Ritual language must remain distinct from ordinary conversation.

Relevant contexts:

  - yajna
  - puja
  - mantra
  - marriage
  - vows
  - blessings
  - prayers
  - spiritual practice
  - divine encounters
  - sacred announcements

Where ritual wording is source-based, preserve source identity.

Do not invent ritual Sanskrit.

Forbidden unless specifically justified by source/context:

  - vulgarity
  - casual sacred terminology
  - crude sexual phrasing

  - forced comedy
  - internet humor
  - disrespectful modern address
  - trivialization of ritual

  - character
  - situation
  - timing
  - relationship
  - traditional context

## 28. SYSTEM BOUNDARIES

LANGUAGE DOES DEFINE:

  - linguistic identity
  - vocabulary
  - grammar
  - register
  - address
  - honorifics
  - pronunciation
  - source-language use
  - translation
  - adaptation classification
  - language continuity

LANGUAGE DOES NOT DEFINE:

  - vocal timbre
  - actor identity
  - voice cloning
  - voice generation
  - acting performance
  - audio mixing
  - music
  - sound effects

Those belong to their respective systems.
