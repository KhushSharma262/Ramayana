# SATI — EPISODE 01 DIALOGUE PROFILE

## 1. CORE DIALOGUE IDENTITY

Sati's dialogue is defined by:
- devotion to Shiva
- emotional intensity
- dignity
- sensitivity
- inner conflict
- independence of thought
- love
- vulnerability
- moral and relational seriousness

Sati must not be written as merely:
- obedient
- rebellious
- romantic
- tragic

She contains contradictions.

---

## 2. BEHAVIORAL ENGINE

Sati may:
- deeply revere Shiva
- defend him
- question circumstances
- become emotionally hurt
- struggle between family loyalty and spousal loyalty
- seek answers
- react strongly to disrespect
- attempt reconciliation
- become increasingly emotionally isolated when misunderstood

Her dialogue should reveal internal conflict rather than flatten it.

---

## 3. VOCABULARY FINGERPRINT

Primary semantic fields:
- Shiva
- husband
- father
- mother
- family
- respect
- honor
- devotion
- truth
- dignity
- affection
- pain
- doubt
- duty
- relationship
- insult
- reconciliation

Her vocabulary can shift dramatically depending on whether she is speaking
as:
- daughter
- wife
- devotee
- individual
- wounded person

---

## 4. SENTENCE FINGERPRINT

Calm:
- composed
- respectful
- clear

Questioning:
- careful
- emotionally invested
- increasingly persistent

Hurt:
- personal
- shorter
- more vulnerable

Defending Shiva:
- direct
- morally forceful
- less willing to compromise

Deep emotional conflict:
- pauses
- incomplete thoughts
- repetition
- sudden changes in sentence length

---

## 5. RELATIONSHIP WITH SHIVA

Sati's speech toward Shiva should carry:
- intimacy
- devotion
- trust
- respect

But devotion must not make her incapable of speaking honestly.

She can:
- ask
- disagree
- express hurt
- seek reassurance

---

## 6. RELATIONSHIP WITH DAKSHA

This is a major linguistic contrast.

Toward Daksha, Sati may move between:
daughterly respect
→ hurt
→ defense
→ confrontation

Her vocabulary becomes increasingly focused on:
- respect
- dignity
- Shiva
- injustice
- relationship
- insult

Her conflict should feel personal because the conflict IS personal.

---

## 7. EMOTIONAL ESCALATION

Sati's escalation should have stages:

1. uncertainty
2. questioning
3. concern
4. hurt
5. defense
6. confrontation
7. emotional rupture

Do not jump directly from calm to explosive anger.

---

## 8. RUDE LANGUAGE

Sati should not become vulgar when angry.

Her harshness should come from:
- moral condemnation
- emotional truth
- direct accusation
- refusal to accept humiliation

This makes her different from Daksha.

Daksha attacks through status and judgment.

Sati attacks when necessary through:
hurt + dignity + moral objection.

---

## 9. AFFECTION

Sati's affection is:
- intimate
- devotional
- sincere
- emotionally vulnerable

Avoid modern romance vocabulary.

---

## 10. WHAT SATI MUST NOT SOUND LIKE

Do not make her:
- permanently soft
- permanently angry
- passive
- modern feminist/corporate in vocabulary
- melodramatic in every scene
- vulgar
- submissive to Daksha
- identical to Parvati

---

## 11. DISTINCTIVE CONTRAST

Sati:
DEVOTION + INNER CONFLICT + EMOTIONAL INTENSITY

Parvati:
DEVOTION + INQUIRY + SPIRITUAL DEVELOPMENT

Sati's dialogue should carry the emotional history of an already-established
relationship.

Parvati's dialogue should carry the energy of discovery and development.

---

## 12. CANON / ADAPTATION

Sati's relationship with Shiva, Daksha and her emotional conflict are
traditional narrative foundations.

Specific sentence patterns and vocabulary are production characterization
unless directly supported by source.

---

## 13. FINAL TEST

Before writing Sati:

"What relationship is pulling her in two directions right now?"

If there is no answer in a conflict scene, the emotional dialogue may be too
flat.

# DIALOGUE FINGERPRINT HARDENING — SATI
# ============================================================

## CHARACTER-SPECIFIC HARD OVERRIDES

Sati's dialogue must preserve her simultaneous identities:

DAUGHTER
WIFE
DEVOTEE
INDIVIDUAL

The conflict between these identities is a primary dialogue engine.

### SATI WORD PRIORITY

HIGH:
Shiva, dignity, respect, father, family, husband, truth, insult,
devotion, relationship, hurt, duty

MEDIUM:
destiny, spiritual questions, reconciliation

LOW:
casual humor, impersonal abstraction

### SATI ESCALATION

uncertainty
→ question
→ discomfort
→ defense
→ hurt
→ moral objection
→ confrontation
→ withdrawal/rupture

Do not skip intermediate states unless the scene requires it.

### SATI ANGER

Her anger is not primarily:
status aggression

It is:
PERSONAL HURT + DIGNITY + MORAL OBJECTION.

### SATI NEVER-SOUND-LIKE RULE

Do not give Sati:
- Daksha's hierarchical vocabulary
- Lakshmana's protective aggression
- Maina's maternal vocabulary
- Parvati's exploratory spiritual questioning
- Rati's primarily spousal emotional perception

### SATI SUBTEXT

She may say:
"I do not understand."

while actually meaning:
"I am hurt by what this implies about Shiva."

The distinction should remain available to performance.

### SATI FINAL TEST

If removing "Shiva" from the line makes the emotional motivation
indistinguishable from any generic daughter arguing with a parent,
the line needs stronger Sati-specific motivation.
