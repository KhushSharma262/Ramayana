# RAMA — EPISODE 01 DIALOGUE PROFILE

## 1. CORE DIALOGUE IDENTITY

Rama's speech is governed by:
- self-control
- compassion
- dharma
- responsibility
- clarity
- patience
- dignity
- truthfulness
- consideration for others

Rama does not need verbal dominance to establish authority.

His authority comes through:
- certainty
- restraint
- moral clarity
- calm reasoning
- willingness to listen
- willingness to accept responsibility

His dialogue should feel profoundly human before it feels ceremonially elevated.

---

## 2. BEHAVIORAL ENGINE

Before generating Rama's line, determine:

What duty is he protecting?
Whom is he considering?
What truth does he understand?
What emotional reaction is he deliberately controlling?
What consequence is he willing to accept?

Rama should frequently consider another person's welfare before asserting
his own preference.

---

## 3. VOCABULARY FINGERPRINT

Primary semantic fields:
- dharma
- duty
- truth
- promise
- compassion
- responsibility
- family
- father
- mother
- brother
- respect
- welfare
- righteousness
- patience
- consequence
- acceptance
- protection
- justice
- gratitude
- devotion

His vocabulary should favor morally precise and emotionally controlled words.

He should not constantly use grand theological vocabulary.

---

## 4. SENTENCE FINGERPRINT

Default:
- balanced
- complete
- moderate length
- clear
- calm
- logically ordered

When explaining:
principle → reason → compassionate conclusion

When consoling:
acknowledgment → reassurance → guidance

When commanding:
clear instruction without unnecessary aggression

When angry:
anger becomes controlled severity rather than verbal chaos.

---

## 5. RHETORICAL BEHAVIOR

Rama generally:
- listens before responding
- does not interrupt unnecessarily
- does not repeat obvious points
- does not humiliate people merely because he can
- does not use complicated language to display intelligence
- can explain difficult moral questions simply

He may use questions to guide someone toward understanding, but should not
sound like a lecturer.

---

## 6. EMOTIONAL CONTROL

Rama can experience:
- grief
- anger
- fear
- affection
- joy
- frustration
- compassion

He must not be written as emotionally empty.

The difference is that emotion is generally governed by self-command.

Anger:
firmness + moral clarity

Grief:
simplicity + emotional truth

Affection:
warmth + respect

Compassion:
reassurance + patience

---

## 7. RUDENESS

Rama should normally avoid gratuitous insults.

If someone acts wrongly, he can condemn:
- the action
- the injustice
- the violation of dharma

without reducing the dialogue to personal abuse.

When severe confrontation is required, the severity should feel deliberate.

---

## 8. HUMOR

Possible but restrained.

Humor should emerge from:
- affection
- situational awareness
- familiarity

Never modern comedy.

---

## 9. AFFECTION

Rama's affection is often communicated through:
- concern
- reassurance
- listening
- protection
- respect
- remembering another person's welfare

He should not need constant declarations of love.

---

## 10. CONFLICT STYLE

Rama's default sequence:

listen
→ understand
→ identify principle
→ respond
→ accept consequence

He should not automatically escalate when challenged.

---

## 11. RELATIONSHIP MODULATION

With Sita:
- affectionate
- respectful
- attentive
- emotionally open where appropriate

With Lakshmana:
- fraternal warmth
- responsibility
- calming when Lakshmana becomes aggressive

With parents:
- reverential
- affectionate
- obedient without sounding robotic

With sages:
- respectful
- attentive
- receptive

With enemies:
- firm
- morally clear
- controlled

With subjects:
- compassionate
- responsible
- dignified

---

## 12. WHAT RAMA MUST NOT SOUND LIKE

Do not make Rama:
- permanently philosophical
- emotionless
- preachy
- arrogant
- constantly poetic
- artificially Sanskritized
- sarcastic
- modern-casual
- passive
- weak
- verbally aggressive without cause

---

## 13. DISTINCTIVE CONTRAST

Rama:
DUTY + COMPASSION + SELF-CONTROL

Lakshmana:
LOYALTY + PROTECTION + REACTIVE FORCE

Vishnu:
COSMIC PURPOSE + DIVINE KNOWLEDGE

Shiva:
SPIRITUAL DEPTH + DETACHMENT + COMPASSION

Rama must not sound like a cosmic deity delivering abstract theology in
ordinary interpersonal dialogue.

---

## 14. SILENCE

Silence is important to Rama.

If another character is emotional, do not automatically give Rama a long
answer.

Sometimes:
- listening
- a short reassurance
- a direct statement
- a pause

is more characteristic than an elaborate speech.

---

## 15. CANON / ADAPTATION

Rama's core behavioral characterization is strongly source-grounded.

Specific recurring vocabulary and sentence fingerprints are production
characterization unless directly supported by a source passage.

Never invent a canonical quotation.

---

## 16. FINAL TEST

A Rama line should answer:

Is it truthful?
Is it necessary?
Is it compassionate where appropriate?
Does it preserve dignity?
Does it reflect responsibility?
Would Rama say it this way to THIS listener?

If not, rewrite.

# DIALOGUE FINGERPRINT HARDENING — MASTER OVERRIDES
# ============================================================

This section is normative.

If any earlier statement in this file conflicts with this section,
this section takes precedence.

The purpose of this profile is not to describe the character.

The purpose is to control the CHARACTER'S ACTUAL WORD CHOICES,
SENTENCE BEHAVIOR, CONVERSATIONAL BEHAVIOR AND EMOTIONAL LANGUAGE.

A personality trait that does not change dialogue behavior is not
considered implemented.

---

## 17. PERSONALITY-TO-DIALOGUE REQUIREMENT

Every major character trait must produce observable dialogue consequences.

The chain must be:

CHARACTER TRAIT
→ MOTIVATION
→ THOUGHT PATTERN
→ WORD SELECTION
→ SENTENCE STRUCTURE
→ CONVERSATIONAL BEHAVIOR
→ EMOTIONAL MODULATION

Example:

protective
→ detects threat
→ focuses on danger
→ chooses defensive/action vocabulary
→ shorter/direct sentences
→ challenges the threat
→ becomes sharper under pressure

A profile is incomplete if it stops at:

"Character is protective."

---

## 18. BASELINE VOICE

Every character must have a stable baseline before emotional changes occur.

Required:

baseline_directness:
baseline_formality:
baseline_sentence_length:
baseline_emotional_visibility:
baseline_metaphor_frequency:
baseline_question_frequency:
baseline_command_frequency:
baseline_explanation_frequency:
baseline_repetition_frequency:
baseline_interruption_tendency:
baseline_silence_tolerance:
baseline_rhetorical_density:
baseline_vocabulary_complexity:

These values describe behavioral tendencies, not rigid numerical rules.

---

## 19. EMOTIONAL TRANSFORMATION

Emotion must MODIFY the character's existing voice.

Never replace the character's voice with a generic "angry voice."

For each major emotional state evaluate:

CALM
JOY
AFFECTION
CONCERN
FEAR
GRIEF
ANGER
DISGUST
SURPRISE
DEVOTION
DETERMINATION
HUMILIATION
UNCERTAINTY

Each state must modify:

word_choice:
sentence_length:
directness:
question_frequency:
command_frequency:
politeness:
repetition:
silence:
metaphor:
information_release:

---

## 20. CHARACTER VOICE MUST SURVIVE EMOTION

When a character becomes angry:

DO NOT:
generate generic angry dialogue.

DO:
apply anger to that character's specific psychology.

Examples:

Rama:
controlled severity

Lakshmana:
protective confrontation

Daksha:
status-based condemnation

Sati:
hurt + dignity + moral confrontation

Shiva:
reduced speech + severe clarity

Maina:
emotional urgency + maternal concern

Rati:
fear of loss + emotional directness

Parvati:
firm questioning + conviction

Kāmadeva:
confidence fracturing under danger

Narada:
controlled probing or deliberate provocation

Brahma:
precise correction

Himavan:
dignified firmness

Sita:
moral clarity + dignity

Vishnu:
calm certainty

---

## 21. VOCABULARY FINGERPRINT

Each character must have:

CORE_WORD_FIELDS

and:

AVOIDED_WORD_FIELDS

and:

CONDITIONAL_WORD_FIELDS

A vocabulary fingerprint must describe semantic preference,
not simply a list of attractive words.

Example:

Lakshmana:
CORE = duty, protection, danger, loyalty, action

Daksha:
CORE = propriety, authority, status, conduct, honor

Their dialogue must therefore diverge even when discussing the same event.

---

## 22. VOCABULARY COLLISION RULE

When two characters are discussing the same subject, they must not
automatically use the same semantic framing.

Example:

SUBJECT = Parvati's future

Himavan:
welfare + responsibility + family

Maina:
safety + happiness + suffering + maternal concern

Narada:
destiny + consequence + divine purpose

Parvati:
understanding + choice + spiritual purpose

The subject may be identical.

The vocabulary framing must differ.

---

## 23. WORD CHOICE PRIORITY

When generating a line, select vocabulary in this order:

1. Character-specific semantic field
2. Relationship-specific vocabulary
3. Emotional-state vocabulary
4. Situation-specific vocabulary
5. General project vocabulary

General vocabulary must never dominate when a character-specific
choice exists.

---

## 24. GENERIC MYTHOLOGICAL DIALOGUE BAN

Do not produce lines that could belong equally to:

Rama
Shiva
Vishnu
Brahma
Narada
Daksha
or any other major character

simply because they sound "mythological."

A line fails if its character identity disappears after removing the
character's name.

---

## 25. GENERIC WISDOM BAN

Not every intelligent character should speak in aphorisms.

Do not automatically create:

"Everything happens for a reason."

"Destiny cannot be changed."

"Truth always triumphs."

"All things happen according to divine will."

Such statements require actual contextual justification.

---

## 26. GENERIC POETRY BAN

Not every emotional line should become poetic.

Use plain, direct language when the character and situation require it.

Poetic language must have a reason:

canonical source
ritual context
emotion
character habit
narrative significance
metre
adaptation decision

---

## 27. GENERIC PHILOSOPHY BAN

A divine or intelligent character must not turn ordinary conversation
into philosophy.

Before philosophical language appears, identify:

speaker:
listener:
subject:
reason_for_philosophical_register:

If no reason exists, simplify the dialogue.

---

## 28. ELOQUENCE CONTROL

Characters must not all be equally articulate.

Differentiate:

clarity:
speed:
precision:
vocabulary_range:
ability_to_explain:
ability_to_argument:
emotional_control:

A character can be intelligent without always expressing themselves
with perfect rhetorical structure.

---

## 29. THOUGHT VS SPEECH

A character may think something without saying it.

Never convert every internal motivation into explicit dialogue.

The character may:
- conceal
- imply
- avoid
- redirect
- remain silent
- answer partially
- change subject

This is essential for human realism.

---

## 30. SUBTEXT

Every important line should be evaluated for:

literal_meaning:
intended_meaning:
emotional_meaning:
hidden_concern:
what_is_not_being_said:

Do not force subtext into every line.

However, emotionally important scenes should not become purely explicit
exposition.

---

## 31. INFORMATION RELEASE

Characters should not automatically reveal everything they know.

Each character may:

fully_answer:
partially_answer:
deflect:
question_back:
withhold:
delay:
redirect:
remain_silent:

Information behavior must reflect character psychology.

---

## 32. QUESTION FINGERPRINT

Questions must be character-specific.

Examples:

Narada:
questions to reveal something

Parvati:
questions to understand something

Maina:
questions because she is concerned

Himavan:
questions to evaluate consequences

Daksha:
questions to challenge or judge

Lakshmana:
questions to identify threat/injustice

Rati:
questions because she senses emotional danger

Shiva:
questions may guide reflection

Rama:
questions may clarify another person's position

Sita:
questions may test moral or emotional reasoning

Brahma:
questions are selective and purposeful

Vishnu:
questions are comparatively rare when divine knowledge is established

---

## 33. COMMAND FINGERPRINT

Commands must differ.

Rama:
responsible instruction

Lakshmana:
urgent/protective instruction

Daksha:
authority-based command

Shiva:
minimal, decisive command

Brahma:
principled directive

Vishnu:
purpose-driven directive

Himavan:
protective/paternal instruction

Maina:
concern-driven request/instruction

Kāmadeva:
persuasive suggestion rather than constant command

Rati:
warning/request rather than dominance

Sati:
emotionally motivated insistence

Parvati:
requests/questions rather than domination

Sita:
firm but dignified assertion

Narada:
guidance/provocation rather than conventional command

---

## 34. INTERRUPTION BEHAVIOR

Do not make every character equally patient.

Track:

interrupts_often:
interrupts_when_angry:
interrupts_when_protective:
rarely_interrupts:
allows_silence:
finishes_other_persons_sentence:
backs_off_after_interruption:

Interruption behavior must correspond to personality.

---

## 35. SILENCE BEHAVIOR

Silence is character-specific.

Some characters fill silence.

Some tolerate silence.

Some use silence strategically.

Some become uncomfortable with silence.

Some use silence as emotional expression.

This must be reflected in screenplay dialogue.

---

## 36. REPETITION BEHAVIOR

Repetition must be psychologically motivated.

Possible reasons:

emotion
fear
emphasis
anger
devotion
persuasion
uncertainty
grief

Do not give every character the same repetitive speech pattern.

---

## 37. INTERRUPTION + EMOTION

As emotional intensity rises, each character should have a specific
response:

fight:
interrupt

freeze:
silence

reason:
explain more carefully

protect:
become direct

withdraw:
reduce speech

plead:
repeat concern

provoke:
ask sharper questions

These are character-level behaviors, not universal rules.

---

## 38. RUDENESS SYSTEM

Rudeness must have a character-specific mechanism.

Rude behavior can arise through:

insult
dismissal
command
interruption
condescension
sarcasm
threat
accusation
coldness
belittling
status assertion

Each character must have a different combination.

Do not equate rudeness with profanity.

---

## 39. INSULT TARGET

When a character insults someone, determine what they attack.

Daksha:
status / conduct / propriety / worthiness

Lakshmana:
cowardice / injustice / betrayal / dishonor

Sati:
moral wrong / disrespect / humiliation

Kāmadeva:
may provoke through teasing rather than insult

Shiva:
rarely insults; condemnation is more likely

Rama:
usually condemns action rather than humiliating person

Others:
must follow their own psychological profile.

---

## 40. INSULT LIMIT

No character receives unlimited insulting vocabulary.

Even a rude character has boundaries.

Record:

normal_insult_level:
anger_insult_level:
forbidden_insults:
forbidden_modernisms:
forbidden_vulgarity:

This prevents a character from becoming cartoonishly abusive.

---

## 41. PRIVATE VS PUBLIC PERSONA

Characters may change their speech according to audience.

Track:

private_family:
private_spouse:
peer:
subordinate:
elder:
ruler:
sage:
deity:
enemy:
assembly:
ritual:

Public restraint must not erase personality.

Private intimacy must not automatically create modern casual speech.

---

## 42. STATUS VS PERSONALITY

Status modifies dialogue.

It does not replace personality.

A king who is gentle must remain gentle.

A king who is arrogant must remain arrogant.

A deity who is compassionate must not become cold merely because they
possess authority.

A subordinate who is courageous must not become linguistically helpless.

---

## 43. RELATIONSHIP MODULATION

Every character must be evaluated against:

mother:
father:
spouse:
sibling:
child:
friend:
guru:
disciple:
king:
subject:
sage:
deity:
enemy:
stranger:

The same character may have radically different dialogue behavior
depending on the relationship.

---

## 44. CHARACTER CONSISTENCY WITHOUT REPETITION

Consistency does NOT mean repeating the same words.

Do not make a character recognizable by repeating a catchphrase.

Recognizability should come from:

semantic preference
sentence structure
reaction pattern
argument style
emotional behavior
relationship behavior
information behavior

---

## 45. NO CATCHPHRASE DEPENDENCY

A character should not repeatedly say one signature phrase simply to
signal identity.

Catchphrases are permitted only when:
- canonically supported
- strongly character-defining
- narratively justified

---

## 46. EMOTIONAL ESCALATION LADDER

Every major conflict should have stages.

Possible:

baseline
→ concern
→ disagreement
→ pressure
→ confrontation
→ emotional escalation
→ peak
→ aftermath

Do not jump directly from normal conversation to maximum intensity
unless the source/event requires it.

---

## 47. DE-ESCALATION

Characters must also have a characteristic way of calming down.

Examples:

Rama:
re-centers on duty

Shiva:
returns to silence/composure

Lakshmana:
responds to Rama or recognition of duty

Daksha:
may retreat into authority/control

Maina:
seeks reassurance

Rati:
seeks emotional connection

Sati:
moves toward conviction or withdrawal

Parvati:
returns to understanding

Narada:
returns to observation

Himavan:
returns to reason

Kāmadeva:
may recover confidence or confront consequence

Sita:
returns to moral clarity

Brahma:
returns to principle

Vishnu:
returns to purpose

---

## 48. AFTERMATH BEHAVIOR

Dialogue after conflict must reflect what just happened.

Do not reset characters to baseline immediately.

Track:

residual_anger:
residual_hurt:
residual_fear:
new_understanding:
relationship_change:
new_distance:
new_closeness:

This is essential for episode continuity.

---

## 49. CHARACTER MEMORY

A character must remember significant conversational events.

If someone insulted them:
later dialogue may change.

If someone reassured them:
later trust may increase.

If someone betrayed them:
later speech may become guarded.

Do not treat every scene as an isolated prompt.

---

## 50. DIALOGUE FINGERPRINT TEST

For every major line, remove the character's name.

Ask:

Could this line belong to another major character?

If YES:
rewrite until at least one character-specific behavior is visible.

Possible distinguishing mechanisms:

word choice
sentence structure
argument strategy
question strategy
emotional response
authority behavior
relationship behavior
information behavior
silence
subtext

---

## 51. TWO-LINE COLLISION TEST

For every major scene, compare at least two characters.

If both characters use:
- identical sentence lengths
- identical vocabulary
- identical rhetorical devices
- identical emotional language
- identical politeness
- identical argument strategy

the scene requires revision.

---

## 52. THREE-CHARACTER COLLISION TEST

In scenes with three or more speaking characters:

Each character must have at least one distinguishable:

vocabulary preference
reaction pattern
sentence tendency
question behavior
authority behavior

Do not allow the whole scene to sound like one writer speaking through
three bodies.

---

## 53. DIALOGUE REALISM RULE

Human realism does not mean modern casual speech.

Natural dialogue may include:

pauses
unfinished thoughts
short answers
misunderstandings
corrections
silence
indirect answers
emotional avoidance

But must remain compatible with the project's cultural and linguistic
register.

---

## 54. EXPOSITION CONTROL

No character should explain information that:

the listener already knows
the speaker already knows the listener knows
the audience can infer naturally

unless the repetition has:
emotional
ritual
rhetorical
dramatic
canonical

justification.

---

## 55. CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE CONTROL

Dialogue must respect what the character actually knows at that point.

Never allow a character to speak using:

future knowledge
secret information
later events
omniscient knowledge

unless the character is specifically established as possessing that
knowledge.

---

## 56. CHARACTER BELIEF CONTROL

Knowledge and belief are different.

A character may:
know X
believe Y
suspect Z

Dialogue must reflect the distinction.

Do not make characters speak with narrator-level certainty about matters
they themselves do not understand.

---

## 57. VOCABULARY MODERNISM CHECK

Reject vocabulary that belongs to:
- modern internet speech
- corporate speech
- contemporary dating language
- modern psychology jargon
- modern political jargon
- modern slang
- meme culture

unless an explicit production decision allows it.

---

## 58. VOCABULARY OVER-ELEVATION CHECK

Reject vocabulary that is unnecessarily ornate simply because the scene
is mythological.

Ancient setting does not require every sentence to sound like a sermon.

---

## 59. CHARACTER-SPECIFIC "NEVER SAY" RULE

Every character must maintain a prohibited behavior list.

The list should include:

words_they_would_not_naturally_use:
rhetorical_devices_they_rarely_use:
emotional_reactions_they_rarely_display:
modern_expressions:
unsupported_insults:
uncharacteristic_metaphors:

This list is as important as their preferred vocabulary.

---

## 60. SOURCE / ADAPTATION FIREWALL

Character behavior must be separated into:

CANONICAL:
directly supported

DERIVED:
strongly inferred from repeated source behavior

TRADITIONAL:
supported by later tradition

ADAPTIVE:
created for this production

UNKNOWN:
not established

A production-created vocabulary fingerprint must never be labeled
"canonical speech."

---

## 61. CANONICAL DIALOGUE PRESERVATION

When a source line exists:

source_line:
speaker:
listener:
context:
canonical_function:

must be preserved before adaptation.

The character profile may guide NEW dialogue but must not rewrite direct
canonical dialogue merely to make it "sound more like the profile."

---

## 62. ADAPTATION OVERRIDE

If a canonical line conflicts with a general character profile:

CANONICAL LINE WINS.

The profile must be revised or the scene-specific reason recorded.

The character profile never outranks direct canonical text.

---

## 63. CHARACTER PROFILE OVERRIDE

If a scene requires a deliberate deviation:

scene:
deviation:
reason:
source_or_directorial_basis:
scope:
approval:

The deviation must remain local.

Do not silently rewrite the entire character profile.

---

## 64. FINAL PRODUCTION GENERATION ORDER

When generating dialogue:

STEP 1:
Identify speaker.

STEP 2:
Identify listener.

STEP 3:
Identify relationship.

STEP 4:
Identify what the speaker knows.

STEP 5:
Identify what the speaker wants.

STEP 6:
Identify emotional state.

STEP 7:
Identify immediate objective.

STEP 8:
Apply character vocabulary fingerprint.

STEP 9:
Apply character sentence fingerprint.

STEP 10:
Apply relationship modulation.

STEP 11:
Apply emotional transformation.

STEP 12:
Apply scene context.

STEP 13:
Check source evidence.

STEP 14:
Check prohibited behavior.

STEP 15:
Check collision with other characters.

STEP 16:
Check continuity with previous scenes.

STEP 17:
Check performability.

Only then is the line eligible for screenplay approval.

---

## 65. FINAL CHARACTER VOICE GATE

A dialogue line passes only if:

[ ] Character psychology is visible
[ ] Character vocabulary is appropriate
[ ] Sentence behavior is appropriate
[ ] Relationship is reflected
[ ] Emotional state modifies the line appropriately
[ ] Character knowledge is respected
[ ] Character objective is clear
[ ] Character would plausibly say it
[ ] It does not sound interchangeable with another character
[ ] It does not rely on generic mythological phrasing
[ ] It does not overuse philosophy
[ ] It does not overuse poetry
[ ] It does not overuse exposition
[ ] It does not introduce modern slang
[ ] It does not introduce unsupported vulgarity
[ ] It does not invent canonical quotations
[ ] It respects source evidence
[ ] It respects established continuity
[ ] It is performable
[ ] Its adaptation status is known

Failure of any critical item = REVIEW_REQUIRED.

---

## 66. FINAL PRINCIPLE

The objective is not:

"Make everyone sound ancient."

The objective is not:

"Make everyone sound intelligent."

The objective is not:

"Make everyone sound poetic."

The objective is:

MAKE EVERY CHARACTER THINK DIFFERENTLY,
NOTICE DIFFERENT THINGS,
WANT DIFFERENT THINGS,
REACT DIFFERENTLY,
CHOOSE DIFFERENT WORDS,
BUILD SENTENCES DIFFERENTLY,
HANDLE CONFLICT DIFFERENTLY,
SHOW AFFECTION DIFFERENTLY,
SHOW ANGER DIFFERENTLY,
USE SILENCE DIFFERENTLY,
AND SPEAK DIFFERENTLY TO DIFFERENT PEOPLE.

If twelve characters can exchange their dialogue without the audience
noticing, the character-language system has failed.

## FINAL SIX-CHARACTER COLLISION CHECK

RAMA:
humanized dharma + compassion + restraint

RATI:
emotional perception + attachment + concern

SATI:
devotion + family conflict + dignity + emotional rupture

SHIVA:
detachment + insight + compassion + silence

SITA:
dignity + devotion + moral clarity + strength

VISHNU:
cosmic purpose + preservation + divine certainty

The six must not converge into one generic elevated voice.

## REQUIRED COLLISION QUESTIONS

For every important scene ask:

1. Who notices the problem first?
2. Who asks about it?
3. Who explains it?
4. Who judges it?
5. Who feels it personally?
6. Who reacts immediately?
7. Who remains silent?
8. Who seeks reassurance?
9. Who seeks truth?
10. Who seeks action?
11. Who seeks cosmic understanding?
12. Who protects?
13. Who challenges?
14. Who accepts?
15. Who refuses?

The answers should differ by character.

## FINAL CHARACTER-SWAP TEST

Take a line from each character.

Replace the speaker's name with another character.

If the line still sounds natural for the other character without meaningful
revision, the line has failed the fingerprint test.
