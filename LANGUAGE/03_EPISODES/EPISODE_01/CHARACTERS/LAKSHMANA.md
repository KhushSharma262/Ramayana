# LAKSHMANA — EPISODE 01 DIALOGUE PROFILE

## 1. DIALOGUE IDENTITY

Lakshmana's dialogue is driven by:
- fierce loyalty
- protectiveness
- duty
- courage
- emotional intensity
- readiness to act
- sensitivity to injustice

He is respectful toward those he reveres, but he is not linguistically passive.

## 2. BEHAVIORAL CORE

Lakshmana:
- reacts strongly to perceived injustice
- defends Rama intensely
- values action
- can become angry quickly
- speaks more forcefully under pressure
- can challenge another person's reasoning
- is capable of sophisticated argument
- becomes less restrained when someone threatens or dishonors Rama/Sita

Valmiki material gives Lakshmana forceful argumentative speech, including
extended reasoning about dharma and prosperity, while other episodes show
his anger becoming a significant dramatic force. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

## 3. VOCABULARY FINGERPRINT

Preferred semantic fields:
- duty
- brother
- protection
- injustice
- danger
- honor
- action
- enemy
- courage
- promise
- loyalty
- insult
- defense
- consequence
- readiness

He gravitates toward ACTION WORDS.

Examples of semantic behavior:
- "What must be done?"
- "Who has wronged him?"
- "How can this be permitted?"
- "I will..."
- "This cannot be allowed..."

These are behavioral patterns, not canonical quotations.

## 4. SENTENCE FINGERPRINT

Calm:
- medium length
- direct
- respectful

Concerned:
- more questions
- faster reasoning

Angry:
- shorter
- sharper
- more declarative
- stronger verbs
- less diplomatic

Protective:
- immediate
- direct
- action-oriented

## 5. ANGER

Lakshmana is one of the characters where anger must visibly affect dialogue.

Anger should produce:
- reduced patience
- stronger directness
- sharper vocabulary
- rhetorical challenges
- forceful statements

But anger does NOT automatically mean:
- profanity
- vulgarity
- modern abuse

## 6. INSULT STYLE

When genuinely provoked, Lakshmana may attack:
- cowardice
- injustice
- betrayal
- dishonor
- failure of duty

His insults should be morally charged rather than random.

## 7. ARGUMENT STYLE

Lakshmana can argue.

His pattern may be:

claim
→ challenge
→ reasoning
→ consequence
→ action

He should not sound like an unintelligent hothead.

## 8. AFFECTION

Lakshmana's affection is often expressed through:
- protection
- service
- concern
- readiness
- sacrifice

He may not need sentimental vocabulary to communicate love.

## 9. HUMILITY

Toward Rama, elders and respected figures, his language can become markedly
more controlled.

This creates an important contrast:

Rama-related respect:
controlled devotion

Perceived injustice:
forceful confrontation

## 10. WHAT LAKSHMANA DOES NOT SOUND LIKE

Do NOT make him:
- permanently angry
- crude
- unintelligent
- constantly shouting
- submissive to everyone
- sarcastic in every line
- modern
- slang-heavy

## 11. CONTRAST

Lakshmana ≠ Daksha.

Lakshmana's harshness protects.

Daksha's harshness judges.

Lakshmana's vocabulary:
duty / danger / defense / loyalty

Daksha's vocabulary:
status / propriety / authority / conduct

## 12. CANON / ADAPTATION STATUS

Core loyalty, courage, anger and argumentative ability are source-supported.

Exact recurring vocabulary is a production characterization unless directly
supported by a specific passage.

## 13. AI GENERATION RULE

Before writing Lakshmana, ask:

"What is he protecting?"

If nothing is being protected, challenged or defended, reduce his aggressive
register.
