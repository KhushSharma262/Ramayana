# RATI — EPISODE 01 DIALOGUE PROFILE

## 1. CORE DIALOGUE IDENTITY

Rati's speech is shaped by:
- emotional intelligence
- attachment
- sensitivity
- affection
- perception of desire
- concern for Kāmadeva
- vulnerability
- dignity

She must not be reduced to "the beautiful wife of Kāmadeva."

Her dialogue should establish her as an emotionally perceptive individual.

---

## 2. BEHAVIORAL ENGINE

Rati naturally notices:
- emotional changes
- danger
- attraction
- Kāmadeva's confidence
- consequences of reckless action

Her dialogue can therefore contain warnings and emotional foresight.

She may understand a danger before Kāmadeva accepts it.

---

## 3. VOCABULARY FINGERPRINT

Primary semantic fields:
- affection
- husband
- love
- desire
- longing
- beauty
- fear
- danger
- separation
- union
- concern
- emotional consequence
- memory
- devotion
- reassurance
- loss

Her vocabulary should be emotionally precise rather than intellectually
abstract.

---

## 4. SENTENCE FINGERPRINT

Normal:
- graceful
- moderate length
- emotionally aware
- conversational

Concerned:
- more questions
- direct warnings
- shorter clauses

Fearful:
- hesitation
- repetition
- names/relational terms
- emotionally exposed language

Grieving:
- reduced ornament
- simpler statements
- repetition where psychologically natural

---

## 5. RELATIONSHIP WITH KAMADEVA

Rati should not simply agree with him.

She may:
- encourage
- tease
- warn
- question
- reassure
- challenge
- fear for him

Her affection does not remove her judgment.

---

## 6. PLAYFULNESS

Rati can participate in Kāmadeva's playful energy, but her playfulness
should be more emotionally perceptive.

Kāmadeva:
confidence and provocation

Rati:
affection and perception

She should not become a second Kāmadeva.

---

## 7. CONFLICT

When she disagrees:

concern
→ explanation
→ warning
→ emotional appeal

rather than:
insult
→ domination
→ humiliation

If deeply hurt, she may become more direct.

---

## 8. EMOTIONAL VOCABULARY

Strongest emotional fields:
- attachment
- anxiety
- longing
- tenderness
- fear of loss
- devotion
- grief
- relief

Her emotional vocabulary should be specific rather than endlessly saying
"मैं चिंतित हूँ" or "मुझे दुख है."

---

## 9. RUDE LANGUAGE

Normally low.

If angry, she can become sharp because of hurt rather than status aggression.

Avoid:
- vulgarity
- modern insults
- sexualized slang
- modern romantic clichés

---

## 10. SILENCE

Rati may communicate emotional realization through silence.

Do not fill every emotional beat with dialogue.

---

## 11. WHAT RATI MUST NOT SOUND LIKE

Do not make her:
- a generic romantic heroine
- constantly seductive
- helpless
- childish
- permanently crying
- vulgar
- modern-flirtatious
- interchangeable with Parvati
- interchangeable with Maina

---

## 12. DISTINCTIVE CONTRAST

Rati:
EMOTIONAL PERCEPTION + ATTACHMENT + CONCERN

Parvati:
SPIRITUAL INQUIRY + DEVOTION + DETERMINATION

Maina:
MATERNAL PROTECTION + FAMILY CONCERN

Sita:
DIGNIFIED DEVOTION + MORAL CLARITY + EMOTIONAL STRENGTH

---

## 13. CANON / ADAPTATION

Rati's relationship with Kāmadeva and her emotional role are grounded in
traditional material.

Specific vocabulary habits are production characterization unless directly
supported by source text.

---

## 14. FINAL TEST

Before writing Rati, identify:
"What does she perceive emotionally that the other character may be missing?"

That perception should often shape her dialogue.

# DIALOGUE FINGERPRINT HARDENING — RATI

## CHARACTER-SPECIFIC HARD OVERRIDES

Rati's dialogue must prioritize:

ATTACHMENT
→ PERCEPTION
→ CONCERN
→ EMOTIONAL CONSEQUENCE

She should notice emotional danger before merely discussing abstract
principles.

### RATI WORD PRIORITY

HIGH:
affection, husband, danger, fear, longing, loss, concern, beauty,
desire, separation, reassurance, consequence

MEDIUM:
duty, destiny, divine matters, social status

LOW:
cosmic abstraction, institutional authority, impersonal philosophical
discourse

### RATI EMOTIONAL TRANSFORMATION

CALM:
warm + observant

CONCERN:
questioning + warning

FEAR:
shorter + more personal

ANGER:
hurt + directness

GRIEF:
simplified + repetitive + emotionally exposed

RELIEF:
warmer + softened

### RATI CONFLICT RULE

Rati should usually attack the CONSEQUENCE of an action rather than
establishing social dominance.

### RATI NEVER-SOUND-LIKE RULE

Do not give Rati:
- Daksha's status vocabulary
- Narada's probing style
- Parvati's philosophical inquiry
- Lakshmana's martial urgency
- Kāmadeva's confident seduction

Her distinguishing center is emotional perception.

### RATI SUBTEXT

Her spoken question may conceal:
fear of losing Kāmadeva,
fear of consequence,
desire for reassurance,
or recognition that he is taking a dangerous risk.

Do not make every concern explicit.

### RATI FINAL TEST

If Rati's line could be spoken by Maina without changing its emotional
meaning, rewrite it to restore her spousal/emotional perspective.
