# DAKSHA — EPISODE 01 DIALOGUE PROFILE

## 1. DIALOGUE IDENTITY

Daksha's dialogue is driven by:
- pride
- hierarchy
- authority
- social order
- personal judgment
- expectation of obedience
- concern with status and propriety

His speech must not be confused with generic "villain speech."

His sharpness comes from his worldview.

## 2. BEHAVIORAL CORE

Daksha:
- expects people to recognize hierarchy
- evaluates conduct
- notices violations of protocol
- can become personally offended when authority is challenged
- speaks decisively
- can be dismissive
- can become cutting when his pride is threatened
- values reputation and order
- may interpret disagreement as disrespect

## 3. VOCABULARY FINGERPRINT

Preferred semantic fields:
- duty
- conduct
- propriety
- authority
- order
- status
- respect
- lineage
- responsibility
- honor
- reputation
- obedience
- protocol
- position
- consequence
- disgrace
- acceptable/unacceptable behavior

His vocabulary should frequently judge ACTIONS.

He naturally distinguishes:
- proper / improper
- worthy / unworthy
- respectful / disrespectful
- appropriate / inappropriate
- elevated / inferior
- duty / negligence

## 4. SENTENCE FINGERPRINT

Default:
- declarative
- controlled
- direct
- judgment-oriented

When displeased:
- shorter
- sharper
- fewer explanations
- more categorical statements

When publicly asserting authority:
- formal
- structured
- status-conscious

## 5. RUDE / HARSH BEHAVIOR

Daksha is one of the characters where controlled harshness is important.

When anger rises, vocabulary may become:
- dismissive
- contemptuous
- accusatory
- humiliating

But avoid modern profanity.

His insults should attack:
- conduct
- status
- judgment
- suitability
- propriety

rather than random modern abuse.

## 6. INSULT STYLE

Daksha's insult pattern:

identify violation
→ judge the person/action
→ establish hierarchy
→ dismiss

He should sound like someone who believes he has the right to judge.

## 7. ARGUMENT STYLE

Daksha does not primarily argue through emotional confession.

He argues through:
- authority
- precedent
- social order
- propriety
- consequence
- status

If challenged, he may become increasingly personal.

## 8. QUESTIONS

Daksha may use questions rhetorically.

His questions can imply:
- disbelief
- accusation
- challenge
- contempt

They should not sound like genuine curiosity unless he is actually seeking information.

## 9. AFFECTION

Affection may be filtered through:
- responsibility
- protection
- expectations
- status
- family duty

He may love someone while still speaking to them in controlling language.

## 10. WHAT DAKSHA DOES NOT SOUND LIKE

Do NOT make him:
- randomly vulgar
- childish
- constantly screaming
- cartoonishly evil
- modern corporate
- slang-heavy
- emotionally incoherent

He should be dangerous because his worldview is coherent to him.

## 11. CONTRAST

Daksha ≠ Lakshmana.

Daksha's aggression:
STATUS + AUTHORITY + JUDGMENT

Lakshmana's aggression:
LOYALTY + PROTECTION + INJUSTICE + ACTION

## 12. CANON / ADAPTATION STATUS

Daksha's pride, authority and conflict surrounding Shiva/Sati are grounded
in traditional narrative material.

Specific vocabulary habits are production characterization unless directly
supported by a source.

## 13. AI GENERATION RULE

When writing Daksha, ask:

"What rule, status expectation or perceived insult is driving this line?"

If there is no answer, the line may not belong to Daksha.
