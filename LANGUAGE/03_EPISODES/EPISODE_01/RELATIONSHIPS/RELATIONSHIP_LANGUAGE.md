# RELATIONSHIP LANGUAGE — EPISODE 01

## SYSTEM IDENTITY

system_name:
  RELATIONSHIP_LANGUAGE

system_scope:
  Episode 01 relationship-specific dialogue behavior

system_purpose:
  Define how a relationship between two characters changes the way they speak to,
  respond to, question, command, comfort, challenge, interrupt, defer to,
  disagree with, or remain silent around one another.

This system governs relational dialogue behavior.

It does NOT replace:

- LANGUAGE_SYSTEM.md
- HINDI.md
- SANSKRIT.md
- AWADHI.md
- PRONUNCIATION.md
- CHARACTER_LANGUAGE.md
- HONORIFICS_AND_ADDRESS.md
- individual character dialogue profiles
- CANON
- CHARACTER identity
- ACTING
- AUDIO
- SCENE direction

Relationship language is an interaction layer between two established character
profiles.

A character must never lose their own established voice merely because they are
speaking to a different person.

character_identity

Therefore:

CHARACTER VOICE ≠ RELATIONSHIP VOICE

Relationship language modifies a character's established voice.

It does not create a generic third voice shared by both characters.

Example:

Rama speaking to Lakshmana must still sound like Rama.

Lakshmana speaking to Rama must still sound like Lakshmana.

The relationship changes:

- degree of formality
- directness
- concern
- restraint
- affection
- questioning
- disagreement
- obedience
- authority
- vulnerability
- address
- silence
- emotional openness

It does not erase either character's individual dialogue fingerprint.

# 03. RELATIONSHIP IS BIDIRECTIONAL

Every relationship must be modeled separately in both directions.

A → B

and

B → A

must never automatically receive identical dialogue rules.

Example:

Rama → Lakshmana

may involve:

- elder-brother responsibility
- affection
- protective concern
- calm authority
- reassurance
- correction

Lakshmana → Rama

may involve:

- reverence
- loyalty
- protective urgency
- emotional openness
- deference
- direct objection when duty or safety demands it

The same relationship therefore produces two different speech behaviors.

# 07. SENTENCE BEHAVIOR

Relationship affects sentence construction.

Close relationships may permit:

- shorter exchanges
- implied meaning
- incomplete sentences
- meaningful silence
- fewer explanations
- emotionally compressed speech

Formal relationships may produce:

- complete sentences
- explicit respect
- careful requests
- indirect disagreement
- formal reasoning

Conflict may produce:

- shorter sentences
- repetition
- direct questions
- interrupted reasoning
- sharper statements

But these patterns must remain compatible with the speaker's individual
character profile.

# 11. CONFLICT ESCALATION

Conflict should develop through stages rather than immediately becoming shouting.

Recommended model:

1. discomfort
2. hesitation
3. questioning
4. clarification
5. disagreement
6. stronger objection
7. emotional disclosure
8. accusation
9. confrontation
10. rupture or resolution

Not every relationship must use every stage.

Character identity determines escalation speed.

- sentence length
- directness
- repetition
- address frequency
- silence
- vocabulary
- interruption
- willingness to explain

But anger must not randomly produce modern insults.

Insults must follow the character's established vocabulary and social identity.

# 54. FINAL PRINCIPLE

Character language answers:

"How does this person speak?"

Relationship language answers:

"How does this person speak to this particular person?"

Honorifics and address answer:

"How does this person address this particular person?"

Scene context answers:

"Why are they speaking this way right now?"

Emotion answers:

"What has changed inside them at this moment?"

Canon answers:

"What is actually supported by the source?"

All five layers must remain distinct.

The goal is not merely correct dialogue.

The goal is dialogue in which the audience can perceive:

WHO is speaking,
WHO they are speaking to,
WHAT their relationship is,
WHAT has happened between them,
WHAT they currently feel,
WHAT they know,
WHAT they want,
WHAT they are unwilling to say,
and WHY they chose those exact words.

# 56. RELATIONSHIP FACT VS CHARACTER PERCEPTION

Three different layers must remain separate:

OBJECTIVE_RELATIONSHIP

What the project establishes the relationship to be.

CHARACTER_PERCEPTION

What character A believes the relationship to be.

OTHER_CHARACTER_PERCEPTION

What character B believes the relationship to be.

These may differ.

Example:

A may believe:
"we are trusted allies"

while B may believe:
"we are only bound by duty."

Dialogue must reflect the speaker's perception where appropriate.

Never convert one character's perception into objective relationship fact.

# 70. RELATIONSHIP VS ADDRESS CONFLICT

# 72. RELATIONSHIP SYMMETRY FAILURE TEST

Never assume:

if A loves B,
then B loves A.

if A trusts B,
then B trusts A.

if A respects B,
then B respects A.

if A fears B,
then B fears A.

if A obeys B,
then B commands A.

if A feels close to B,
then B feels close to A.

Each direction must be independently established.

# 73. EMOTIONAL ASYMMETRY

Relationships may contain unequal emotional investment.

Possible:

A deeply attached → B emotionally reserved.

A trusts → B doubts.

A fears losing B → B does not recognize the attachment.

A seeks reconciliation → B refuses.

Such asymmetry is legitimate and must not be normalized away.

# 79. RELATIONSHIP GENERATION ORDER

Generate relational dialogue in this order:

1. What does the speaker want from the listener?
2. What does the speaker believe about the listener?
3. What does the speaker know?
4. What does the speaker not know?
5. What is the relationship currently?
6. What is the relationship history?
7. What authority exists?
8. What emotional state exists?
9. What cannot be openly said?
10. What would this speaker naturally say?
11. How would this listener naturally respond?
12. What remains unsaid?

This prevents generic relationship dialogue.

# 80. RELATIONSHIP COLLISION TEST

Take one line from a relationship scene and replace the listener.

If the line remains equally natural:

RELATIONAL_SPECIFICITY:

LOW

If the line changes meaning significantly:

RELATIONAL_SPECIFICITY:

HIGH

Important dialogue should generally have sufficient relational specificity.

This does not mean every line must explicitly mention the relationship.

---

# 81. LISTENER RESPONSE TEST

Every significant line should be tested against the listener.

Ask:

Would this listener interpret the line correctly?

Would this listener misunderstand it?

Would this listener become defensive?

Would this listener become reassured?

Would this listener remain silent?

Would this listener challenge it?

The response must arise from the listener's character and relationship state.

# 85. RELATIONSHIP REPETITION CONTROL

# 88. RELATIONSHIP QA SEVERITY

CRITICAL:

- impossible relationship state
- wrong relationship direction
- character knows impossible information
- relationship contradicts established canon
- major continuity break

HIGH:

- wrong authority
- wrong emotional state
- wrong address caused by relationship error
- major trust/affection error
- unexplained relationship transition

MEDIUM:

- weak relational specificity
- repeated relational vocabulary

LOW:

- minor phrasing inconsistency
- minor relational subtlety loss

# 92. MASTER RULE

A relationship is not merely:

"who these two characters are to each other."

It is a continuously tracked system of:

history
+
perception
+
knowledge
+
trust
+
authority
+
affection
+
conflict
+
obligation
+
public behavior
+
private behavior
+
memory
+
current circumstance.

The final dialogue must emerge from that complete state.

The system must never generate:

"how people in this type of relationship usually talk."

It must generate:

"how THIS CHARACTER, with THIS history, THIS knowledge, THIS emotional state,
THIS social position, and THIS exact relationship with THIS OTHER CHARACTER,
would speak to THIS person in THIS moment."

That distinction is mandatory for Episode 01.
