# DIALOGUE

## SYSTEM IDENTITY

system_name:
  DIALOGUE

system_scope:
  Master production system for creating, adapting, validating, and approving
  dialogue for the RAMAYAN project.

system_level:
  SCRIPT_LANGUAGE

primary_language:
  Hindi

secondary_languages:
  Awadhi
  Sanskrit

primary_source_policy:
  Ramcharitmanas-first

purpose:
  Convert approved source material, character identity, relationship state,
  scene context, emotional state, and production requirements into believable,
  culturally appropriate, character-specific, canon-traceable spoken dialogue.

+

+

+
EMOTION
+

+

+

+

+
CONTINUITY

A line must never exist merely because it:

sounds poetic
sounds ancient
sounds dramatic
sounds divine
sounds cinematic
sounds philosophical
sounds emotional

# 03. DIALOGUE PROVENANCE

Every line must belong to one of:

DIRECT_CANONICAL_QUOTATION
TRANSLATED_CANONICAL_QUOTATION
SOURCE_DERIVED_DIALOGUE
ADAPTED_CANONICAL_DIALOGUE
NEW_CONNECTIVE_DIALOGUE
DIRECTORIAL_DIALOGUE
PRODUCTION_CREATED_DIALOGUE

Never allow an unclassified line into the final screenplay.

---

# 04. CANONICAL QUOTATION RULE

A line may be called:

DIRECT_CANONICAL_QUOTATION

only when:

exact source text is verified
AND
source location is verified
AND
speaker attribution is verified.

Otherwise it must receive another classification.

---

# 05. NO INVENTED CANON

Never present:

AI-written dialogue
writer-written dialogue
director-written dialogue
translation
interpretation
adaptation

as canonical quotation without independent verification.

---

# 06. CHARACTER VOICE PRIORITY

Every speaker must retain the approved character profile.

Character voice includes:

vocabulary
sentence structure
question style
command style
argument style
emotional reaction
rudeness
affection
silence
interruption
information release
humor
moral framing
knowledge boundaries

Relationship changes the expression.

It does not replace the character.

---

# 07. RELATIONSHIP PRIORITY

The dialogue must use the approved relationship state.

Check:

authority
affection
trust
distance
vulnerability
conflict
history
public/private state
knowledge asymmetry

Do not assume emotional symmetry.

---

# 08. KNOWLEDGE CONTROL

Before writing a line determine:

what speaker knows
what speaker believes
what speaker does not know
what listener knows
what listener believes
what audience knows

Never give a character information they have no established access to.

---

# 09. OBJECTIVE CONTROL

Every important exchange should have a speaker objective.

Possible objectives:

ask
answer
warn
persuade
comfort
challenge
command
refuse
reassure
confess
conceal
test
correct
protect
teach
seek_permission
seek_information
express_grief
express_devotion
express_affection
establish_boundary
reconcile
separate

One line may have multiple functions.

---

# 10. LISTENER EFFECT

A line should produce or attempt an effect.

Possible effects:

reassurance
fear
confusion
obedience
resistance
reflection
anger
comfort
trust
distrust
disclosure
silence
action
inaction

The listener's response must be compatible with their character.

---

# 11. DIALOGUE IS INTERACTION

Do not write dialogue as independent speeches.

Model:

speaker
→
listener
→
response
→
new state

Each important exchange may alter:

knowledge
emotion
relationship
decision
scene direction

---

# 12. QUESTION DESIGN

Questions must reflect:

speaker personality
knowledge
relationship
objective
emotional state

Do not ask questions solely to explain information to the audience.

---

# 13. ANSWER DESIGN

Answers should address what was actually asked.

Characters may:

avoid
deflect
misunderstand
refuse
answer partially
answer indirectly

only when consistent with their character and objective.

---

# 14. COMMAND DESIGN

Commands must reflect:

authority
urgency
relationship
speaker personality

A command may be:

direct
formal
protective
urgent
gentle
severe
ritual
military

Do not make every command harsh.

---

# 15. REQUEST DESIGN

Requests must reflect:

relative authority
relationship
urgency
vulnerability
context

Do not make every request overly polite or artificially elaborate.

---

# 16. DISAGREEMENT

Disagreement must have a reason.

Possible:

moral
personal
familial
political
strategic
emotional
protective
religious
duty-based

Do not create arguments merely for dramatic intensity.

---

# 17. CONFLICT ESCALATION

Possible progression:

hesitation
question
clarification
objection
stronger objection
emotional disclosure
accusation
confrontation
rupture
resolution

The character determines escalation speed.

---

# 18. ANGER

Anger may alter:

sentence length
directness
vocabulary
repetition
silence
interruption
address
explanation

Anger does not automatically remove respect.

---

# 19. GRIEF

Grief may produce:

short speech
silence
repetition
fragmentation
difficulty answering
memory
withdrawal
direct emotional expression

Do not force eloquent grief.

---

# 20. FEAR

Fear may produce:

questions
hesitation
warnings
short sentences
reassurance-seeking
silence
avoidance

Fear does not automatically make every character weak.

---

# 21. AFFECTION

Affection may be expressed through:

concern
reassurance
attention
protection
patience
remembering
gentle correction
silence
sacrifice

Do not force verbal declarations.

---

# 22. DEVOTION

Devotion must remain character-specific.

Do not make every devotional character:

constantly praising
constantly poetic
emotionally identical

Devotion may appear through:

obedience
service
trust
questioning
sacrifice
faith
silence
concern

---

# 23. SILENCE

Silence is a valid dialogue event.

Possible meaning:

thought
respect
grief
anger
fear
devotion
intimacy
rejection
shock
acceptance

Record its intended function when important.

---

# 24. INTERRUPTION

Interruption requires:

speaker
reason
urgency
relationship
social permission
emotional state

Do not add interruptions merely to make dialogue cinematic.

---

# 25. REPETITION

Repetition is allowed when it serves:

emotion
emphasis
ritual
memory
warning
escalation
poetry
canonical structure

Otherwise remove repetitive wording.

---

# 26. NATURAL HUMAN SPEECH

Dialogue should permit:

pauses
breath
short responses
incomplete thoughts
self-correction
silence
response delay

But avoid:

modern filler
slang
internet speech
Gen-Z speech
artificial casualness

---

# 27. NO GENERIC MYTHOLOGICAL DIALOGUE

Reject lines that could be spoken by almost any character.

Examples of risk:

generic destiny statements
generic wisdom
generic divine proclamations
generic moral speeches
generic praise
generic emotional declarations

The line must belong to this speaker.

---

# 28. NO CONSTANT PHILOSOPHY

Even philosophical characters can discuss:

family
travel
food
danger
practical tasks
ritual
health
immediate concerns

Do not make every conversation philosophical.

---

# 29. NO CONSTANT POETRY

Poetry must be justified by:

source
emotion
character
ritual
narrative function

New dialogue should not become poetic merely because the story is mythological.

---

# 30. NO CONSTANT ELOQUENCE

Allow ordinary human speech.

Every sentence does not need to be profound.

---

# 31. EXPOSITION CONTROL

Never make characters explain information they already know solely for the
audience.

Use:

question
revelation
narration
memory
confession
instruction
natural discussion

where appropriate.

---

# 32. INFORMATION RELEASE

Information should be released according to:

character knowledge
character objective
scene purpose
relationship
dramatic timing

Never reveal information merely because the script writer needs the audience
to know it.

---

# 33. SUBTEXT

Subtext may exist beneath:

concern
anger
fear
affection
respect
hurt
duty
jealousy
devotion

Do not force subtext into every line.

---

# 34. PUBLIC VS PRIVATE

Dialogue must account for who is present.

The same character may speak differently:

alone
with family
before subjects
before sages
during ritual
during crisis
before a deity
during grief

Do not automatically make private speech casual.

---

# 35. ADDRESS

Address must follow:

HONORIFICS_AND_ADDRESS.md

Dialogue writers must not independently invent:

pronouns
titles
epithets
vocatives
kinship terms

---

# 36. LANGUAGE

Language must follow:

HINDI.md
AWADHI.md
SANSKRIT.md

Do not insert Awadhi or Sanskrit merely to make dialogue sound ancient.

---

# 37. TRANSLATION

Source-derived dialogue must follow:

TRANSLATION_AND_ADAPTATION.md

Never rewrite source meaning merely to make a line sound cinematic.

# 38. SOURCE ADAPTATION

record:

---

# 39. CHARACTER-SPECIFIC WORD CHOICE

Each character must have:

preferred vocabulary
conditional vocabulary
avoided vocabulary

Word choice must remain consistent.

---

# 40. VOCABULARY COLLISION TEST

If two unrelated characters repeatedly use the same unusual expressions:

flag:

CHARACTER_VOCABULARY_COLLISION

unless source or context justifies it.

---

# 41. RELATIONSHIP COLLISION TEST

If the same dialogue would sound equally appropriate when the listener changes:

check whether relational specificity is too weak.

---

# 42. MODERNISM FILTER

Reject unjustified:

slang
internet language
modern dating language
corporate vocabulary
social-media expressions
Gen-Z expressions
modern therapeutic terminology
unnecessary English
modern profanity

---

# 43. CULTURAL DIGNITY

No:

mockery
vulgarity
forced humor
casual sacred terminology
modern romantic clichés
cheap melodrama

unless explicitly justified by source and context.

---

# 44. HUMOR

Humor must arise naturally from:

character
relationship
situation

Do not insert jokes into serious scenes merely for pacing.

---

# 45. INSULTS

Insults must reflect the speaker.

Record:

target
reason
intensity
relationship
public/private context

No generic modern abuse.

---

# 46. CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE FIREWALL

No character may:

predict unestablished events
know another character's private thoughts
know unseen events
quote unknown conversations
recognize unknown information

unless source or approved supernatural mechanism supports it.

---

# 47. DIVINE KNOWLEDGE FIREWALL

Divine status does not automatically justify every knowledge claim.

The specific knowledge must be supported by:

source
approved characterization
or explicit project decision.

---

# 48. DIALOGUE LENGTH

Sentence and exchange length must reflect:

emotion
character
scene purpose
relationship
source form

Do not impose a universal sentence length.

---

# 49. MONOLOGUE CONTROL

A long speech requires justification.

Possible reasons:

source quotation
ritual
teaching
narration
public declaration
confession
formal address
dramatic necessity

Otherwise divide into interaction.

---

# 50. INFORMATION DUMP TEST

If a speech explains:

history
worldbuilding
relationship
theology
character motivation

ask whether the information can naturally emerge through interaction.

---

# 51. DIALOGUE CONTINUITY

Before writing a scene load:

previous scene
current relationship
current emotional state
known information
unresolved conflicts
promises
previous address
previous terminology

A character cannot reset between scenes.

---

# 52. RELATIONSHIP MEMORY

Dialogue must preserve:

previous conflicts
previous promises
previous kindness
previous warnings
previous revelations
previous wounds
previous reconciliations

---

# 53. SCENE OBJECTIVE

Every dialogue scene must define:

scene_purpose:
primary_conflict:
primary_information:
relationship_change:
emotional_change:
decision_change:

---

# 54. DIALOGUE BEAT STRUCTURE

A scene may contain:

opening state
trigger
response
escalation
turn
decision
aftermath

Not every scene requires all seven.

---

# 55. DIALOGUE TURN

A turn is a moment when:

knowledge changes
emotion changes
relationship changes
decision changes
power changes
objective changes

Important scenes should contain meaningful turns.

---

# 56. RESPONSE VALIDITY

Every important response must be evaluated:

Would this listener naturally respond this way?

Check:

personality
knowledge
emotion
relationship
status
objective

---

# 57. NO AUTOMATIC EMOTIONAL MIRRORING

If A is angry, B need not be angry.

If A cries, B need not cry.

If A loves, B need not reciprocate.

If A fears, B may respond differently.

---

# 58. NO AUTOMATIC RESOLUTION

Conflict may end with:

silence
separation
partial understanding
continued conflict
deferred resolution
reconciliation
irreversible rupture

Do not force closure.

---

# 59. CANON VS NEW DIALOGUE

Source-derived dialogue must remain traceable.

New dialogue must be clearly labelled.

Never blur the two during screenplay preparation.

---

# 60. DIALOGUE GENERATION ORDER

1. Identify source.
2. Identify speaker.
3. Identify listener.
4. Identify relationship.
5. Identify scene.
6. Identify knowledge.
7. Identify objective.
8. Identify emotion.
9. Identify register.
10. Select language.
11. Select vocabulary.
12. Select address.
13. Draft.
14. Check naturalness.
15. Check character voice.
16. Check relationship.
17. Check canon.
18. Check continuity.
19. Check pronunciation.
20. Check adaptation status.
21. Check subtitles.
22. Approve.

# 63. AUTOMATIC REJECTION

Reject dialogue if:

speaker identity is wrong

OR
character knowledge is impossible

OR
source attribution is false

OR
adaptation is disguised as canon

OR
relationship is wrong

OR
address is unsupported

OR
language is unjustified

OR
meaning is materially changed without approval

OR
dialogue sounds interchangeable between characters

OR
modern language is unjustified

OR
sacred content is fabricated.

---
