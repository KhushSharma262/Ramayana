# NARRATION

## SYSTEM IDENTITY

system_name:
  NARRATION

system_scope:
  Master narration architecture and production rules for the RAMAYAN project.

system_level:
  SCRIPT_LANGUAGE

purpose:
  Control narrator identity, narrative perspective, source fidelity, narration
  handoffs, knowledge boundaries, narration language, adaptation, continuity,
  and performance text.

IMPORTANT:

This file defines HOW narration is written.

It does NOT write Episode 01 narration.

---

# 01. NARRATION ARCHITECTURE

The project-approved Episode 01 narration architecture is:

TULSIDAS
→
SHIVA–SATI–PARVATI STORY
→
SHIVA + PARVATI
→
TULSIDAS HANDOFF
→
SHIVA
→
RAMA-KATHA

This architecture is fixed unless explicitly changed through project change
control.

---

---

# 03. TULSIDAS NARRATOR

Where Tulsidas narrates:

the narration must remain clearly attributable to Tulsidas.

Do not accidentally make it:

modern documentary narration
anonymous omniscient narration
Shiva narration
character dialogue

unless explicitly approved.

---

# 04. SHIVA AS IN-WORLD NARRATOR

When Shiva narrates to Parvati:

Shiva remains:

a character
+
a husband
+
a speaker
+
an in-world narrator

His narration must remain compatible with:

his character voice
his knowledge
his relationship with Parvati
the approved source
the narrative context

He must not suddenly sound like an external narrator.

---

# 05. NARRATOR KNOWLEDGE

Every narrator has a knowledge scope.

Possible:

LIMITED
CHARACTER_BASED
SOURCE_BASED
OMNISCIENT
DIVINE
APPROVED_PROJECT_SPECIFIC

Do not assume omniscience without approval.

---

# 06. KNOWLEDGE FIREWALL

Narration must not reveal:

private thoughts
future events
unseen events
unknown motives
secret conversations

unless the approved narrator has access to them.

---

# 07. NARRATOR PERSPECTIVE

Narration must identify whether it is:

external
character_based
in_world
retrospective
devotional
descriptive
transitional

Do not mix perspectives without a controlled transition.

---

# 08. NARRATION SOURCE STATUS

Every important narration unit must be:

DIRECT_CANONICAL_NARRATION
TRANSLATED_CANONICAL_NARRATION
SOURCE_DERIVED_NARRATION
ADAPTED_NARRATION
NEW_CONNECTIVE_NARRATION
DIRECTORIAL_NARRATION

Never present new narration as canonical.

---

# 10. NARRATION VS DIALOGUE

Narration must provide information that naturally belongs to narration.

Dialogue must contain information characters would naturally speak.

Do not use narration to avoid writing dialogue.

Do not use dialogue to force exposition.

---

# 11. NARRATION PURPOSE

Every narration unit should serve one or more:

transition
description
context
time_shift
location_shift
character_introduction
event_connection
source_preservation
emotional framing
foreshadowing
storytelling
ritual framing
scene closure

---

# 12. NARRATION EXCESS

Avoid narration that merely describes what the audience can already see.

Narration should add:

context
meaning
perspective
history
transition
source-derived information

when required.

---

# 13. NARRATION RESTRAINT

Do not narrate:

every emotion
every movement
every visual detail
every obvious action

Visual storytelling belongs to the visual systems.

---

# 14. NO CINEMATIC TRAILER NARRATION

Avoid generic:

"और फिर..."
"भाग्य ने..."
"समय ने..."
"एक नई कहानी शुरू हुई..."

unless genuinely appropriate.

Narration must have specific informational or narrative purpose.

---

# 15. NO GENERIC WISDOM NARRATION

Do not add philosophical commentary merely because the story is mythological.

Any philosophical narration must have:

source
narrator
context
purpose

---

# 16. NARRATION LANGUAGE

Primary narration language:

Hindi

Awadhi/Sanskrit only when justified by:

source
poetry
ritual
quotation
narrative architecture

---

# 17. NARRATION REGISTER

Register may be:

devotional
formal
literary
descriptive
reflective
dramatic
ceremonial

It must remain appropriate to narrator identity.

---

# 18. NARRATION ACCESSIBILITY

Narration must be understandable to a modern audience without becoming modern
slang or casual speech.

---

# 19. NARRATION SOURCE PRESERVATION

If source narration is quoted:

preserve exact source text separately.

Production narration may be a translation/adaptation.

Never confuse the two.

---

# 20. NARRATION ADAPTATION

If narration is newly written:

label:

NEW_CONNECTIVE_NARRATION

If derived from source:

label:

SOURCE_DERIVED_NARRATION

If adapted:

label:

ADAPTED_NARRATION

# 22. TULSIDAS → STORY TRANSITION

The transition from Tulsidas narration into the Shiva–Sati–Parvati story must
make the narrative layer clear.

Do not make the audience uncertain about:

who is speaking
when the narrative perspective changes
whether the next speech is narration or character dialogue.

---

# 23. SHIVA–PARVATI TRANSITION

When the story moves into Shiva and Parvati's interaction:

the audience must understand that:

Shiva is now an in-world speaker
Parvati is the listener
their relationship is active
the narrative frame has changed

without requiring unnatural exposition.

---

# 24. SHIVA NARRATION TO RAMA-KATHA

When Shiva begins the Rama-katha:

the narration must remain:

Shiva's narration
+
within the established Shiva–Parvati relationship
+
source-traceable.

Do not convert Shiva into an external omniscient narrator.

---

# 25. NARRATIVE FRAME INTEGRITY

Every narration unit must belong to exactly one active narrative frame.

If multiple frames are intentionally layered:

---

# 26. NARRATION TIME

Track:

story_time:
narration_time:
retrospective_distance:

A narrator may describe past events.

Do not accidentally give a character knowledge from the future.

---

# 27. FORESHADOWING

Foreshadowing must be classified:

source_supported
traditional
adapted
new_production

Do not present production-created foreshadowing as prophecy.

---

# 28. PROPHECY

Prophecy must be source-verified when presented as canonical.

If newly written:

label it as production-created.

---

# 29. NARRATIVE OMNISCIENCE

Omniscience is not a stylistic shortcut.

A narrator may know only what the project establishes.

---

# 30. NARRATION EMOTION

Narration may carry:

devotion
wonder
grief
solemnity
reverence
tension
anticipation

But narrator emotion must remain consistent with narrator identity.

---

# 31. NARRATION CHARACTERIZATION

Tulsidas and Shiva must not sound identical.

Their:

vocabulary
sentence structure
emotional framing
knowledge scope
narrative perspective
relationship to audience

must remain distinguishable.

---

# 32. NARRATION AND CHARACTER VOICE

Character-based narration must pass through the character's dialogue profile.

Narration is not exempt from character identity when the narrator is an
in-world character.

---

# 33. NARRATION AND RELATIONSHIP

In-world narration must respect the relationship with the listener.

For Shiva → Parvati:

consider:

affection
respect
intimacy
teaching
shared spiritual context
listener knowledge
listener questions

---

# 34. LISTENER AWARENESS

If narration is spoken to a specific listener:

do not explain things the listener unquestionably already knows unless there is
a narrative reason.

---

# 35. LISTENER QUESTIONS

When the listener interrupts or asks questions:

the narration becomes interaction.

At that point:

DIALOGUE rules
+
RELATIONSHIP rules

must also apply.

---

# 36. NARRATION AS STORYTELLING

A character narrator may:

pause
respond
clarify
correct
reframe
answer
continue

Do not treat character narration as uninterrupted voice-over.

---

# 37. NARRATION PAUSE

Pauses may indicate:

transition
emotion
reflection
importance
listener reaction

Do not insert pauses merely for dramatic effect.

---

# 38. NARRATION REPETITION

Avoid repeating information already delivered through:

dialogue
visuals
previous narration

unless repetition serves:

emphasis
memory
ritual
source fidelity
narrative structure

---

# 39. NARRATION INFORMATION DENSITY

Balance:

source fidelity
clarity
pace
visual storytelling
dialogue

Do not overload narration.

---

# 40. NARRATION VISUAL BOUNDARY

Do not describe every visual detail.

SCENES and SHOTS control visual construction.

NARRATION supplies only what the narrative layer requires.

---

# 41. NARRATION PERFORMANCE

Performance text may specify:

pause
breath
emphasis
pronunciation
line breaks

without changing the underlying source/adaptation text.

---

# 42. NARRATION PRONUNCIATION

Important names and terms must follow:

PRONUNCIATION.md

Do not create narrator-specific pronunciation without justification.

---

# 43. NARRATION SUBTITLES

Subtitle text must preserve:

meaning
narrator identity
cultural terms
proper names
source meaning

Compression must not change narrative meaning.

---

# 44. NARRATION CONTINUITY

Every scene must inherit:

narrator
narrative frame
knowledge state
language
register
story time

unless an approved transition occurs.

# 46. AUTOMATIC REJECTION

Reject narration if:

narrator is wrong

OR
knowledge is impossible

OR
source attribution is false

OR
adaptation is disguised as canon

OR
narrator perspective changes silently

OR
Shiva stops sounding like Shiva

OR
Tulsidas stops sounding like the approved narrator

OR
production-created prophecy is presented as canonical

OR
narration duplicates obvious visuals without purpose.

---

KNOWLEDGE
+

+
LANGUAGE
+

+

+
