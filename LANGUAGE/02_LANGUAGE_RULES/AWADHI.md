## 1. PURPOSE

This file defines how Awadhi is identified, preserved, researched, transcribed, translated, adapted, and performed in the RAMAYAN project.

Awadhi is treated as a distinct source language from Modern Standard Hindi.

This file is especially concerned with the Awadhi of Goswami Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas, while recognizing that Awadhi has regional, historical, and modern variation.

It does NOT attempt to reconstruct every Awadhi variety or claim that all modern Awadhi speech is identical to sixteenth-century literary Awadhi.

---

## 2. CORE PROJECT RULE

RAMCHARITMANAS = AWADHI SOURCE TEXT.

MODERN HINDI = ADAPTATION / PRODUCTION LANGUAGE.

Therefore:

Awadhi must not be silently rewritten as Modern Standard Hindi.

Modern Hindi must not be retroactively presented as the original language of Ramcharitmanas.

A quotation from Ramcharitmanas must remain identifiable as a source quotation even when its meaning is explained or adapted into Hindi.

---

## 3. HISTORICAL AND LINGUISTIC IDENTITY

Awadhi belongs to the Eastern Hindi group of Indo-Aryan languages.

The Ramcharitmanas is a major literary work in Awadhi.

Its language is related to Hindi but is not identical to Modern Standard Hindi.

Modern Standard Hindi developed around a different linguistic base, principally Khari Boli.

Therefore apparent similarity between Awadhi and Hindi must never be used as evidence that the two are interchangeable.

---

## 4. RAMCHARITMANAS REGISTER

The project distinguishes:

A. GENERAL AWADHI
   Descriptive information about Awadhi as a language.

B. RAMCHARITMANAS AWADHI
   The literary language and usage represented in Tulsidas's text.

C. MODERN AWADHI
   Contemporary regional speech varieties.

These categories must not be merged automatically.

A modern Awadhi form is not automatically a valid replacement for a Ramcharitmanas form.

A Ramcharitmanas form is not automatically a claim about every modern Awadhi speaker.

---

## 5. PRIMARY TEXT AUTHORITY

For Ramcharitmanas quotations, the approved project edition must be recorded.

Every source quotation should preserve:

- original text
- kanda
- stanza / doha reference
- metre where applicable
- edition
- textual variant if relevant
- literal meaning
- contextual meaning
- approved translation/adaptation

Never reconstruct a quotation from memory when the actual source text is available.

---

## 6. SOURCE TEXT PRESERVATION

When a passage is classified as direct_canon:

DO NOT:

- modernize its grammar silently
- replace Awadhi vocabulary with Hindi vocabulary
- change endings for contemporary Hindi
- simplify its syntax without recording the adaptation
- replace poetic forms with prose without marking the change
- invent missing words
- merge different editions silently

The original source must remain recoverable.

---

## 7. DEVANAGARI IS NOT THE LANGUAGE

Devanagari is the script.

Awadhi is the language.

Therefore:

Devanagari text does not automatically mean Hindi.

A Devanagari passage must be identified by its linguistic source.

The same script may represent:

- Awadhi
- Hindi
- Sanskrit
- Braj
- other languages

---

## 8. BASIC WORD ORDER

Awadhi generally follows a head-final / verb-final pattern.

The neutral structure is broadly:

SUBJECT + OBJECT / COMPLEMENTS + VERB

This is shared with Hindi but does NOT make Awadhi grammatically identical to Hindi.

Do not convert Awadhi sentences into Hindi word order merely because the resulting sentence looks familiar.

---

## 9. POSTPOSITIONS / CASE MARKING

Awadhi uses postpositional/case-marking constructions.

Important documented distinctions from Standard Hindi include:

Awadhi accusative/dative forms may use ka/kə-type marking where Modern Standard Hindi commonly uses ko.

Awadhi locative marking may use ma-type forms where Standard Hindi commonly uses mein.

These are linguistic distinctions, not spelling variations.

They must not be normalized automatically into:

को
में

when preserving Awadhi.

Exact form must be verified against the source and edition.

---

## 10. AGENTIVE MARKING

Descriptions of Awadhi distinguish it from Western/Standard Hindi in its treatment of agentive marking.

Do not assume that Hindi ने can simply be inserted into every Awadhi perfective transitive construction.

The actual Awadhi source construction must be checked.

---

## 11. CASE SYSTEM

Awadhi descriptions document case functions including:

- nominative
- accusative
- dative
- instrumental
- genitive
- ablative
- locative
- vocative
- comitative in some descriptions

Different grammatical descriptions may group or label these functions differently.

Therefore the project must preserve the actual form first and assign grammatical labels second.

Do not force Awadhi into the exact case terminology used for Modern Standard Hindi.

---

## 12. GENDER

Awadhi has grammatical gender.

Gender is generally preserved, but descriptions of Awadhi note that gender behavior is not identical to the more rigid patterns of Western/Standard Hindi.

Do not assume every Hindi agreement rule transfers directly.

Source-specific forms must be checked.

---

## 13. NUMBER

Awadhi distinguishes singular and plural.

There is no requirement to create a separate dual category for the project.

Number may be expressed through:

- pronouns
- nouns
- suffixes
- agreement
- context

---

## 14. FIRST-PERSON PRONOUNS

Awadhi has forms that differ from Modern Standard Hindi.

A major documented distinction is the use of ham-type forms for first person, including contexts where Modern Standard Hindi uses main for singular.

Documented Awadhi examples include forms such as:

हम
हमका
हमै
हमार

Exact form varies by grammatical function, source, region, and textual tradition.

Do not mechanically replace Awadhi हम with Hindi मैं.

---

## 15. SECOND-PERSON PRONOUNS

Awadhi has documented second-person forms including:

तू / related forms
तुम / related forms
आप / honorific forms

Other forms occur in Awadhi sources and dialects.

Do not import the complete Modern Hindi तू–तुम–आप system without checking the source.

---

## 16. THIRD-PERSON PRONOUNS

Awadhi uses demonstrative/pronominal systems that differ from Standard Hindi.

Documented descriptions include forms corresponding broadly to:

ऊ / उ

with distinctions that may involve distance or discourse context.

Do not normalize every Awadhi third-person form to:

वह
वे
यह
ये

when preserving source language.

---

## 17. POSSESSIVE FORMS

Awadhi possesses distinct genitive forms.

Documented examples include:

मोर
तोर
हमार
तुमार / तोहार-type forms

These may inflect or vary according to grammatical environment.

Important distinction:

Awadhi मोर is not merely a decorative substitute for Hindi मेरा.

It belongs to the Awadhi grammatical system.

---

## 18. PRONOUN + CASE FORMS

Awadhi pronouns may have forms that differ substantially from Hindi oblique forms.

Examples documented in grammatical descriptions include forms corresponding to:

हमका
तोका / तुहिका-type forms
मोर / मोका-type forms

The exact form must be source-verified.

Do not generate Awadhi pronouns by applying Hindi oblique rules to Hindi pronouns.

---

## 19. VERB SYSTEM

Awadhi verbs differ from Modern Standard Hindi in morphology and endings.

The project must therefore maintain a dedicated Awadhi verb reference rather than treating Awadhi verbs as Hindi verbs with altered spelling.

---

## 20. HABITUAL / IMPERFECTIVE FORMS

A documented Awadhi characteristic is the habitual/imperfective ending associated with -at / -त forms.

Example pattern:

बोलत
रहत

This differs from the Modern Standard Hindi masculine/feminine habitual pattern:

बोलता / बोलती
रहता / रहती

The Awadhi -त pattern must not be modernized automatically.

---

## 21. GENDER AND THE HABITUAL FORM

In documented Awadhi descriptions, the habitual -त form can remain unchanged across masculine and feminine subjects in constructions where Modern Hindi would use -ता / -ती.

Therefore:

Do not assume:

male = -ता
female = -ती

when analyzing an Awadhi source.

Check the Awadhi construction and auxiliary.

---

## 22. PERFECTIVE VERBS

Awadhi has distinctive perfective morphology.

Descriptions document forms involving endings such as:

-इस
-इन

in particular grammatical environments.

Example forms may include:

कहिस
मारिस

These forms must not be treated as typographical corruption of Modern Hindi.

---

## 23. AWADHI -IS FORMS

The -इस type of verbal form is an important Awadhi marker documented in descriptions of the language.

When encountered in an Awadhi source:

- preserve it
- identify its grammatical function
- verify the source reading
- do not silently convert it to Hindi past tense

---

## 24. AUXILIARIES

Awadhi auxiliary forms differ from Standard Hindi.

The project must maintain a verified Awadhi auxiliary table before generating original Awadhi dialogue.

Do not invent auxiliaries by changing Hindi है/हूँ/हैं into visually similar forms.

For Ramcharitmanas quotations, preserve the actual source form.

---

## 25. NEGATION

Awadhi has its own negative constructions and particles.

Do not assume that every Hindi नहीं construction can be copied into Awadhi unchanged.

For source quotations:

preserve original negative construction.

For newly created Awadhi dialogue:

use a verified Awadhi grammatical model, not Hindi with a few Awadhi vocabulary substitutions.

---

## 26. QUESTIONS

Awadhi interrogatives have relationships to Indo-Aryan forms also found in Hindi, but exact morphology and usage may differ.

Common interrogative concepts include:

who
what
where
when
why
how
which
whose

Exact Awadhi forms must be sourced before original dialogue is generated.

---

## 27. NEGATIVE / INTERROGATIVE GENERATION RULE

AI must never create an Awadhi question or negative sentence by:

1. writing Hindi
2. replacing several words with Awadhi vocabulary
3. changing one verb ending
4. labeling the result Awadhi

That process is prohibited.

Awadhi generation must be grammar-based.

---

## 28. VOCABULARY

Awadhi vocabulary may contain:

- inherited Indo-Aryan vocabulary
- Sanskrit-derived vocabulary
- regional vocabulary
- literary vocabulary
- forms shared with neighboring languages

Shared vocabulary does not establish identical grammar.

A word appearing in Hindi and Awadhi must not automatically be classified as Hindi when it occurs in an Awadhi text.

---

## 29. SANSKRITIZATION IN TULSIDAS

Tulsidas's literary Awadhi can contain substantial Sanskritic vocabulary and compounds.

This does NOT mean that Awadhi itself should be represented as Sanskritized Hindi.

The lexical choices belong to Tulsidas's literary style and must be studied in context.

Research on Awadhi literary composition notes a stronger Sanskritizing tendency in Tulsidas than in some earlier Awadhi literary usage.

Therefore:

Tulsidas's Sanskritic vocabulary must be preserved where canonical, but must not become an excuse to fabricate Sanskrit-heavy modern dialogue.

---

## 30. SANSKRIT QUOTATIONS INSIDE AWADHI

Ramcharitmanas contains passages and metrical material with Sanskrit influence, including Sanskrit shlokas.

Such material must be classified separately.

Do not label an entire passage "Sanskrit" merely because it contains Sanskrit vocabulary.

Do not label a Sanskrit shloka as Awadhi because it occurs inside Ramcharitmanas.

Language identification must happen at passage level where necessary.

---

## 31. POETIC LANGUAGE

Ramcharitmanas is poetry.

Poetic grammar and word order may therefore be influenced by:

- metre
- rhyme/sound
- cadence
- poetic compression
- parallelism
- alliteration
- source tradition

A poetic construction must not automatically be treated as an ordinary conversational Awadhi sentence.

---

## 32. CHAUPAI

Chaupai is the principal metre of Ramcharitmanas.

The commonly described structure uses four metrical quarters/lines, with each quarter based on sixteen matras.

In performance and textual analysis, the internal caesura and metrical structure must be respected.

Do not rewrite a chaupai as prose and continue calling it the original chaupai.

If adapted into prose/dialogue, classify the result as an adaptation.

---

## 33. DOHA

Doha is a major interwoven metrical form in Ramcharitmanas.

Its conventional structure is based on 13 and 11 matras across the two halves of each line.

Doha should be treated as poetry, not as ordinary prose dialogue.

When quoted in performance, metrical integrity should be preserved unless a deliberate production adaptation has been approved.

---

## 34. SORATHA

Soratha is related to the doha tradition but reverses the conventional matra pattern associated with the doha.

It occurs within Ramcharitmanas and must be classified separately from doha.

Do not call every two-line metrical passage a doha.

---

## 35. OTHER METRES

Ramcharitmanas contains additional metrical forms, including chhands and occasional Sanskrit metres.

The project should classify the actual metre rather than assuming:

all verse = chaupai

or:

all two-line verse = doha.

---

## 36. MANGALACHARAN / SHLOKA

Ramcharitmanas includes Sanskrit shlokas in appropriate locations, including opening invocations.

These must be maintained as Sanskrit-source material when they are direct Sanskrit verses.

They belong under the Sanskrit and source-text systems as well as the broader Ramcharitmanas architecture.

---

## 37. DIRECT QUOTATION RULE

A direct quotation from Ramcharitmanas must be reproduced from the approved source edition.

The project must not:

- paraphrase and call it a quotation
- combine half-lines from different locations
- modernize spelling without recording it
- change grammar for actor convenience without labeling the adaptation
- invent missing text

---

## 38. TRANSLATION RULE

Every important Awadhi quotation may have multiple representations:

1. ORIGINAL AWADHI
2. LITERAL TRANSLATION
3. CONTEXTUAL MEANING
4. PERFORMANCE TRANSLATION
5. FINAL SCREENPLAY VERSION

These are different layers.

They must not overwrite one another.

---

## 39. AWADHI → HINDI ADAPTATION

When converting Awadhi into production Hindi:

PRESERVE:

- narrative meaning
- theological meaning
- emotional meaning
- relationship meaning
- action meaning
- important cultural terminology

ADAPT:

- grammar when necessary for Hindi
- syntax when necessary for natural Hindi
- archaic forms when they obstruct comprehension
- metre when the passage is being converted into ordinary dialogue

The final result must be labeled as adaptation, not original Awadhi.

---

## 40. WHAT MUST NOT BE MODERNIZED

Do not modernize the following inside direct quotations unless the project is explicitly producing an adapted version:

- pronouns
- verb endings
- case markers
- vocabulary
- metre
- poetic syntax
- source spelling
- repeated poetic structures

---

## 41. WHAT MAY BE MODERNIZED IN ADAPTATION

A performance adaptation may modernize:

- sentence structure
- difficult vocabulary
- archaic grammatical forms
- metrical constraints
- repetition that is not narratively necessary

But the adaptation must retain the source meaning and be marked as an adaptation.

---

## 42. AWADHI DIALOGUE GENERATION

Original Awadhi dialogue is permitted only when:

- the project explicitly requires Awadhi
- the register is justified
- grammatical evidence is available
- vocabulary is sourced
- pronunciation is verified
- the line is reviewed by the language QA process

"Hindi + Awadhi words" is not sufficient.

---

## 43. HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTION LIMIT

The project must not claim that newly generated Awadhi dialogue is the exact speech of a sixteenth-century person unless supported by evidence.

The safest classifications are:

source quotation

derived/adapted Awadhi

linguistically informed production Awadhi

historical reconstruction

The final classification must be explicit.

---

## 44. MODERN AWADHI WARNING

Modern Awadhi sources are valuable for understanding living grammar and pronunciation.

However, modern forms must not automatically replace Ramcharitmanas forms.

Modern regional variation may reflect:

- geographical variation
- language contact
- grammatical change
- Hindi influence
- Bhojpuri influence
- Urdu influence
- other regional developments

These must be documented rather than silently blended.

---

## 45. HINDI INTERFERENCE CONTROL

The following are warning signs that generated text may actually be Hindi rather than Awadhi:

- Hindi मैं used where an Awadhi source requires a different form
- Hindi मेरा/तेरा replacing source Awadhi possessives without reason
- Hindi को replacing Awadhi case marking
- Hindi में replacing Awadhi locative marking
- Hindi -ता/-ती replacing Awadhi -त where inappropriate
- Hindi है/हैं patterns imposed mechanically
- Hindi past forms replacing Awadhi forms
- modern Hindi vocabulary replacing source vocabulary
- modern Hindi syntax presented as original Awadhi

Such text requires review.

---

## 46. DIALECT CONTROL

Do not invent a single universal "Awadhi accent."

Awadhi has regional variation.

For the Ramcharitmanas project, the principal reference should be the linguistic form represented in the approved textual tradition unless a production decision specifies a living speech model.

Regional pronunciation must be separately documented.

---

## 47. PRONUNCIATION BOUNDARY

Detailed pronunciation belongs in:

02_LANGUAGE_RULES/PRONUNCIATION.md

Awadhi entries should therefore flag pronunciation-sensitive items rather than duplicating the entire phonetic system here.

Important areas include:

- vowel length
- nasalization
- retroflex/dental contrasts
- aspirated consonants
- conjuncts
- word-final vowels
- poetic metre
- source-specific pronunciation

---

## 48. TEXTUAL VARIANTS

Ramcharitmanas has an extensive manuscript and printed textual tradition.

Where editions differ:

record the variant.

Do not silently select one reading and represent it as universally uncontested.

Record:

- edition
- reading A
- reading B if relevant
- textual basis
- project-selected reading
- reason for selection
- confidence

---

## 49. ORTHOGRAPHIC VARIANTS

Differences between editions may involve spelling, vowel representation, nasalization, endings, punctuation, or editorial normalization.

A spelling difference is not automatically a different word.

Conversely, a grammatical difference must not be dismissed as mere spelling.

Textual review is required before normalization.

---

## 50. EDITION CONTROL

The project must maintain one approved primary Ramcharitmanas edition for production.

Secondary editions may be consulted for:

- variants
- commentary
- difficult readings
- metrical verification
- cross-checking

No secondary edition silently replaces the approved primary edition.

---

## 51. SOURCE CLASSIFICATION

Every Awadhi passage used in the project should be classified as one of:

DIRECT_CANON
DERIVED_CANON
TRADITIONAL_INTERPRETATION
TRANSLATION
ADAPTATION
LINGUISTIC_RECONSTRUCTION
PRODUCTION_AWADHI
DIRECTORIAL_CHOICE
UNKNOWN
UNCERTAIN

---

## 52. AI SAFETY RULE

AI must never be treated as evidence for Awadhi.

An AI model may:

- propose a translation
- identify possible grammar
- suggest alternatives
- assist comparison

But its output must be independently verified against:

- the source text
- a grammar
- a dictionary
- linguistic research
- qualified scholarly material

---

## 53. AWADHI TERMINOLOGY REGISTRY

Important Awadhi words and forms should be registered with:

- word
- grammatical category
- meaning
- source
- source passage
- modern Hindi equivalent
- Sanskrit equivalent where relevant
- pronunciation reference
- register
- confidence
- notes

Do not build the project vocabulary by translating a Hindi dictionary backward into Awadhi.

---

## 54. AWADHI → HINDI WORD MAPPING

A mapping such as:

मोर → मेरा

may be useful for translation.

It is NOT permission to replace मोर with मेरा inside a direct quotation.

Mappings are translation tools, not source normalization rules.

---

## 55. PERFORMANCE RULE

When an Awadhi verse is performed:

- preserve the source text
- preserve metre where applicable
- preserve meaningful repetition
- preserve pronunciation
- preserve emotional intent
- avoid artificially modern pronunciation
- avoid exaggerated "village" accent
- avoid caricatured rusticity

Awadhi should sound like a real language, not a costume.

---

## 56. ACTING LANGUAGE RULE

Actors should not be instructed to pronounce Awadhi by simply reading Modern Hindi spellings with a regional accent.

Performance preparation should use:

- verified pronunciation
- syllable segmentation where needed
- meaning
- grammatical structure
- emotional context
- metre for verse

---

## 57. SUBTITLE RULE

If original Awadhi is performed:

Hindi subtitles may translate the meaning into accessible Hindi.

The subtitle should not falsely imply that the spoken line itself was Modern Standard Hindi.

For important canonical verses, the project may retain the original Awadhi in one subtitle layer and provide an accessible Hindi meaning in another layer when technically feasible.

---

## 58. NARRATION RULE

If narration quotes Ramcharitmanas directly, the original Awadhi should be preserved where canonical quotation is intended.

If narration explains the passage in Hindi, classify it as Hindi narration/adaptation.

Do not blend the two layers without labeling them.

---

## 59. CHARACTER LANGUAGE RULE

Awadhi usage must be assigned according to:

- source requirement
- scene requirement
- narrative mode
- character identity
- relationship
- social setting
- poetic/ritual context

Do not give every character identical Awadhi speech.

---

## 60. AWADHI AND SACRED SPEECH

Sacred content does not automatically require Sanskrit.

A canonical Ramcharitmanas passage may remain Awadhi while expressing highly sacred content.

Conversely, a Sanskrit mantra or shloka must remain Sanskrit where the source requires it.

Language choice follows source and context, not a simplistic sacred = Sanskrit rule.

---

## 61. AWADHI AND HUMOR

Do not create artificial comic Awadhi by exaggerating dialect markers.

Humor, sarcasm, wit, or irony must arise from the character and source/context.

---

## 62. AWADHI AND EMOTION

Emotional intensity may affect:

- word order
- repetition
- address
- sentence length
- omission
- interjection
- poetic compression

But emotional intensity must not be used as an excuse to replace Awadhi grammar with modern Hindi.

---

## 63. PROHIBITED PRACTICES

DO NOT:

- call Ramcharitmanas Modern Hindi
- generate Awadhi by adding -त to Hindi randomly
- replace Hindi words with a few Awadhi synonyms
- invent historical Awadhi
- invent dialect pronunciation
- silently modernize canonical verses
- fabricate quotations
- merge Awadhi and Bhojpuri
- merge Awadhi and Braj
- treat every eastern Hindi form as Awadhi
- treat every old-looking Hindi word as Awadhi
- use modern Hindi grammar while labeling the output Awadhi

---

## 64. REQUIRED RESEARCH FOR UNCERTAIN FORMS

If an Awadhi form is uncertain, research should prioritize:

1. approved Ramcharitmanas edition
2. established Ramcharitmanas scholarship
3. descriptive Awadhi grammar
4. Central Institute / Government linguistic resources
5. historical linguistic research
6. specialist dictionaries
7. reputable secondary scholarship

AI output is never the final authority.

---

## 65. SOURCE CONFLICT RULE

If sources disagree:

record the disagreement.

Do not silently average the forms.

Record:

- source A
- source B
- exact disagreement
- source type
- authority
- project decision
- uncertainty

Ramcharitmanas-first remains the project hierarchy for Ramcharitmanas production decisions.

## 67. FINAL AWADHI STANDARD

For this project:

AWADHI IS NOT DECORATIVE OLD HINDI.

AWADHI IS NOT HINDI WITH A VILLAGE ACCENT.

AWADHI IS NOT HINDI WITH A FEW REGIONAL WORDS.

AWADHI IS A DISTINCT LANGUAGE SYSTEM WITH ITS OWN:

- grammar
- pronouns
- morphology
- case marking
- vocabulary
- phonology
- literary tradition
- poetic conventions

Ramcharitmanas material must be treated as source-language material first and adapted only through a documented process.

FINAL STANDARD:

SOURCE-FAITHFUL
+
LINGUISTICALLY ACCURATE
+
TEXTUALLY TRACEABLE
+
PERFORMABLE
+
NON-CARICATURED
+
CLEARLY DISTINGUISHED FROM MODERN HINDI

END OF AWADHI RULES
