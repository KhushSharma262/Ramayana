# ANIMAL VARIATION RULES

## Purpose

Defines the boundary between natural visual variation and unauthorized
animal continuity change.

---

# 1. CORE RULE

Continuity does not mean pixel identity.

The same animal may naturally vary between frames and shots.

Variation must remain:

- biologically plausible
- species-appropriate
- identity-preserving
- state-consistent
- causally explainable

---

# 2. IDENTITY

Identity variation tolerance:

VERY LOW

The animal must remain recognizable as the same individual.

---

# 3. ANATOMY

Anatomical variation tolerance:

VERY LOW

No unexplained:

- limb changes
- body-part changes
- skull changes
- body-proportion changes
- joint changes
- tail changes
- horn/antler changes

---

# 4. POSTURE

Posture variation:

HIGHER

Allowed when consistent with:

- movement
- balance
- activity
- emotion
- fatigue
- terrain
- interaction

---

# 5. BREATHING

Breathing may vary naturally according to:

- rest
- activity
- fear
- heat
- illness
- fatigue
- exertion

---

# 6. EYE MOVEMENT

Eye direction may vary naturally.

It must remain consistent with:

- perception
- attention
- stimulus
- anatomy
- camera visibility

---

# 7. EAR / TAIL / WING MOVEMENT

Species-specific appendage movement may vary naturally.

Variation must correspond to:

- attention
- emotion
- communication
- balance
- movement
- environment

---

# 8. FUR / FEATHER / SCALE VARIATION

Small physical displacement is allowed.

Examples:

- wind
- movement
- contact
- grooming
- water
- dirt

Persistent markings must not change.

---

# 9. MOVEMENT VARIATION

Movement may vary slightly in:

- stride
- timing
- head motion
- foot placement
- balance correction
- acceleration
- deceleration

The underlying species biomechanics must remain stable.

---

# 10. ENVIRONMENTAL VARIATION

Environmental variation may occur due to:

- wind
- rain
- dust
- mud
- sunlight
- shade
- temperature
- terrain

It must not silently alter persistent animal state.

---

# 11. EMOTIONAL VARIATION

Emotional/performance variation is permitted within the established state.

It must not contradict:

- established fear
- pain
- fatigue
- relationship
- stimulus
- previous event
- current objective

---

# 12. PERSISTENT STATE

The following must not vary randomly:

- injury
- scar
- blood
- missing body part
- established marking
- equipment
- ownership
- species
- age
- sex
- transformation state

unless an actual continuity event occurs.

---

# 13. CORRELATED VARIATION

Variations should be causally correlated.

Example:

running
? increased breathing
? increased muscle movement
? weight transfer
? environmental disturbance
? fatigue

Independent random variation should not create contradictory states.

---

# 14. VARIATION ACCUMULATION

Small changes must not accumulate into identity drift.

Variation must periodically be checked against the authoritative identity state.

---

# 15. REGENERATION

A new seed or regeneration attempt does not authorize new animal state.

Only authorized changes may alter continuity.
