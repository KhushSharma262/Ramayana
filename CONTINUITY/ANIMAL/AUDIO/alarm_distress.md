# ALARM AND DISTRESS

## Purpose

Defines animal alarm and distress audio.

---

# 1. ALARM

Alarm may be triggered by:

- predator
- unfamiliar human
- fire
- sudden movement
- environmental threat
- injury
- territorial intrusion
- species-specific threat

---

# 2. DISTRESS

Distress may result from:

- injury
- capture
- separation
- extreme fear
- physical danger
- maternal separation

only where biologically/canonically appropriate.

---

# 3. REACTION LATENCY

Sound must follow plausible detection and reaction timing.

---

# 4. PROPAGATION

Alarm may propagate:

Animal A detects threat
? Animal A signals
? nearby animals detect signal
? group state changes
? collective response

---

# 5. NO AUTOMATIC SCREAMING

Animals must not continuously vocalize during danger.

Species-specific behavior determines whether and how they vocalize.
