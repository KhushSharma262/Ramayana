# RAMAYANA CAMERA — HARDENED CONTINUITY CONTRACT

## MASTER PRINCIPLE

CAMERA is the controlled observer of the established world.

CAMERA may determine how the world is observed.

CAMERA may NOT silently redefine the world.

## AUTHORITY

GLOBAL STATE
→ GLOBAL EVENT
→ SPATIAL
→ PHYSICS
→ CHARACTER / ANIMAL / PROP / ENVIRONMENT
→ PERFORMANCE
→ LIGHTING
→ TIMELINE

provide authoritative world truth.

CAMERA owns:

- camera identity
- camera configuration
- camera optics
- camera position
- camera orientation
- framing
- composition
- focus
- exposure
- movement
- camera physics
- visual camera language
- camera-specific state

## SINGLE SOURCE OF TRUTH

Every camera attribute must have exactly one authoritative owner.

Camera-specific attributes must be owned by CAMERA.

Cross-domain attributes remain owned by their respective domains.

CAMERA may reference them but cannot silently overwrite them.

## CAMERA STATE

The authoritative camera state is:

IDENTITY
→ BASELINE
→ PREVIOUS STATE
→ CAMERA EVENT
→ STATE DELTA
→ CURRENT STATE
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ PERSISTENCE
→ NEXT STATE

The Camera State Ledger must connect to the GLOBAL state ledger.

The Camera ledger is an interface, not a competing global truth.

## PHYSICAL FEASIBILITY

Every camera movement must pass:

GEOMETRY
→ CLEARANCE
→ RIG LIMIT
→ OPERATOR LIMIT
→ ACCELERATION
→ VELOCITY
→ TRAJECTORY
→ ENVIRONMENT
→ PHYSICS

unless explicitly classified as supernatural, divine, dream, memory, subjective or otherwise intentionally impossible.

## OPTICAL FEASIBILITY

Lens state must remain compatible with:

- sensor
- focal length
- aperture
- focus distance
- depth of field
- field of view
- distortion
- filter stack
- calibration

## SPATIAL FEASIBILITY

Camera must remain compatible with:

- world coordinates
- scene coordinates
- camera coordinates
- subject coordinates
- spatial geometry
- occlusion
- parallax
- perspective
- frustum

## CHARACTER FEASIBILITY

Camera must preserve:

- character position
- eye position
- eyeline
- gaze
- screen position
- relative distance
- relative orientation
- visibility
- occlusion
- movement phase

unless an intentional transition changes them.

## AXIS

The active axis must be explicitly constructed.

Dynamic axes must update when subjects move.

Axis crossing must be explicit and validated.

## CUTS

Every cut must compare:

PRE-CUT CAMERA STATE
against
POST-CUT CAMERA STATE

including:

- spatial relationship
- axis
- eyeline
- screen direction
- action phase
- movement phase
- visibility
- framing
- lens
- focus
- exposure

## NATURAL VARIATION

Natural camera variation is allowed only inside defined tolerances.

Natural variation must never become identity drift.

## AI

AI_GENERATION is implementation.

AI must receive established camera state.

AI output must be analyzed to recover/estimate:

- camera pose
- lens
- FOV
- focus
- depth of field
- camera height
- distance
- orientation
- trajectory
- perspective
- distortion
- framing
- axis
- movement

Generated output must be validated against required state.

## CROSS-MODEL

Different AI models may interpret camera parameters differently.

Every model must have a compatibility mapping.

Equivalent camera intent must be validated rather than assumed.

## UNKNOWN

Unknown ≠ unchanged.

Unknown ≠ default.

Unknown must remain explicitly marked until resolved.

## CHANGE PROPAGATION

CAMERA CHANGE
→ ATTRIBUTE CHANGE
→ DEPENDENCY GRAPH
→ IMPACT ANALYSIS
→ EARLIEST INVALID OUTPUT
→ REPAIR
→ REVALIDATION
→ APPROVAL
→ LOCK
→ DOWNSTREAM PROPAGATION

## RETROACTIVE CHANGE

A later-discovered camera error must not be patched only at the final frame.

The earliest incorrect camera state must be identified and repaired.

All dependent outputs must then be revalidated.

## REALISM

Camera realism includes:

- optical imperfections
- sensor behavior
- operator movement
- rig inertia
- focus behavior
- exposure latency
- motion blur
- rolling shutter
- environmental contamination
- physical camera limitations

Artificial imperfections must not be confused with authentic camera behavior.

## LOCK

An approved camera state becomes locked.

Changing a locked state requires:

- reason
- authority
- impact analysis
- repair
- revalidation
- approval
- new version
